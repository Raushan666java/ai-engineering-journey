# 05 - The SC Category Playbook

## Learning Objectives

After this chapter you will be able to state precisely which recruitment rules your SC status changes and which it does not, compute the real number of seats you are competing for, explain the own-merit adjustment rule and why it should raise your target rather than lower it, build a certificate file that survives document verification, and run the recruitment cycle from notification day to document verification without a procedural loss.

## 1. What SC status changes, and what it does not

| Changes | Does not change |
|---|---|
| **Age relaxation:** +5 years (cap 35 on both JTO and SET, instead of 30) | The paper: same 200 questions, same syllabus, same difficulty |
| **Relaxed qualifying marks:** 35% Part I, 35% Part II, 45% overall (vs 40/40/50) | The **selection** cutoff: that is still category merit |
| **Reserved seats:** 15% of vacancies, subject to the roster | Document verification standards |
| **Fee:** SC/ST/PwBD are commonly exempt from the application fee (**verify in the 2027 notification**) | Medical standards |
| **Own-merit adjustment:** you can occupy an unreserved seat on merit | The need to clear every sectional floor |
| **Backlog carry-forward:** unfilled reserved vacancies are generally carried forward, not de-reserved | Competition within your own category |

**The mental model:** SC status changes the **entry rules and the seat allocation**. It does not change the **paper, the difficulty, or the fact that selection is decided by rank inside a small, motivated pool.** Candidates who confuse a relaxed floor with a relaxed contest are the ones who score 88 and wonder what happened.

## 2. The vacancy arithmetic

### 2.1 The nominal rule and the real number

Reservation is 15% of vacancies **as per the roster**, not 15% of a round number. The 2026 cycle shows why you should never compute your own expectation:

| Cycle | Total | SC seats | SC share | Observation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **SET 2026** | 120 | **18** (14 Telecom + 4 Finance) | 15.0% | Matches the nominal rule |
| **JTO 2026** | 100 | **12** | 12.0% | Below nominal; OBC-NCL was only 10, so this split is a roster artefact, not a percentage |

**Practical consequence:** for 2027, assume **12-18 SC seats across both streams**, and for the Telecom stream specifically assume **roughly 10-15**. Plan for the lower end.

### 2.2 Backlog

Unfilled reserved vacancies in central government and central PSU recruitment are generally **carried forward as backlog** rather than converted to unreserved. Two implications:

1. A year with poor SC representation can produce a *larger* SC seat count the following year.
2. Backlog seats are usually advertised separately, with their own eligibility window. Read the notification's vacancy table, not the headline total.

### 2.3 The ratio that actually matters

| Layer | Number | Ratio |
|---|---|---|
| Total candidates who sat SET 2026 | ~14,149 | 118 : 1 overall |
| SC share of a technical applicant pool (estimate) | ~12-14% | ~1,700-2,000 candidates |
| **SC seats (Telecom)** | **14** | **~120-140 : 1** |
| Candidates who actually prepared seriously | maybe 25-30% of the above | **~35-45 : 1** |

That last line is the honest one. In a 60%-and-named-branch-filtered exam, the field is thin. **You are not competing with 2,000 people; you are competing with the few hundred who studied.**

## 3. Qualifying marks vs selection: the trap of the relaxed floor

Your floors in absolute marks:

| Floor | Requirement | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| Part I (aptitude) | 35% of 40 | **14** |
| Part II (technical) | 35% of 160 | **56** |
| Overall | 45% of 200 | **90** |

Failing **any one** of the three disqualifies you, regardless of the other two. A candidate with 145/160 technical and 12/40 aptitude is rejected.

But look at what each score actually buys:

| Score /200 | What it means |
|---|---|
| 90 | Qualified. Almost certainly not selected. |
| 100 | Qualified. Bottom of the realistic SC band (2026 estimate was 85-100 for *qualification-adjacent* estimates; selection sits higher). |
| **110** | Your **floor**. Competitive in a weak year or a favourable vacancy count. |
| **120** | Your **target**. Competitive in the realistic scenario. |
| **135** | Stretch. Safe in a difficult scenario (easy paper, fewer seats, strong field). |

**Rule: never let the relaxed floor become your planning number.** The floor exists so that a bad aptitude day does not erase a good technical performance. It is not a target, and treating it as one is the most common SC-specific strategic error.

## 4. The own-merit rule (the advantage almost nobody uses)

Central government reservation policy includes a principle that most candidates have never read:

> An SC/ST candidate who is selected **on the basis of his or her own merit** in an open competition is **adjusted against an unreserved vacancy**, and is **not counted against the reserved quota**.

In practice:

1. If your score clears the **unreserved** cutoff, you are placed against a UR seat - and the SC seat you would have occupied stays available for another SC candidate.
2. Your selection is therefore **more secure**, because you are no longer dependent on where you rank inside a small SC band.
3. It also means a strong SC candidate can be selected even in a year where the SC band is unusually crowded.

**Strategic implication: your real target is the UR band (105-120 on 2026-grade difficulty), not the SC band.** Chasing the UR number does two things at once: it makes you selectable under either head, and it removes the risk that a single strong competitor pushes you out of a narrow SC list.

(Authority: DoPT OM No. 36011/1/98-Estt.(Res), 1 July 1998, and subsequent consolidation. Verify the current version applies to BSNL's 2027 notification.)

## 5. The certificate - the biggest disqualification risk in the whole process

More eligible SC candidates lose BSNL selections to paperwork than to the exam. There is no appeal that fixes a certificate after the verification date.

### 5.1 The Central List trap

For **central government and central PSU** recruitment, your caste must appear in the **Central List of Scheduled Castes for the State/UT of your origin** - not merely in a State List.

There are castes that are SC in a state list but absent from the central list for that state, and vice versa. If yours is one, your certificate is worthless for BSNL no matter how valid it is for state jobs. **Check the central list before you apply, not after you are selected.**

### 5.2 What the certificate must contain

| Field | Requirement | Common failure |
|---|---|---|
| Candidate name | Must match matriculation certificate **exactly** | Initials expanded or contracted |
| Father's / mother's name | Match | Spelling variant |
| Date of birth | Match | Typo |
| Caste | As per central list, exact spelling | "Chamar" vs "Chamara" style variants |
| Category | Explicitly "Scheduled Caste" | Generic "reserved category" wording |
| Issuing authority | Competent authority per state rules | Tehsildar signature without seal, or an unauthorised office |
| Seal, signature, date | Present | Photocopy of a photocopy |

### 5.3 Validity

| Certificate | Validity |
|---|---|
| **SC** | No expiry (it is a status, not an income test) |
| OBC-NCL | Typically annual (not applicable to you) |
| EWS | Annual (not applicable to you) |

Because SC certificates do not expire, an old one is *usually* acceptable - but an old one issued **before your name was spelled the way it appears on your degree** is a problem. If in doubt, get a fresh one.

### 5.4 Your document file (build it in week 1, not week 40)

| Document | Copies | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| SC certificate, central format | 3 attested + original | Fresh if any field is doubtful |
| Class 10 certificate (name, DOB) | 3 + original | The reference document for all name matching |
| B.Tech degree / provisional | 3 + original | |
| All semester mark sheets | 3 + original | Percentage computation evidence |
| ID proof (Aadhaar/PAN/passport) | 3 + original | Name must match |
| Passport photos | 10 | Same specification as the application photo |
| Application form printout | 2 | Signed |
| Admit card | 2 | |

**Name-consistency rule:** every document in that file should agree on the exact spelling of your name, your father's name and your date of birth. If they do not, fix the outliers now, while you have months. After the DV date, you have nothing.

## 6. Age and runway

| | General cap | SC cap | Your age |
|---|---|---|---|
| SET | 30 | **35** | 25 now, 26 in 2027 |
| JTO | 30 | **35** | same |

Neither exam limits the number of attempts. You are age-bound, not attempt-bound, which means:

- **2027 is not your only shot.** You have roughly nine years of eligibility.
- That is a reason to build a **durable** preparation (concepts, not memorised papers), not a reason to relax. The 2028 and 2029 cycles will reward the same syllabus.
- It is also a reason to run parallel tracks (section 11) instead of staking everything on 12-18 seats in a single year.

## 7. Document verification: the stage-by-stage failure map

| Stage | What they check | How people fail |
|---|---|---|
| Application scrutiny | Category, branch, percentage, age | Wrong branch code; percentage below 60 (SET); category left blank |
| CBT | Sectional floors | Part I below 14 (SC) |
| Merit list | Category-wise rank | - |
| Document verification | Originals vs application data | Certificate not central format; name mismatch; degree not full-time regular |
| Medical | Fitness standards | Undisclosed condition that fails the standard |
| Appointment | Police verification, character | Prior record |

## 8. SC-specific risk register

| Risk | Likelihood | Impact | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Certificate in state format / caste not in central list | Medium | **Fatal** | Verify against the central list this week |
| Name spelling mismatch across documents | Medium | **Fatal** | Align all documents now |
| Treating 90 as the target | **High** | Severe (missed year) | Target 120, floor 110 |
| Not knowing the own-merit rule | High | Moderate (unnecessary risk) | Aim at the UR band |
| Assuming SC seats scale with 15% | High | Moderate (bad planning) | Read the notification table; plan for 12-18 |
| Missing the fee-exemption step | Low | Low (money) | Check the notification; keep the receipt either way |
| Overlapping PwBD claim without certificate | Low | Severe | Only claim what you can document |
| Single-track preparation | Medium | Severe | Run the parallel portfolio in section 11 |

## 9. Score targets, block by block, for an SC candidate

Target 120. This is the block-level budget that produces it.

| Block | Marks | Minimum | Target | Stretch |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Part I - Aptitude | 40 | 24 | **32** | 36 |
| IP / Data Networks | 24 | 16 | **22** | 24 |
| Emerging Technologies | 24 | 14 | **20** | 23 |
| Wireline | 24 | 12 | **17** | 20 |
| Transmission | 24 | 12 | **16** | 20 |
| Basic Telecom | 24 | 11 | **15** | 19 |
| Wireless | 24 | 10 | **14** | 18 |
| Project Management | 16 | 9 | **13** | 15 |
| **Total** | **200** | **108** | **149 attempted-value** | - |

Note the last row: the targets sum above 120 deliberately. You will not hit every block target on exam day, and the buffer is what protects a 120. Plan for 149, score 120, sleep well.

## 10. Cycle-day playbooks

### 10.1 Notification day (expect late Jan / early Feb 2027)

- [ ] Download the PDF from `externalexam.bsnl.co.in`, not from an aggregator
- [ ] Extract: vacancy table (stream-wise, category-wise), age as-on date, percentage rule, fee, closing date
- [ ] Re-check the syllabus weights against `01-master-syllabus.md`; reorder priorities if they moved
- [ ] Confirm your branch appears in the eligible list **verbatim**
- [ ] Note the closing date in three places; BSNL has extended deadlines before, but never plan for an extension

### 10.2 Application day

| Field | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Category | Wrong category = wrong merit list = no remedy |
| Branch / discipline | Must match the eligible list exactly |
| Percentage | Compute per the notification's stated method, not your own |
| Age as-on date | Compute against the stated date, not today |
| Photo and signature | Specification failure is the most common technical rejection |
| Fee | SC exemption if offered; keep proof either way |
| Final submit | Payment without final submission is a non-application |

### 10.3 Exam morning (minute by minute)

| Time | Action |
|---|---|
| T-150 min | Wake, light breakfast, no new material |
| T-90 | Travel buffer; read the formula sheet once |
| T-45 | At centre; documents in hand |
| T-15 | Seated; system check; write nothing yet |
| T+0 | Read instructions; note the question count and marking |
| T+2 | Start Part I (aptitude), 36 s per question |
| T+26 | Start Part II pass 1, harvest the sure shots |
| T+95 | Pass 2, convert the flagged questions |
| T+162 | Pass 3, no blanks left |
| T+180 | Submit; do not review solved questions |

### 10.4 Result day

- Verify your score against your own marking, not against rumours.
- If shortlisted: freeze all documents in one folder the same day; start the medical checklist.
- If not: the error log and the mock tracker already tell you which block cost you the year. Start the next cycle from that block, not from chapter one.

## 11. The parallel portfolio (because 12-18 seats is a small number)

BSNL should be the **primary** track, not the only one. All of the following recruit engineers and follow central reservation policy - verify each notification's branch list and dates:

| Track | Fit for CSE | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| BSNL SET / JTO 2027 | Strong | Primary |
| TCIL (telecom PSU) | Strong | Smaller, irregular cycles |
| BEL / BHEL / NHPC / NTPC / IOCL / GAIL / EIL | Medium | Engineer-trainee style exams, GATE-linked for some |
| DRDO / ISRO / NPCIL | Medium | Own exams, technical depth |
| IBPS / SBI / RBI IT officer | Strong | Different syllabus, but your CSE base transfers |
| GATE CSE 2028 | Strong | Keeps MTech/IIT and GATE-based PSU doors open |
| State PSU / state govt IT posts | Medium | State SC lists apply here, not the central list |

**Portfolio rule:** one primary track gets 80% of your hours. The rest are opportunistic - apply, don't study separately.

## 12. Anti-patterns

1. **"I'm SC, so 95 is enough."** It is not. 95 qualifies and loses.
2. **Applying late.** BSNL portals have crashed near deadlines. Apply in the first week.
3. **Certificate on the last day.** Issuing authorities take weeks.
4. **Studying only the technical part.** Part I disqualifies more SC candidates than Part II does, because the floor is low and the neglect is total.
5. **Single-track obsession.** Twelve seats do not deserve 100% of your risk.
6. **Skipping the diagnostic** because it feels bad. The baseline is data.
7. **Reading the syllabus instead of drilling L4.** See `01-master-syllabus.md`: 23 numerical families decide the merit list.

## Summary

SC status buys you a five-year age runway, a lower qualifying floor, 15%-ish of the seats and the own-merit adjustment. It does not buy a lower selection cutoff, an easier paper, or leniency at verification. The three decisions that matter most are: target the unreserved band (120), keep the certificate file flawless from week one, and treat the relaxed floor as a safety net rather than a destination.

## Practical Takeaways

- Target **120/200**, floor 110, stretch 135 - never plan against 90.
- Verify your caste against the **Central List** for your state this week.
- Align name, father's name and DOB across every document now.
- Assume **12-18 SC seats**; read the 2027 vacancy table when it appears.
- Aim at the UR band to benefit from the own-merit adjustment.
- Run a portfolio: BSNL primary, everything else opportunistic.
- Part I is a disqualification risk, not a formality.

## Chapter Quiz

**Q1.** An SC candidate clears the unreserved cutoff in an open competition. Under the own-merit principle, the candidate is:
(a) counted against the SC quota (b) adjusted against an unreserved vacancy (c) disqualified (d) placed on a waiting list
**Answer: (b).**

**Q2.** The SC qualifying requirement for BSNL SET is:
(a) 40/40/50 (b) 35/35/45 (c) 30/30/40 (d) no sectional floor
**Answer: (b).**

**Q3.** A state-issued SC certificate for a caste absent from the Central List is, for BSNL recruitment:
(a) fully valid (b) valid with an affidavit (c) not acceptable (d) valid only for JTO
**Answer: (c).**

**Q4.** With 120 vacancies and a nominal 15% SC reservation, the realistic SC seat count for planning is:
(a) exactly 18 every year (b) 12-18, read from the notification (c) 30 (d) 6
**Answer: (b).** The 2026 JTO cycle gave 12/100.

**Q5.** The largest procedural risk to an otherwise eligible SC candidate is:
(a) the CBT difficulty (b) certificate format and name matching (c) the medical test (d) the application fee
**Answer: (b).**
