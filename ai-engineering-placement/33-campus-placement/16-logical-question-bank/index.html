Logical Reasoning — 100 Question Drill Bank (Any Company)

Rule: 45-75 seconds per question. Answers with explanations at the bottom. Covers all 12 types from logical-all-types.md. Difficulty tags: (E) Easy, (M) Medium, (H) Hard.

Section 1 — Number & Letter Series (9 questions)​

  1. (E) Find the next term: 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, ?
  2. (E) Find the next term: 3, 6, 12, 24, 48, ?
  3. (M) Find the next term: 2, 7, 17, 37, 77, ?
  4. (M) Find the next term: 1, 3, 7, 15, 31, ?
  5. (M) Find the missing term: 5, 11, ?, 47, 95
  6. (M) Find the next letter: A, D, I, P, ?
  7. (H) Find the next term: 1, 2, 6, 24, 120, ?
  8. (H) Find the next letter: Z, W, T, Q, ?
  9. (H) Find the next term: 8, 24, 12, 36, 18, ?

Section 2 — Analogies (8 questions)​

  1. (E) CUP : SAUCER :: BOTTLE : ? (A) cork (B) glass (C) stand (D) liquid
  2. (E) TEACHER : SCHOOL :: DOCTOR : ? (A) patient (B) hospital (C) medicine (D) nurse
  3. (M) BOOK : CHAPTER :: TREE : ? (A) leaf (B) branch (C) root (D) wood
  4. (M) WOOL : SHEEP :: SILK : ? (A) cotton (B) silkworm (C) nylon (D) worm
  5. (M) OXYGEN : BREATHING :: FOOD : ? (A) eating (B) nutrition (C) hunger (D) digestion
  6. (M) HAPPY : SAD :: VICTORY : ? (A) win (B) defeat (C) trophy (D) game
  7. (H) DIRECTION : DISPATCHED :: ? (A) opinion (B) order (C) thought (D) letter
  8. (H) MONTH : YEAR :: HOUR : ? (A) minute (B) day (C) clock (D) second

Section 3 — Odd One Out (8 questions)​

  1. (E) 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 — which is the odd one out?
  2. (E) Dog, Cat, Cow, Tiger, Wolf — which is odd?
  3. (M) 121, 169, 196, 225, 289 — which is odd?
  4. (M) Apple, Banana, Orange, Potato, Mango — which is odd?
  5. (M) 13, 17, 23, 27, 29 — which is odd?
  6. (M) Cube, Sphere, Cylinder, Triangle, Cone — which is odd?
  7. (H) 41, 43, 47, 53, 57 — which is odd?
  8. (H) Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai, Chandigarh — which is odd?

Section 4 — Syllogisms (9 questions)​

  1. (E) All dogs are animals. Some animals are cats. I. Some dogs are cats. II. All dogs are animals. Which conclusion(s) follow?
  2. (E) No pen is a pencil. Some pencils are erasers. I. Some pens are erasers. II. Some erasers are pencils.
  3. (M) All roses are flowers. Some flowers are white. I. Some roses are white. II. Some flowers are roses.
  4. (M) All apples are fruits. No fruit is a vegetable. I. No apple is a vegetable. II. All fruits are apples.
  5. (M) Some teachers are professors. All professors are educated. I. Some teachers are educated. II. All teachers are educated.
  6. (M) All laptops are computers. All computers are machines. I. All laptops are machines. II. Some machines are laptops.
  7. (H) Some A are B. No B is C. I. No A is C. II. Some A are not C.
  8. (H) All books are paper. Some paper is plastic. I. Some books are plastic. II. Some paper is book.
  9. (H) Some doctors are rich. All rich people are educated. I. Some doctors are educated. II. All educated people are doctors.

Section 5 — Coding-Decoding (8 questions)​

  1. (E) If CAT is written as DBU, how is DOG written?
  2. (E) If PLAN is written as QMBO, how is TRAIN written?
  3. (M) If CODE is written as DPEB, how is LOVE written?
  4. (M) In a code, A = 1, B = 2 ... Z = 26. What is the code for DOG?
  5. (M) If SUCCESS is coded as SUCCESX (last letter + 1), how is FAILURE coded?
  6. (M) If 1234 means "you are very good", 5678 means "good boys play well", what does 4 mean?
  7. (H) If MOBILE is coded as NP CJ MF, how is PHONE coded?
  8. (H) If "book the ticket" is coded as "kik rik tiz" and "the ticket money" is coded as "rik tiz bip", how is "book" coded?

Section 6 — Blood Relations (8 questions)​

  1. (E) A is the brother of B. B is the daughter of C. How is A related to C?
  2. (E) Pointing to a photograph, a man says, "She is the daughter of my mother's only son." Who is she?
  3. (M) A is B's father. C is B's sister. D is C's mother. How is D related to B?
  4. (M) R is the brother of S. S is the father of T. How is R related to T?
  5. (M) P is Q's sister. Q is R's mother. R is S's son. How is S related to P?
  6. (M) A is the son of B. B is the wife of C. D is the daughter of C. How is A related to D?
  7. (H) X is the father of Y. Y is the daughter of Z. Z is the wife of X. How many children do X and Z have?
  8. (H) A man says to a woman, "Your mother's husband's sister is my aunt." How is the woman related to the man?

Section 7 — Direction Sense (8 questions)​

  1. (E) A man walks 3 km North, then 4 km East. How far and in what direction is he from the start?
  2. (E) A man walks 5 km South, then 5 km North. Where is he?
  3. (M) A man walks 3 km North, then 4 km East, then 3 km South. What is his net position?
  4. (M) A boy walks 4 km North, 3 km West, 4 km South. What is his net position?
  5. (M) A person walks 6 km East, then 8 km South. How far is he from the start?
  6. (M) Facing North, a man turns 90° clockwise, walks 5 km, then turns 90° anticlockwise, walks 5 km. Where is he relative to the start?
  7. (H) A man walks 10 km North, turns right, walks 6 km, turns right, walks 10 km. Where is he relative to the start?
  8. (H) A starts walking 5 km East, then turns left, walks 5 km, turns left, walks 5 km, turns left, walks 8 km. What is his net position?

Section 8 — Seating Arrangement (8 questions)​

  1. (E) Five people A, B, C, D, E sit in a row facing North. A sits at one end. B is second to the right of A. C is immediate left of B. D is between B and E. Who sits in the middle?
  2. (E) In a row of 30 students, Ravi is 12th from the left. What is his position from the right?
  3. (M) Six people sit in a circle. A is opposite B. C is to the right of A. D is between A and E. If F is to the left of B, who is opposite C?
  4. (M) Five friends P, Q, R, S, T sit in a row. P is at the center. Q is to the left of P. R is at an end. S is immediate right of T. If T is between P and R, what is the arrangement?
  5. (M) Seven people sit in a row. A is 3rd from the left. B is 2nd from the right. C is exactly between A and B. How many people sit between A and B?
  6. (M) In a row of 40 students, Aman is 14th from the left and Bhavna is 18th from the right. How many students are between them?
  7. (H) Eight people sit around a circular table facing center. A sits third to the right of B. C sits second to the left of B. D is between A and C. Who is exactly opposite D?
  8. (H) P, Q, R, S, T, U sit in a row. P is 2nd to the right of Q. R is 3rd to the left of S. T is immediate left of U. Q is at one end. Who is in the middle?

Section 9 — Puzzles & Input-Output (8 questions)​

  1. (E) Input: 8 3 6 1 5. Step 1: 1 8 3 6 5. Step 2: 1 3 8 6 5. What is Step 3?
  2. (E) Input: 9 4 7 2. Step 1: 2 9 4 7. Step 2: 2 4 9 7. What is Step 3?
  3. (M) Input: 15 8 22 5 12. Step 1: 5 15 8 22 12. What is the rule?
  4. (M) Three people A, B, C each like one fruit: mango, apple, orange. A does not like mango. B likes orange. What does C like?
  5. (M) Four houses in a row. Red house is left of blue. Green is right of blue. Yellow is at an end. Which house is in the middle?
  6. (M) A, B, C, D are in a race. A beats B. C beats D. B beats C. What is the order (first to last)?
  7. (H) Input: 42 17 29 8 55. Step 1: 8 42 17 29 55. Step 2: 8 17 42 29 55. Step 3: 8 17 29 42 55. If the rule is "smallest moves left one position per step", what is Step 4?
  8. (H) Five friends P, Q, R, S, T each have a different height. P is taller than Q. R is shorter than S. T is taller than S. Q is taller than R. Who is the second tallest?

Section 10 — Data Sufficiency (9 questions)​

  1. (E) What is the value of x? (I) x + y = 10 (II) x − y = 2
  2. (E) Is x even? (I) x is a multiple of 4. (II) x > 10
  3. (M) What is the age of the son? (I) Father is 30 years old. (II) Father is 3 times the son's age.
  4. (M) What is the profit percentage? (I) CP = ₹500 (II) SP = ₹575
  5. (M) Who is the tallest among A, B, C? (I) A is taller than B. (II) C is shorter than B.
  6. (M) What is the average of 5 numbers? (I) Sum of the numbers is 175. (II) The numbers are consecutive.
  7. (M) Is x > y? (I) x = 2y (II) y > 0
  8. (H) What is the value of x + y + z? (I) x + y = 20 (II) y + z = 30
  9. (H) How many students are in the class? (I) 60% are boys. (II) There are 24 girls.

Section 11 — Statement & Conclusion (8 questions)​

  1. (E) Statement: "The school will remain closed on Tuesday due to the state holiday." I. The school is closed on state holidays. II. Tuesday is a state holiday this week.
  2. (E) Statement: "Eat breakfast daily to stay healthy." I. Breakfast is important for health. II. People who skip breakfast are unhealthy.
  3. (M) Statement: "The government increased fuel prices." I. Fuel prices affect the cost of transport. II. All citizens drive cars.
  4. (M) Statement: "Company X hired 200 new employees." I. Company X is growing. II. Company X had no employees before.
  5. (M) Statement: "Regular exercise improves concentration." I. Exercise is good for the mind. II. Everyone who exercises has perfect concentration.
  6. (M) Statement: "The bus was delayed due to heavy traffic." I. Traffic can delay buses. II. The bus is always late.
  7. (H) Statement: "New technology has made computers faster and cheaper." I. Technology improves with time. II. All computers are now cheap.
  8. (H) Statement: "Only trained pilots may fly aircraft." I. Untrained people cannot fly aircraft. II. All pilots are trained.

Section 12 — Figure Series & Non-Verbal (9 questions)​

  1. (E) A figure rotates 90° clockwise each step. If the current figure has the arrow pointing North, what does the next figure look like?
  2. (E) A square gains one line inside each step. Step 1 has 1 line, Step 2 has 2. How many lines in Step 4?
  3. (M) A circle alternates shaded/unshaded. Step 1 shaded, Step 2 unshaded. What is Step 5?
  4. (M) A dot moves one corner clockwise each step. Step 1: top-left. Step 2: top-right. What is Step 3?
  5. (M) A triangle flips horizontally each step. Step 1 points left, Step 2 points right. What does Step 3 look like?
  6. (M) An arrow rotates 45° each step. Step 1 points North, Step 2 points North-East. What does Step 5 point to?
  7. (H) A figure has a dot moving clockwise AND a line gaining length each step. If Step 2 has dot top-right and line length 2, what does Step 3 have?
  8. (H) The number of sides increases by 1 each step: triangle, square, pentagon. What is Step 4?
  9. (H) Two shapes: a square rotates 90° clockwise, a circle moves one position anticlockwise each step. If square is at position 1 and circle at position 3, what happens in Step 2?

Answer Key​

Section 1 — Number & Letter Series​

  1. 49 (squares: 2², 3², 4², 5², 6², 7²)
  2. 96 (×2 each step)
  3. 157 (×2 + 3 each step: 77 × 2 + 3)
  4. 63 (×2 + 1 each step: 31 × 2 + 1)
  5. 23 (×2 + 1: 11 × 2 + 1 = 23; then 23 × 2 + 1 = 47 ✓)
  6. Y (differences +3, +5, +7, +9: P(16) + 9 = 25 = Y)
  7. 720 (factorials: 1!, 2!, 3!, 4!, 5!, 6!)
  8. N (−3 each step: Q(17) − 3 = 14 = N)
  9. 54 (pattern ×3, ÷2 alternating: 18 × 3 = 54)

Section 2 — Analogies​

  1. C) stand (cup sits on a saucer; bottle sits on a stand)
  2. B) hospital (person : workplace)
  3. B) branch (part-to-whole: chapter is part of book, branch is part of tree)
  4. B) silkworm (product : source)
  5. B) nutrition (X provides Y)
  6. B) defeat (antonym pair)
  7. B) order (direction = dispatched order; both words of authority)
  8. B) day (12 months in a year, 24 hours in a day — unit of a larger unit)

Section 3 — Odd One Out​

  1. 9 (only composite number; rest are primes)
  2. Tiger (the only wild big cat; Dog, Cat, Cow are domesticated and Wolf is a canid — Tiger is the only felid)
  3. 196 (14²; the others are 11², 13², 15², 17² — odd bases). Standard key: 196 (only even-base square).
  4. Potato (vegetable; the rest are fruits)
  5. 27 (only composite; rest prime)
  6. Triangle (2D shape; the rest are 3D solids)
  7. 57 (only composite: 3 × 19)
  8. Chandigarh (a union territory; Delhi, Mumbai, Kolkata, Chennai are state capitals)

Section 4 — Syllogisms​

  1. Only II (I cannot be guaranteed — dogs and cats may not overlap)
  2. Only II (conversion of "some pencils are erasers"; I has no link)
  3. Only II (conversion: "some flowers are roses"; I not guaranteed)
  4. Only I (no apple is a vegetable; II reverses wrongly — "all fruits are apples" false)
  5. Only I (some teachers (the professors) are educated; II claims all teachers)
  6. Both I and II (transitive chain + conversion of I)
  7. Only II (some A (those that are B) cannot be C; I says NO A is C which is too strong — A could have elements outside B that are C)
  8. Only II (conversion of "all books are paper"; I has no link)
  9. Only I (the rich doctors are educated; II reverses)

Section 5 — Coding-Decoding​

  1. EPH (+1 each letter)
  2. USBJO (+1 each letter: T→U, R→S, A→B, I→J, N→O)
  3. MPWF (+1 each letter)
  4. 26 (D=4, O=15, G=7 → 4+15+7 = 26)
  5. FAILURF (last letter +1; rest same)
  6. "good" (common word in both codes is 4 and "good")
  7. QIPOF (+1 each letter; MOBILE→NPCJMF is +1 per letter, spaces inserted as pattern)
  8. kik ("the ticket" appears in both sentences → "rik tiz"; book = kik)

Section 6 — Blood Relations​

  1. Son (A is B's brother; B is C's daughter → C is A's parent; A is male → son)
  2. His daughter (my mother's only son = me (man); daughter of me = my daughter)
  3. Mother (D is C's mother; C is B's sister → D is B's mother)
  4. Uncle (R is S's brother; S is T's father → R is T's paternal uncle)
  5. Brother-in-law (P is Q's sister; Q is R's mother → Q is S's wife; S is P's brother-in-law)
  6. Brother (A is B's son; B is C's wife → C is A's father; D is C's daughter → A and D are siblings; A is male → brother)
  7. Cannot be determined (we only know Y (a daughter); X and Z may have other children)
  8. Cousin (the woman's mother's husband = her father; his sister = the man's aunt → the woman's father is the man's father's brother → they share grandparents → first cousins)

Section 7 — Direction Sense​

  1. 5 km North-East (3-4-5 triangle)
  2. At the start (5 South + 5 North cancel)
  3. 4 km East (3N − 3S cancel; 4E remains)
  4. 3 km West (4N − 4S cancel; 3W remains)
  5. 10 km South-East (6-8-10 triangle)
  6. 5 km East, 5 km North of start (turns relative to new facing; net NE position — walk 5E then 5N)
  7. 6 km East of start (10N cancels 10S; 6E remains)
  8. 3 km South of start (5E then 5N then 5W then 8S → net: 5E − 5W = 0; 5N − 8S = 3S)

Section 8 — Seating Arrangement​

  1. B (order: A, _, B, C... no — A at end, B 2nd right of A: A _ B _ _; C left of B: A C B _ _; D between B and E: A C B D E → middle = B)
  2. 19th from the right (30 − 12 + 1 = 19)
  3. D (6 seats: A at 1, B at 4 (opposite); C right of A = seat 2; F left of B = seat 3; D at 5, E at 6 → arrangement A C F B D E; opposite C (seat 2) = seat 5 = D)
  4. Q, P, T, S, R — T in the middle (R at one end (seat 5); T between P and R → T at 4; S immediate right of T → S at 5; Q immediate left of P → Q at 1, P at 2 → order Q P T S R; middle = T)
  5. 3 people (A 3rd from left = position 3; B 2nd from right of 7 = position 6; C between → positions 4, 5 = 2 people between; wait — exactly between A and B: (3+6)/2 = 4.5 not integer → redo: 7 people, A at 3, B at 6; people between = 4 and 5 → 2 people)
  6. 8 students (Bhavna is 23rd from left; between = 23 − 14 − 1 = 8)
  7. Cannot be determined (the clues do not fully fix the 8-seat circle; position D depends on the unstated direction of A's "third to the right" turn)
  8. Q R P T S U — middle pair P and T (Q at end (seat 1); P 2nd right of Q → seat 3; S at 5, R 3rd left of S → seat 2; T left of U → T at 4, U at 6 → order Q R P T S U)

Section 9 — Puzzles & Input-Output​

  1. 1 3 6 8 5 (smallest already in place; next smallest 3 then 6 then 8 swap left: Step 3 = 1 3 6 8 5)
  2. 2 4 7 9 (ascending one swap per step)
  3. Smallest moves to front one position per step (5 < 8 < 15, so 5 leads)
  4. Mango (B likes orange; A not mango → A apple; C = mango)
  5. Blue (order: Red Blue Green Yellow with yellow at end → Red, Blue, Green, Yellow → middle two... order Red-Blue-Green-Yellow: middle = Blue & Green; with yellow at end and red leftmost: Red, Blue, Green, Yellow → middle = Blue, Green; the "middle" single = Blue by position 2)
  6. A, B, C, D (A beats B; B beats C; C beats D)
  7. 8 17 29 42 55 (already sorted; Step 4 = same as Step 3)
  8. T (T > S > R < Q... order: T tallest? P > Q > R, S > R, T > S → P vs T unknown; chains: T > S > R, Q > R; P > Q → P could be tallest; second tallest = Q if P tallest. Given ambiguity, key: order P > Q > R and T > S > R; T vs Q unknown → cannot be determined. Standard key: Cannot be determined)

Section 10 — Data Sufficiency​

  1. C — both together (x = 6, y = 4)
  2. A — I alone (multiple of 4 is always even; II insufficient)
  3. C — both together (son = 10)
  4. C — both together (profit = 15%)
  5. C — both together (A > B > C → A tallest)
  6. A — I alone (average = 175/5 = 35; II gives no numbers)
  7. C — both together (y > 0 and x = 2y → x > y)
  8. E — even both insufficient (three unknowns, two equations)
  9. C — both together (24 girls = 40% → 60 students)

Section 11 — Statement & Conclusion​

  1. Both I and II (both follow from the statement)
  2. Only I (II overgeneralizes)
  3. Only I (II extreme — not all citizens drive)
  4. Only I (hiring suggests growth; II unsupported)
  5. Only I (II uses "everyone" — extreme)
  6. Only I (II "always" — extreme)
  7. Only I (II "all computers cheap" — extreme)
  8. Both I and II (only trained may fly → untrained cannot; all pilots are trained)

Section 12 — Figure Series​

  1. Arrow pointing East (90° clockwise from North)
  2. 4 lines (one per step)
  3. Shaded (alternates: 1 shaded, 2 unshaded, 3 shaded, 4 unshaded, 5 shaded)
  4. Bottom-right (top-left → top-right → bottom-right)
  5. Points left (alternates left, right, left)
  6. South (45° per step: Step 1 = N, 2 = NE, 3 = E, 4 = SE, 5 = S)
  7. Dot at bottom-right, line length 3 (each element follows its own rule)
  8. Hexagon (sides +1 each step)
  9. Square rotates to position 2, circle moves to position 2 (square clockwise, circle anticlockwise — both land at adjacent positions)
