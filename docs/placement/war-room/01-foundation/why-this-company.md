# Why Do You Want to Join Us?

> **Status:** ○ Planned · Edit this line to ◐ Practicing · ● Drilled

This question is the second one in 80% of interviews. The interviewer is checking one thing: **did you do 15 minutes of homework on us, or did you spray your resume at 100 companies?**

The answer is built, not improvised. Three concrete reasons, no hype, one sentence that ties their work to yours.

---

## Inhale.

Open the company's website in a new tab. Read it for ten minutes before you read the script below.

Now come back. Take a breath. Then read.

---

## The Script — 60 seconds

> **Opening (10s)** — "Sir, three specific reasons. I researched [Company Name] before this interview, so let me be concrete."
>
> **Reason 1 — Product/Tech (15s)** — "First, your [specific product / engineering blog post / open-source repo] caught my attention. The way you've built [specific feature — e.g., your search infrastructure, your payment reconciliation system, your AI assistant] is exactly the kind of system I want to work on, because I've already built a smaller version of it in my [Project Name]."
>
> **Reason 2 — Team/Culture (15s)** — "Second, your engineering culture — I read [name of engineer's blog post / podcast / talk on YouTube]. The emphasis on [code review rigor / writing culture / shipping small frequent changes / engineer ownership] matches how I want to work."
>
> **Reason 3 — Growth (15s)** — "Third, this role gives me a clear next step. You're hiring for [specific stack — Laravel + AWS / Python + LLMs / React + Node]. That's the stack I've been deepening for the last [N] months. I want to do that work at a company that ships to real users, not just for college projects."
>
> **Close (5s)** — "That's why this is one of three companies I'm actively interviewing with, not one of fifty I've applied to."

Sixty seconds. Three specific reasons. Closes with a line that signals you're selective.

---

## The Anatomy — why this wins

The interviewer is testing for **specificity**. Generic answers ("you're a leader in tech, great work culture") tell them you copy-pasted the answer. Specific answers ("I read your blog post on X, your team wrote Y") tell them you spent real time.

Reason 1 ties their product to **your work**. Not "your product is cool" — *"I've built a smaller version of it."* That signals capability, not flattery.

Reason 2 cites a **named source** (a blog, a talk, a tweet). The interviewer thinks: *this person actually reads.*

Reason 3 names **the exact stack** in the JD. It tells the hiring manager you read the job description, not just the company name.

The close is the kill shot. *"One of three companies I'm interviewing with"* signals you have options. People want what other people want.

## How to research a company in 15 minutes

You don't need an hour. You need a structured 15.

```text
Minute 0–3   — Homepage. What do they sell? Who is the customer?
Minute 3–6   — Engineering blog / Medium / Dev.to. Read 1 recent post.
Minute 6–9   — LinkedIn page of one engineer on the team. What do they post?
Minute 9–12  — Their GitHub org. What's their main public repo?
Minute 12–15 — The JD again. Pick 3 exact phrases from it to mirror back.
```

By minute 15 you have:

- One specific product or feature to name
- One blog post / talk to cite
- One engineer's name (drop only if asked — *"I read your CTO's post on…"*)
- Three exact stack keywords from the JD

That's the raw material. Now you write the script.

## Fill in your blanks

```text
[Company Name]:
[Specific product / feature / engineering blog post]:
[Smaller version you built — your project]:
[Named blog post / podcast / talk]:
[Cultural value you align with]:
[Exact stack from JD]:
[Months you've been deepening this stack]:
```

If you genuinely can't find a blog or engineer post — many smaller companies don't have one — pivot Reason 2 to: *"I spoke to [employee on LinkedIn / alumni from my college / someone at a meetup]. They said [specific concrete thing about the work]."*

If you can't find a real human either, swap Reason 2 to: *"Your product solves [specific pain] for [specific user]. I've worked on a similar problem in [your project], so I know how hard [specific technical challenge] is."*

## The 5-day practice drill

- [ ] **Day 1 — Pick your top 3 target companies.** Spend the 15-min research drill on each. Save the notes.
- [ ] **Day 2 — Write the 60s script for company #1.** Read it aloud. Time it. Land at 55–65 seconds.
- [ ] **Day 3 — Write scripts for companies #2 and #3.** Notice which reasons repeat — those are your generic ones, replace them.
- [ ] **Day 4 — Record yourself delivering the script.** Watch the playback. Mark any sentence that sounds like flattery (it'll feel oily on video).
- [ ] **Day 5 — Cold drill.** Pick a random company you're targeting, give yourself 15 minutes to research, then deliver the 60s script on camera. That's the real test.

## What NOT to say

These are the answers that get you rejected. Memorize the list so you never accidentally say one.

| Don't say | Why it kills | Say instead |
|---|---|---|
| *"Good salary and benefits"* | They know. Everyone wants money. You sound transactional. | Save compensation for the offer call. |
| *"It's close to my home"* | Convenience signals you'd leave for any closer offer. | Don't mention location unless they ask. |
| *"You're a big brand / MNC"* | Brand chasing = no homework done. | Cite a specific product or post, not the brand. |
| *"Great work-life balance"* | They hear this 50 times a day. Cliché. | Skip culture clichés. Cite a named blog or talk. |
| *"My friend works here"* | Nepotism whiff. Doesn't tell them why **you** fit. | Use the referral for the application, not the answer. |
| *"You're hiring freshers"* | Sounds like you'd take any job. | Cite the role's specifics, not the eligibility. |
| *"I want to learn"* | They're hiring engineers, not students. | Show what you've already built — then say how this role extends it. |

## Recovery — when you didn't research

It happens. Recruiter calls you cold. You forgot to read the company page. The question lands.

Here's the line:

> *"Sir, can I be honest — I have a one-line answer ready, but I'd rather give you the real one. Could you tell me what your team is working on right now? I want to anchor my answer to something specific, not just recite the website."*

This is risky. It buys you 30 seconds of their answer, which you then mirror back into your reason. It only works if you sound **genuinely curious**, not unprepared.

Then deliver:

> *"What you just described — [paraphrase their answer] — is exactly the kind of system I want to work on. I've built a smaller version of [related thing] in [your project]. That's why this role fits."*

You converted a blank into a conversation. Senior interviewers respect this more than a memorized script. Junior ones might not. Read the room.

## Variants

=== "30s — Quick screen"

    > "Three reasons. One — your [specific product] is the kind of system I want to work on, and I've built a smaller version in [project]. Two — your engineering blog on [topic] matches how I want to write code. Three — the stack you're hiring for is the stack I've been going deep on for the last six months. That's a clean fit."

    Use when: HR phone screen, recruiter call, 5-minute pre-screen.

=== "60s — Default"

    See main script above.

    Use when: standard interview, 30-minute slot, after they asked "tell me about yourself."

=== "2min — Panel / senior round"

    Use the 60s script as the spine. After the close, pause. If they want more, continue:

    > "I'll add a fourth — I'm at a stage where the team I join matters more than the company name. I want a team that ships, reviews each other's code, and doesn't drown in process. From [blog post / talk / Glassdoor pattern], that seems like your team."

    Use when: panel interview with the hiring manager, founder round at a startup, 45-min slot.

## Common traps

| Trap | Why it kills | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Generic ("you're a great company") | Tells them you didn't research | Always name one specific product, post, or person |
| Same script for every company | Recruiter can tell — they hear it daily | Rewrite Reason 1 and 2 per company; Reason 3 can repeat |
| Quoting their mission statement | Sounds like website copy | Quote a specific engineer or blog post, not marketing copy |
| Praising the brand | Brand chasing = junior energy | Praise the work, not the logo |
| Talking only about what *you* get | One-sided, no fit signal | Reason 1 = their work. Reason 3 = your fit. Balance both. |
