# One Hour Before

> **Status:** ○ Planned · Edit this line to ◐ Practicing · ● Drilled

You're in transit. The interview is in 60 minutes. The next hour is not for studying — it's for *arriving calm*. The candidate who arrives calm beats the candidate who arrives crammed.

This drill is the final hour. Travel buffer → arrival → restroom → water → breath → posture → smile → walk in.

---

## Inhale.

The work is done. The studying is done. The clothes are on. Right now, your only job is to *arrive*.

Don't open a tech topic. Don't quiz yourself. Don't read LeetCode. You will psych yourself out.

---

## The Script — what you tell yourself

> *"The studying is over. My only job now is to arrive calm, on time, and ready to have a conversation."*

Conversation. Not test. Conversation.

---

## The Anatomy — why the final hour matters

The cortisol curve in the final hour is the danger zone. Most candidates either:

- **Over-study** — opens phone, reads tech notes, finds one topic they "forgot," spirals into panic.
- **Under-prep mentally** — arrives, walks in cold, fumbles the opening.

The right move is *mental warm-up, not knowledge warm-up*. Breath. Posture. Smile. The material is already loaded.

---

## The Final-Hour Calendar

### 60 min before — Travel buffer

- [ ] **You should already be in transit.** If you're not yet leaving, leave now.
- [ ] **Goal: arrive at the building 20–30 minutes early.** Then you have time to settle.

**While in transit (do NOT do):**

- Don't open notes, war-room files, LeetCode, or tech videos.
- Don't text 10 friends "wish me luck."
- Don't scroll Reddit / Twitter — emotional volatility.

**While in transit (DO):**

- Listen to one calm song or podcast you've heard before. Familiar audio.
- Practice box breathing on the train/in the car. 4-4-4-4. Ten cycles.
- Look out the window. Eyes on horizon, not on phone.

### 30 min before — Arrive at the area

- [ ] **You're at the office building or in the parking lot.**
- [ ] **Find a café or quiet spot for the next 15 minutes.** Don't sit in the office lobby for 30 minutes — it gets uncomfortable.
- [ ] **Order water or a light drink.** No new caffeine. Sit. Breathe.

### 20 min before — Restroom + Reset

- [ ] **Use the restroom.** Even if you don't think you need it. Adrenaline hides the urge until mid-interview.
- [ ] **Wash hands. Cold water on the wrists** — drops heart rate.
- [ ] **Mirror check** — hair, collar, teeth, fly. Done in 10 seconds.
- [ ] **Posture in mirror** — shoulders back, chin up, smile briefly. Carry this posture out.
- [ ] **Phone on silent or airplane mode.** Not vibrate. Silent.
- [ ] **Refill water bottle.** Sip on the way to reception.

### 15 min before — Walk into the lobby

- [ ] **Arrive at reception.** *"Hi, I'm [Name], here for an interview with [Person] at [time]."*
- [ ] **Smile at the receptionist.** They sometimes report back. Always nice. Never rushed.
- [ ] **Sit upright in the waiting area.** Both feet flat. Not slouched. Not crossed-legged. Posture sets state.
- [ ] **Bag at your side, resume copies on top.** Not buried.
- [ ] **Phone face-down.** Don't scroll.

### 10 min before — Final mental prep

- [ ] **Read your prepared questions for the interviewer** — the 3-page printout you brought. This is the ONLY thing you read in this hour. Why? Because *those questions are your power move* — being mentally fresh on them means you'll deliver them with intent.
- [ ] **Don't re-read tech notes.** Don't re-read STAR. Don't re-read TMAY. You'll panic if any of them feel rusty in this state.
- [ ] **Box breathing — 2 minutes.** Subtle. No one notices.
- [ ] **Affirmation — silent.** *"I've done the prep. I know my material. I'm here to have a conversation. Whatever they ask, I have something to say."*

### 5 min before — Standing by

- [ ] **Stand up if seated for 30+ minutes.** Roll shoulders. Stretch fingers. Get blood moving.
- [ ] **One last smile in any reflective surface.** Window, phone screen, glass partition.
- [ ] **Breathe slowly.** Inhale through nose 4 counts. Exhale through mouth 6 counts. Three times.
- [ ] **Posture: chin up, shoulders back, eyes forward.**

### 0 min — Interviewer calls your name

- [ ] **Stand smoothly.** Don't fumble bag.
- [ ] **Smile. Make eye contact. Walk over.**
- [ ] **Firm handshake** *(if culturally appropriate)*. Or warm namaste / nod. Not weak. Not crushing.
- [ ] **Greeting:** *"Hi [Name], thanks for having me — really excited to be here."* (Or skip "Sir/Ma'am" unless you've gauged the room.)
- [ ] **Follow them into the room. Sit when invited. Both feet flat. Hands on the table or relaxed in lap.**
- [ ] **Wait for their first question. Pause one beat before answering.**

## What to read in the waiting room

**Only your prepared questions for the interviewer.**

Why? Because:

1. They're already memorized — re-reading reinforces, doesn't overwhelm.
2. Reading them puts you in *curiosity mode* (asking) instead of *test mode* (answering). Different mental state.
3. The first thing you'll need from your brain in the interview is calm conversational tone. Reading your own thoughtful questions primes that tone.

**Do NOT read in the waiting room:**

- Tech topic notes. You'll find one you "forgot" and panic.
- War-room scripts. You'll start rehearsing aloud in your head and miss your name being called.
- Resume. You wrote it. You know it. Re-reading it doesn't add anything.
- News, social media, LeetCode. All bad. Cognitive bandwidth, anxiety, or both.

## The mindfulness micro-drills

Three tools. All silent. All take under a minute. Use anywhere.

### Tool 1 — Box breathing (60 seconds)

- Inhale 4 counts through nose.
- Hold 4 counts.
- Exhale 4 counts through mouth.
- Hold 4 counts.
- Repeat 4×.

Used by Navy SEALs before missions. Drops heart rate, calms the limbic system. No one watching can tell you're doing it.

### Tool 2 — 5-4-3-2-1 grounding (90 seconds)

When anxiety spikes, name silently:

- 5 things you can see.
- 4 things you can touch.
- 3 things you can hear.
- 2 things you can smell.
- 1 thing you can taste.

Pulls you out of "what if I fail" thought spiral, back into the room.

### Tool 3 — Power posture (60 seconds)

In the restroom, stand for 60 seconds:

- Feet shoulder-width apart.
- Hands on hips OR raised above head.
- Chin up. Shoulders back.

Amy Cuddy's research is contested on hormones, but the *behavioral effect* is well-documented — you walk out more confident. Costs nothing. Try it.

## The 5-day practice drill

- [ ] **Day 1 — Practice the 60-min arrival flow** for a non-interview event. A coffee meeting, a meetup, anything where being early matters. Note how the routine feels.
- [ ] **Day 2 — Box breathing 5×/day, no special occasion.** Build the muscle so it's automatic on the day.
- [ ] **Day 3 — Walk into a lobby and read your "interviewer questions" for 10 minutes.** See if reading them actually does feel different from reading tech notes.
- [ ] **Day 4 — Silent affirmation rehearsal.** Practice the *"I've done the prep…"* line silently 10× until it surfaces automatically when nervous.
- [ ] **Day 5 — Full dress rehearsal.** Wear the outfit, pack the bag, leave the house, arrive at a random office building, do the full final-hour routine, walk away.

## Recovery — when the final hour goes wrong

**Lift delay / locked office:**

- Stay calm. Use the wait for box breathing.
- If after the scheduled time, call the recruiter. *"I'm in the lobby, the lift is stuck — could someone come escort me?"*

**Pre-interview panic attack (rare but real):**

- Find a restroom stall or quiet corner.
- 5-4-3-2-1 grounding (above).
- Cold water on wrists and back of neck.
- Three slow breaths. Stand. Smile. Walk out.

**You realize you forgot resume copies:**

- Most interviewers don't read your printed resume — they have it on screen. Don't apologize unprompted. If they ask, *"I have it digitally — happy to email it now."*

**Interview gets delayed 30+ minutes:**

- Don't open the laptop. Don't crack tech notes. Stay in the same calm state.
- Re-read interviewer questions. Sip water. Repeat box breathing.

## Variants

=== "Onsite interview"

    Default flow above. Add: arrive at building 30 min early, café for 15, lobby for 15.

=== "Virtual interview from home"

    Subtract travel time. Sit at desk 20 min before. Tech-check once at 15 min before, then close the meeting tab. Do NOT keep clicking 'test camera' — it spikes anxiety. Trust the earlier check.

=== "Virtual from a hotel / borrowed space"

    Add 30 min for setting up the room — adjust lighting, hide visible mess in camera frame, test wifi, put a "Do Not Disturb" sign on the door if shared space.

=== "Interview in unfamiliar city"

    Travel buffer = 60 min, not 30. Map the route the day before. Note metro/bus routes in case Uber surge.

=== "Multi-round day (3+ back-to-back)"

    Between rounds: don't text friends, don't review notes. Restroom → water → 2 min breath → next round.

## Common traps

| Trap | Why it kills | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Studying in the lobby | Surfaces a "forgotten" topic, panic | Only re-read interviewer questions |
| Phone vibrating in pocket mid-interview | Distracts you and interviewer | Silent mode, not vibrate |
| Arriving 2 min early | No buffer for restroom/breath | Arrive 20–30 min early |
| Arriving 60 min early | Awkward lobby loitering | Café first, lobby at 15 min |
| Cold caffeine right before | Jitters during opening | Stop caffeine 90 min before |
| Heavy meal in last hour | Sluggish, sleepy | Light snack only |
| Texting "wish me luck" | Each reply spikes anxiety | Phone silent, ignore all |
| Slouching in waiting area | Sets weak state | Sit upright, both feet flat |
| Re-reading tech notes | Panic spiral on one weak topic | Only re-read interviewer questions |
| Skipping restroom break | Adrenaline hides urge until mid-interview | Always use restroom, even if not needed |
