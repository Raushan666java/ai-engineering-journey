Chapter 6: Permutation & Combination, Probability — Basic Counting, Arrangements, Selections, Coins, Dice, Cards, Balls

Learning Objectives​

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

  • Apply the fundamental principle of counting to solve arrangement problems
  • Calculate permutations (arrangements) of distinct and identical objects
  • Calculate combinations (selections) from a group of items
  • Solve probability problems involving coins, dice, cards, and balls
  • Differentiate between permutation and combination in exam questions
  • Use shortcut formulas for IBPS SO-level probability problems
  • Solve miscellaneous counting problems efficiently

Theory​

1. Fundamental Principle of Counting​

Multiplication Rule:

If one task can be done in m ways and another independent task can be done in ways, then both tasks together can be done in m × n ways.

Addition Rule:

If one task can be done in m ways and another disjoint task can be done in ways, then either of the two tasks can be done in m + n ways.

2. Factorial​

n! = n × (n-1) × (n-2) × ... × 3 × 2 × 1

Special case: �! = 1

Factorial Values:

  • 1! = 1
  • 2! = 2
  • 3! = 6
  • 4! = 24
  • 5! = 120
  • 6! = 720
  • 7! = 5040
  • 8! = 40320
  • 9! = 362880
  • 10! = 3628800

3. Permutation (Arrangement)​

A permutation is an arrangement of objects in a specific order. Order matters.

Formula:

Number of ways to arrange objects out of distinct objects:

P(n, r) = n! / (n - r)!

Special Cases:

  • Number of arrangements of distinct objects in a line: !
  • Number of arrangements of objects in a circle: (n-1)!

Permutations with Repetition (Arrangements with Identical Objects):

If there are objects where p are of one type, q of another type, and of another:

Number of arrangements = n! / (p! × q! × r!)

Permutations with Repetition Allowed:

If each of positions can be filled from objects (repetition allowed):

Number of arrangements = n^r

4. Combination (Selection)​

A combination is a selection of objects where order does NOT matter.

Formula:

Number of ways to select objects out of distinct objects:

C(n, r) = n! / [r! × (n - r)!]

Properties of Combinations:

C(n, r) = C(n, n-r) C(n, 0) = C(n, n) = 1 C(n, 1) = n C(n, r) + C(n, r-1) = C(n+1, r)

Important Identity:

C(n, 0) + C(n, 1) + C(n, 2) + ... + C(n, n) = 2^n

5. Probability​

Definition:

If an experiment has S equally likely outcomes and an event E has F favourable outcomes:

P(E) = F / S = Number of favourable outcomes / Total number of outcomes

Range of Probability:

0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1

  • P(E) = 0: Impossible event
  • P(E) = 1: Certain event

Complement of an Event:

P(Not E) = 1 - P(E)

Addition Rule:

P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A ∩ B)

For mutually exclusive events: P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B)

Multiplication Rule (Independent Events):

P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B)

Conditional Probability:

P(A|B) = P(A ∩ B) / P(B)

Expected Value:

E(X) = Σ xi × P(xi)

6. Coin Problems​

For tossing coins:

  • Total outcomes = 2^n
  • Number of ways to get exactly heads = C(n, r)
  • P(exactly r heads) = C(n, r) / 2^n

8. Card Problems (Standard 52-Card Deck)​

  • 4 suits: Spades, Hearts, Diamonds, Clubs
  • 13 cards per suit: A, 2-10, J, Q, K
  • 26 red cards (Hearts + Diamonds), 26 black cards (Spades + Clubs)
  • 12 face cards (J, Q, K in each of 4 suits)
  • 4 aces

Key Probabilities:

  • P(drawing a king) = 4/52 = 1/13
  • P(drawing a heart) = 13/52 = 1/4
  • P(drawing a face card) = 12/52 = 3/13

9. Ball Problems​

Drawing balls from a bag without replacement uses combinations.

Formula for probability of drawing specific type from a mixed bag:

If bag has  red and  blue balls, probability of picking exactly red in draws (without replacement):

P = C(a, r) × C(b, n-r) / C(a+b, n)

Examples​

Example 1: Fundamental Counting Principle​

Question: How many 3-digit numbers can be formed using the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 without repetition?

Solution:

First digit: 5 choices (1-5) Second digit: 4 choices (remaining digits) Third digit: 3 choices

Total = 5 × 4 × 3 = 60 numbers

Example 2: Permutation​

Question: In how many ways can 6 books be arranged on a shelf?

Solution:

6 distinct books can be arranged in 6! ways. 6! = 6 × 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 × 1 = 720 ways

Example 3: Permutation with Selection​

Question: How many 4-letter words (with or without meaning) can be formed from the letters of the word "COMPUTER" without repetition?

Solution:

Number of letters in COMPUTER = 8 We need to select and arrange 4 letters out of 8.

P(8, 4) = 8! / (8-4)! = 8! / 4! = (8 × 7 × 6 × 5 × 4!) / 4! = 8 × 7 × 6 × 5 = 1680 words

Example 4: Combination​

Question: In how many ways can a committee of 3 students be selected from a class of 15 students?

Solution:

C(15, 3) = 15! / (3! × 12!) = (15 × 14 × 13) / (3 × 2 × 1) = 2730 / 6 = 455 ways

Example 5: Coin Probability​

Question: Two unbiased coins are tossed simultaneously. Find the probability of getting exactly one head.

Solution:

Total outcomes when tossing 2 coins: {HH, HT, TH, TT} = 4 Favourable outcomes (exactly one head): {HT, TH} = 2

P(exactly one head) = 2/4 = 1/2

Example 6: Dice Probability​

Question: Two dice are rolled. Find the probability that the sum of the numbers is 7.

Solution:

Total outcomes = 6 × 6 = 36 Favourable outcomes (sum = 7): (1,6), (2,5), (3,4), (4,3), (5,2), (6,1) = 6

P(sum = 7) = 6/36 = 1/6

Example 7: Card Probability​

Question: One card is drawn from a well-shuffled pack of 52 cards. Find the probability that it is either a king or a queen.

Solution:

Total cards = 52 Number of kings = 4 Number of queens = 4 Kings and queens are mutually exclusive, so: P(King or Queen) = 4/52 + 4/52 = 8/52 = 2/13

Example 8: Ball Probability​

Question: A bag contains 5 red balls and 7 blue balls. Two balls are drawn at random without replacement. Find the probability that both are red.

Solution:

Total balls = 5 + 7 = 12 Total ways to choose 2 balls = C(12, 2) = 66 Ways to choose 2 red balls = C(5, 2) = 10

P(both red) = 10/66 = 5/33

Example 9: Permutation with Identical Objects​

Question: How many distinct arrangements can be made from the letters of the word "MISSISSIPPI"?

Solution:

Total letters = 11 M = 1, I = 4, S = 4, P = 2

Number of arrangements = 11! / (4! × 4! × 2!) = 39916800 / (24 × 24 × 2) = 39916800 / 1152 = 34650 arrangements

Example 10: Addition Theorem​

Question: The probability that A solves a problem is 0.7 and the probability that B solves it is 0.5. If the probability that both solve it is 0.35, find the probability that at least one solves it.

Solution:

P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A ∩ B) = 0.7 + 0.5 - 0.35 = 0.85

Example 11: Conditional Probability​

Question: Two dice are rolled. Given that the sum is greater than 8, find the probability that the sum is 10.

Solution:

Total outcomes for sum > 8: Sum 9: (3,6),(4,5),(5,4),(6,3) = 4 Sum 10: (4,6),(5,5),(6,4) = 3 Sum 11: (5,6),(6,5) = 2 Sum 12: (6,6) = 1 Total outcomes with sum > 8 = 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 10

P(sum = 10 | sum > 8) = P(sum=10 AND sum>8) / P(sum>8) = (3/36) / (10/36) = 3/10

Example 12: Selection with Constraints​

Question: A committee of 5 is to be formed from 6 men and 4 women. In how many ways can it be done if the committee must have at least 3 women?

Solution:

Case 1: 3 women, 2 men = C(4,3) × C(6,2) = 4 × 15 = 60 Case 2: 4 women, 1 man = C(4,4) × C(6,1) = 1 × 6 = 6

Total = 60 + 6 = 66 ways

Example 13: IBPS SO Level — Probability with Cards​

Question: From a well-shuffled pack of 52 cards, 2 cards are drawn at random. Find the probability that both are aces.

Solution:

Total ways to draw 2 cards = C(52, 2) = 1326 Ways to draw 2 aces = C(4, 2) = 6

P(both aces) = 6/1326 = 1/221

Example 14: IBPS SO Level — Permutation with Constraints​

Question: How many 4-digit numbers can be formed using the digits 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 without repetition if the number must be even?

Solution:

For an even number, the last digit must be 0, 2, or 4.

Case 1: Last digit = 0 First digit: 5 choices (1,2,3,4,5) Second digit: 4 choices Third digit: 3 choices Total for Case 1 = 5 × 4 × 3 = 60

Case 2: Last digit = 2 First digit: 4 choices (cannot be 0 or 2) Second digit: 4 choices (remaining including 0) Third digit: 3 choices Total for Case 2 = 4 × 4 × 3 = 48

Case 3: Last digit = 4 (same as Case 2) Total for Case 3 = 48

Total even numbers = 60 + 48 + 48 = 156

Example 15: Expected Value​

Question: In a game, a player tosses a fair coin. If it shows heads, he wins ₹10; if tails, he loses ₹5. Find his expected gain.

Solution:

P(Heads) = 1/2, Gain = ₹10 P(Tails) = 1/2, Gain = -₹5

Expected Value = (1/2 × 10) + (1/2 × (-5)) = 5 - 2.5 = ₹2.5

Shortcut Methods​

Shortcut 1: Permutation vs Combination​

Ask: "Does swapping two items give a different result?"

  • Yes → Permutation (order matters)
  • No → Combination (order does not matter)

Shortcut 2: Simplifying P(n,r) and C(n,r)​

For P(n,r): multiply n, n-1, n-2, ... for r terms (don't expand factorials fully) For C(n,r): use the formula C(n,r) = C(n, n-r) — choose the smaller r for easier computation

Shortcut 3: Sum of Two Dice​

Memorise: sum of 7 is the most probable (6/36 = 1/6) The probabilities are symmetric around 7.

Shortcut 4: Card Probabilities​

P(face card) = 3/13, P(ace) = 1/13, P(heart) = 1/4 These are the most commonly tested card probabilities.

Shortcut 5: "At Least One" Probability​

P(at least one) = 1 - P(none)

This is especially useful for coin tosses and dice rolls.

Shortcut 6: Ball Problems​

Always use combinations for "drawn without replacement" problems. The formula C(a, r) × C(b, n-r) / C(a+b, n) is the key.

Shortcut 7: Arrangements with Constraints​

For arrangements where certain items must be together, treat them as a single unit and arrange internally. For arrangements where certain items must NOT be together, find total arrangements and subtract those where they ARE together.

Shortcut 8: Digit Sum and Selection​

For number formation problems, always consider cases based on the constraint (even/odd/divisible by 5).

Shortcut 9: Independent Events​

If events are independent, multiply probabilities: P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B)

Shortcut 10: Mutually Exclusive Events​

If events cannot happen simultaneously (mutually exclusive), add probabilities: P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B)

TypeScript Implementation: Permutation, Combination & Probability Calculator​

// pnc-probability.ts — Permutation, Combination & Probability tools

class Factorial {
  static compute(n: number): number {
    if (n < 0) throw new Error("Negative factorial undefined");
    if (n <= 1) return 1;
    let result = 1;
    for (let i = 2; i <= n; i++) result *= i;
    return result;
  }

  static memoized: Record<number, number> = { 0: 1, 1: 1 };
  static computeMemo(n: number): number {
    if (this.memoized[n] !== undefined) return this.memoized[n];
    this.memoized[n] = n * this.computeMemo(n - 1);
    return this.memoized[n];
  }
}

class PermutationCalculator {
  static permutation(n: number, r: number): number {
    if (r > n) return 0;
    let result = 1;
    for (let i = n; i > n - r; i--) result *= i;
    return result;
  }

  static circular(n: number): number {
    return Factorial.compute(n - 1);
  }

  static withRepetition(n: number, r: number): number {
    return Math.pow(n, r);
  }

  static withIdentical(n: number, groups: number[]): number {
    let result = Factorial.compute(n);
    for (const g of groups) result /= Factorial.compute(g);
    return result;
  }

  static arrangementsWithConstraint(
    total: number,
    togetherCount: number
  ): number {
    // Treat 'togetherCount' items as a single unit
    const units = total - togetherCount + 1;
    return Factorial.compute(units) * Factorial.compute(togetherCount);
  }
}

class CombinationCalculator {
  static combination(n: number, r: number): number {
    if (r > n) return 0;
    if (r === 0 || r === n) return 1;
    if (r > n - r) r = n - r; // Use smaller r for efficiency
    let result = 1;
    for (let i = 1; i <= r; i++) {
      result = (result * (n - i + 1)) / i;
    }
    return result;
  }

  static selectionWithConstraint(
    typeA: number,
    typeB: number,
    selectA: number,
    selectB: number
  ): number {
    return (
      this.combination(typeA, selectA) * this.combination(typeB, selectB)
    );
  }

  static atLeast(
    totalA: number,
    totalB: number,
    select: number,
    minA: number
  ): number {
    let total = 0;
    for (let a = minA; a <= Math.min(totalA, select); a++) {
      const b = select - a;
      if (b >= 0 && b <= totalB) {
        total += this.combination(totalA, a) * this.combination(totalB, b);
      }
    }
    return total;
  }
}

class ProbabilityCalculator {
  static coinToss(n: number, heads: number): number {
    return CombinationCalculator.combination(n, heads) / Math.pow(2, n);
  }

  static atLeastOneCoin(n: number): number {
    return 1 - 1 / Math.pow(2, n);
  }

  static diceSum(nDice: number, targetSum: number): number {
    // Counts favourable outcomes for 2 dice
    if (nDice === 2) {
      if (targetSum < 2 || targetSum > 12) return 0;
      const count = 6 - Math.abs(targetSum - 7);
      return count / 36;
    }
    return 0; // Simplified for 2 dice only
  }

  static cardDraw(
    totalCards: number,
    specificCards: number,
    draw: number,
    desired: number
  ): number {
    const favourable = CombinationCalculator.combination(specificCards, desired) *
      CombinationCalculator.combination(totalCards - specificCards, draw - desired);
    const total = CombinationCalculator.combination(totalCards, draw);
    return favourable / total;
  }

  static ballPick(
    red: number,
    blue: number,
    draw: number,
    desiredRed: number
  ): number {
    const favourable =
      CombinationCalculator.combination(red, desiredRed) *
      CombinationCalculator.combination(blue, draw - desiredRed);
    const total = CombinationCalculator.combination(red + blue, draw);
    return favourable / total;
  }

  static atLeastOne(
    total: number,
    defective: number,
    draw: number
  ): number {
    return 1 - this.cardDraw(total, total - defective, draw, draw);
  }

  static conditionalProbability(
    probAandB: number,
    probB: number
  ): number {
    return probAandB / probB;
  }

  static expectedValue(
    outcomes: { value: number; probability: number }[]
  ): number {
    return outcomes.reduce((sum, o) => sum + o.value * o.probability, 0);
  }
}

📝 Solved Examples (20 MCQs)​

Set 1: Permutation & Combination (Questions 1–8)​

Question 1: How many 4-digit numbers can be formed using the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 without repetition?

Answer & Solution
**Formula:** Number of arrangements = P(n,r) = n!/(n−r)!

P(6,4) = 6 × 5 × 4 × 3 = 360 numbers

Answer: 360

Question 2: In how many ways can 7 books be arranged on a shelf?

Answer & Solution
**Formula:** n distinct objects can be arranged in n! ways.

7! = 5040 ways

Answer: 5040

Question 3: How many distinct arrangements can be made from the word "BANANA"?

Answer & Solution
**Formula:** n!/(p! × q! × r!) where p,q,r are counts of identical items.

B = 1, A = 3, N = 2, total = 6 Arrangements = 6!/(3! × 2!) = 720/(6 × 2) = 720/12 = 60

Answer: 60

Question 4: A committee of 5 is to be formed from 8 men and 4 women. In how many ways can it be done if the committee must have exactly 3 men?

Answer & Solution
**Formula:** C(men, selected) × C(women, selected)

Select 3 men from 8: C(8,3) = 56 Select 2 women from 4: C(4,2) = 6 Total = 56 × 6 = 336 ways

Answer: 336

Question 5: How many 4-digit numbers divisible by 5 can be formed from digits 0-9 without repetition?

Answer & Solution
**Formula:** Numbers divisible by 5 end in 0 or 5. Consider cases.

Case 1 (last digit = 0): First digit: 9 choices (1-9), 2nd: 8, 3rd: 7 → 9×8×7 = 504 Case 2 (last digit = 5): First digit: 8 choices (1-9 except 5), 2nd: 8, 3rd: 7 → 8×8×7 = 448 Total = 504 + 448 = 952

Answer: 952

Question 6: In how many ways can 6 people be seated around a circular table?

Answer & Solution
**Formula:** Circular arrangements = (n−1)!

(6−1)! = 5! = 120 ways

Answer: 120

Question 7: A box contains 5 red, 3 blue, and 2 green balls. In how many ways can 3 balls be selected such that at least one is red?

Answer & Solution
**Formula:** Total selections − selections with no red = C(10,3) − C(5,3)

Total = C(10,3) = 120 No red = C(5,3) = 10 (selecting 3 from 5 non-red) At least one red = 120 − 10 = 110

Answer: 110 ways

Question 8: How many 6-digit numbers can be formed using the digits 1, 1, 2, 2, 2, 3?

Answer & Solution
**Formula:** n!/(p! × q! × r!)

Total = 6 digits: 1 appears 2 times, 2 appears 3 times, 3 appears 1 time Arrangements = 6!/(2! × 3! × 1!) = 720/(2 × 6 × 1) = 720/12 = 60

Answer: 60

Set 2: Probability (Questions 9–20)​

Question 9: A coin is tossed 5 times. Find the probability of getting exactly 3 heads.

Answer & Solution
**Formula:** P(r heads in n tosses) = C(n,r)/2^n

Total outcomes = 2⁵ = 32 Favourable = C(5,3) = 10 P = 10/32 = 5/16

Answer: 5/16

Question 10: Two dice are rolled. Find the probability that the sum is a multiple of 3.

Answer & Solution
**Formula:** Count favourable pairs and divide by 36.

Total outcomes = 36 Sum = 3: (1,2),(2,1) = 2 Sum = 6: (1,5),(2,4),(3,3),(4,2),(5,1) = 5 Sum = 9: (3,6),(4,5),(5,4),(6,3) = 4 Sum = 12: (6,6) = 1 Total favourable = 2 + 5 + 4 + 1 = 12 P = 12/36 = 1/3

Answer: 1/3

Question 11: From a pack of 52 cards, one card is drawn. Find the probability that it is a red face card.

Answer & Solution
**Formula:** P = Number of favourable / Total cards

Red cards = 26 (Hearts + Diamonds) Face cards (J,Q,K) in red suits = 3 × 2 = 6 P = 6/52 = 3/26

Answer: 3/26

Question 12: A bag contains 4 red and 6 black balls. Two balls are drawn at random. Find the probability that both are of the same colour.

Answer & Solution
**Formula:** P(both red) + P(both black)

Total = C(10,2) = 45 Both red = C(4,2) = 6 Both black = C(6,2) = 15 P = (6+15)/45 = 21/45 = 7/15

Answer: 7/15

Question 13: Three cards are drawn from a well-shuffled pack of 52 cards. Find the probability that all three are kings.

Answer & Solution
**Formula:** P = C(4,3)/C(52,3)

C(4,3) = 4 C(52,3) = 22100 P = 4/22100 = 1/5525

Answer: 1/5525

Question 14: Find the probability that a leap year has 53 Sundays.

Answer & Solution
**Formula:** A leap year has 366 days = 52 weeks + 2 days. These 2 days can be any of 7 pairs.

Extra days: (Sun,Mon), (Mon,Tue), (Tue,Wed), (Wed,Thu), (Thu,Fri), (Fri,Sat), (Sat,Sun) = 7 possibilities Favourable (contains Sunday): (Sun,Mon), (Sat,Sun) = 2 P = 2/7

Answer: 2/7

Question 15: A box contains 10 bulbs, 3 of which are defective. Two bulbs are drawn at random. Find the probability that at least one is defective.

Answer & Solution
**Formula:** P(at least 1) = 1 − P(none defective)

P(none defective) = C(7,2)/C(10,2) = 21/45 = 7/15 P(at least 1 defective) = 1 − 7/15 = 8/15

Answer: 8/15

Question 16: If P(A) = 0.6, P(B) = 0.4, and P(A∩B) = 0.2, find P(A∪B).

Answer & Solution
**Formula:** P(A∪B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A∩B)

P(A∪B) = 0.6 + 0.4 − 0.2 = 0.8

Answer: 0.8

Question 17: Two cards are drawn without replacement. Find the probability that the first is a king and the second is a queen.

Answer & Solution
**Formula:** P(king then queen) = P(king) × P(queen after king)

P(1st king) = 4/52 P(2nd queen | 1st king) = 4/51 P = (4/52) × (4/51) = 16/2652 = 4/663

Answer: 4/663

Question 18: A die is rolled twice. Find the probability that the sum is greater than 8.

Answer & Solution
**Formula:** Count favourable pairs with sum > 8.

Total = 36 Sum 9: (3,6),(4,5),(5,4),(6,3) = 4 Sum 10: (4,6),(5,5),(6,4) = 3 Sum 11: (5,6),(6,5) = 2 Sum 12: (6,6) = 1 Favourable = 4 + 3 + 2 + 1 = 10 P = 10/36 = 5/18

Answer: 5/18

Question 19: In a lottery, there are 10 tickets numbered 1 to 10. Two tickets are drawn. Find the probability that both numbers are even.

Answer & Solution
**Formula:** P = C(even numbers, 2)/C(10,2)

Even numbers from 1 to 10: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 = 5 C(5,2) = 10 C(10,2) = 45 P = 10/45 = 2/9

Answer: 2/9

Question 20: A speaks the truth in 75% of cases and B in 80% of cases. Find the probability that they contradict each other when narrating the same event.

Answer & Solution
**Formula:** P(contradiction) = P(A true, B false) + P(A false, B true)

P(A true) = 0.75, P(A false) = 0.25 P(B true) = 0.80, P(B false) = 0.20 P(contradiction) = (0.75 × 0.20) + (0.25 × 0.80) = 0.15 + 0.20 = 0.35

Answer: 0.35

Mermaid Diagram: Selection with Constraints​

mermaid flowchart TD A["Select r items from n"] --> B{"Constraint?"} B -->|"At least k of type A"| C["Sum of cases with k, k+1, ..."] B -->|"Exactly k of type A"| D["C(a,k) × C(b,n-k)"] B -->|"Specific item always included"| E["C(n-1, r-1)"] B -->|"Specific item never included"| F["C(n-1, r)"] C --> G["Answer"] D --> G E --> G F --> G

Summary​

  • Fundamental Counting Principle is the building block: multiply independent choices, add disjoint alternatives
  • Permutation involves arrangement (order matters): P(n,r) = n!/(n-r)!
  • Combination involves selection (order does not matter): C(n,r) = n!/(r!(n-r)!)
  • Probability = Favourable outcomes / Total outcomes
  • Coin tosses: 2^n total outcomes
  • Dice rolls: 6^n total outcomes; sum of 7 is most probable for 2 dice
  • Cards: 52 total; use combinations for multiple draws
  • Balls: Use hypergeometric formula for draws without replacement
  • For "at least" problems, use the complement: P(at least one) = 1 - P(none)
  • In IBPS SO, most probability problems involve 1-2 step calculations using combinations

Question 2​

A bag has 4 red and 6 blue balls. Two balls are drawn. Probability that both are blue?

Answer
C(6,2)/C(10,2) = 15/45 = 1/3

Question 3​

What is the probability of getting at least one head when tossing 3 coins?

Answer
P(at least one head) = 1 - P(no heads) = 1 - 1/8 = 7/8

Question 4​

A card is drawn from a pack of 52. Probability that it is a red king?

Answer
Red kings = 2 (King of Hearts, King of Diamonds) P = 2/52 = 1/26

Question 5​

In how many ways can 4 boys and 3 girls be arranged in a row such that all girls sit together?

Answer
Treat 3 girls as 1 unit: 5 items to arrange = 5! = 120 Girls internally: 3! = 6 Total = 120 × 6 = 720 ways
