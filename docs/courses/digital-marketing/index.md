# Complete Digital Marketing for Technical Founders

A full marketing course written for people who can build things but cannot yet
get paid for them: developers selling contract work, engineers launching a
product, and anyone who has shipped something nobody bought.

Every chapter follows the same shape: objectives, theory, worked examples with
real arithmetic, summary, exercises. The examples use Indian rupees and real
platform mechanics, because vague advice is the reason marketing courses fail.

---

## Who this is for

You can write the code. You cannot reliably answer three questions:

1. Who exactly is this for?
2. Why would they choose me over the alternative, which is often "do nothing"?
3. How do they find out I exist?

Marketing is the discipline that answers those three, in that order. It is not
advertising. Advertising is the last and most expensive step, and it is the one
beginners start with.

## The order matters

Most people learn marketing backwards. They start at advertising because it is
visible, then wonder why ₹50,000 of ads produced nothing.

The correct dependency chain is:

```
who  ->  why you  ->  what you sell  ->  how they hear about it  ->  how you measure it
 02        02              03                    05,06,07,08          09
```

Positioning errors cannot be fixed by better ads. A wrong offer cannot be fixed
by a better funnel. This course follows the chain, so each chapter uses the
output of the one before it.

---

## How to study this

**Do not read it end to end.** Each chapter ends with exercises that produce an
artifact. Produce the artifact before moving on, even badly. A bad landing page
you can measure beats a good one you are still designing.

**Time budget:** 90 minutes per chapter, twice a week, for six weeks. The
exercises are the course; the theory exists so the exercises are not guesses.

**Numbers rule.** Where this course gives a number, it is labelled either as a
*measured* figure from a named source, or as an *estimate* with its assumptions
shown. Treat unlabelled numbers anywhere else, including in other courses, as
decoration.

## Prerequisites

None technically, but two things help:

- something you have built that another person could use
- the willingness to talk to ten strangers about it

If you have the first and not the second, chapter 10 is the one that matters
most, and you should read it first.

## A warning that belongs at the front

Marketing is not a substitute for having something worth buying. If the offer is
weak, every chapter here will help you discover that faster, which is the
service you are paying for. Discovering it in week two is cheap. Discovering it
after six months of building is not.
