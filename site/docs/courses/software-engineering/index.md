# Software Engineering — A Comprehensive Textbook

## Course Overview

Software engineering is the systematic application of engineering principles to the design, development, operation, and maintenance of software systems. This textbook provides a rigorous treatment of the fundamental concepts, methodologies, and best practices that constitute the discipline of software engineering. The content is organised into sixteen chapters that progress from foundational process models through to advanced topics in security, formal methods, and metrics, culminating in a comprehensive capstone project.

The objective of this textbook is to equip the reader with both theoretical knowledge and practical competence. Each chapter presents core theory illustrated through concrete examples, case studies, and templates, followed by exercises designed to reinforce understanding and develop analytical skills. The material is appropriate for undergraduate and graduate courses in software engineering, as well as for practitioners seeking a structured reference.

## Chapter Summary

### Chapter 1 — Software Process Models

This chapter introduces the concept of a software process and examines the major process models that have shaped the field. Topics include the Waterfall model, V-model, Incremental and Iterative development, the Spiral model, the Unified Process, and the Agile Manifesto with its principal embodiments in Extreme Programming and Scrum. A comparative analysis of process models is provided to guide selection based on project characteristics.

### Chapter 2 — Requirements Engineering

Requirements engineering is the discipline of establishing what a software system must do. This chapter covers the classification of requirements into functional, non-functional, and domain types; the feasibility study; elicitation techniques including interviews, surveys, observations, and workshops; specification using IEEE 830, user stories, and use cases; requirements validation; and requirements management encompassing traceability, prioritisation, and change control.

### Chapter 3 — System Modelling

System modelling produces abstract representations of a system from multiple perspectives. This chapter presents the Unified Modeling Language (UML) diagram types: use case, class, sequence, activity, state machine, component, and deployment diagrams. It also covers the distinction between UML and SysML, data flow diagrams, entity-relationship diagrams, model-driven engineering, and the Object Constraint Language (OCL).

### Chapter 4 — Architectural Design

Architectural design defines the overall structure of a software system. This chapter addresses architectural decisions, the fundamental architectural patterns including layered, MVC, repository, client-server, pipe-and-filter, microservices, event-driven, and broker patterns, and common application architectures for transaction processing systems, information systems, and language processing systems.

### Chapter 5 — Design and Implementation

This chapter bridges high-level design and concrete implementation. It covers design principles including SOLID, DRY, KISS, YAGNI, separation of concerns, and information hiding; design heuristics; the concepts of coupling and cohesion; design review techniques; and implementation concerns including software reuse, configuration management, build tools, and integrated development environments.

### Chapter 6 — Software Testing

Software testing is the primary means of verification and validation. This chapter covers the fundamental concepts of V&V, the levels of testing from unit through integration, system, and acceptance; testing types including functional, structural, regression, smoke, performance, security, and usability testing; white-box techniques such as statement, branch, path, and condition coverage; black-box techniques including equivalence partitioning, boundary value analysis, decision tables, and state transition testing; test automation; and test-driven and behaviour-driven development.

### Chapter 7 — Software Evolution

Software systems must evolve to remain useful. This chapter addresses the categories of software maintenance — corrective, adaptive, perfective, and preventive; reverse engineering and refactoring; the challenges of legacy systems; regression testing in the context of evolution; and Lehman's laws of software evolution dynamics.

### Chapter 8 — Project Management

Software project management involves planning, estimation, scheduling, risk management, and team organisation. This chapter covers estimation techniques including function points and the COCOMO family of models; scheduling with PERT, Gantt charts, and CPM; risk management processes; and team organisation patterns such as the chief programmer team, egoless programming, and democratic teams.

### Chapter 9 — Quality Management

Software quality management ensures that processes and products meet specified standards. This chapter examines the ISO 9001 quality standard, the Capability Maturity Model Integration (CMMI) from levels 1 through 5, Six Sigma and the DMAIC cycle, quality metrics including defect density and mean time between failures, review techniques such as walkthroughs, inspections, and technical reviews, and continuous process improvement.

### Chapter 10 — Configuration Management

Configuration management controls the evolution of software artefacts. This chapter covers version control with Git, change management, build management, release management, continuous integration and continuous delivery pipelines, DevOps practices, and semantic versioning.

### Chapter 11 — Agile Methodologies

This chapter provides an in-depth examination of agile methods. Topics include the Scrum framework with its roles, events, and artefacts; the Kanban method with work-in-progress limits, lead time, and cycle time; scaled agile frameworks including SAFe and LeSS; agile estimation techniques such as planning poker, story points, and velocity; and agile testing practices.

### Chapter 12 — DevOps and CI/CD

DevOps represents the convergence of development and operations. This chapter covers DevOps culture, continuous integration and continuous delivery pipelines implemented with Jenkins, GitLab CI, and GitHub Actions, infrastructure as code using Terraform and Ansible, containerisation with Docker and Kubernetes, monitoring and logging practices, and deployment strategies including blue-green, canary, and rolling deployments.

### Chapter 13 — Security Engineering

Security must be engineered into software from the outset. This chapter covers the secure development lifecycle, threat modelling with STRIDE and attack trees, security design patterns, the OWASP Top 10 vulnerabilities, input validation, authentication and authorisation mechanisms, cryptography in software, and penetration testing methodology.

### Chapter 14 — Formal Methods

Formal methods apply mathematical techniques to the specification, development, and verification of software. This chapter covers formal specification languages including Z, VDM, and the B-Method; formal verification through model checking and theorem proving; correctness proofs and Hoare logic; abstraction and refinement; and the application of formal methods in safety-critical systems.

### Chapter 15 — Software Metrics

Measurement is essential for control and improvement. This chapter presents product metrics including lines of code, McCabe's cyclomatic complexity, Halstead's software science metrics, function points, and object-oriented metrics; process metrics including defect removal efficiency and process maturity; and project metrics including earned value analysis and burn-down charts.

### Chapter 16 — Capstone Project

The capstone chapter integrates all preceding material into a comprehensive project exercise. It guides the reader through planning a software project, producing a software requirements specification document, performing architectural design using the Attribute-Driven Design method, executing an implementation sprint, designing a testing strategy, creating a quality assurance plan, and formulating a deployment plan.

## How to Use This Textbook

Each chapter follows a consistent structure: learning objectives, theoretical exposition with diagrams described in prose, worked examples and case studies, a summary of key points, and a graded set of exercises comprising review questions, application problems, and a challenge problem. Readers are encouraged to attempt all exercises and to complete the capstone project as the integrative assessment.

## Recommended Prerequisites

Readers should have basic programming competence in at least one high-level language, familiarity with fundamental data structures and algorithms, and an understanding of the software development lifecycle as practised in introductory computing courses.
