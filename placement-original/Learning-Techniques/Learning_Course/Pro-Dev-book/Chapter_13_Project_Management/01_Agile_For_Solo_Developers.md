# 📋 Agile For Solo Developers

### 🧩 The Kanban Mindset

Many developers believe that "Project Management" is something only Product Managers or Scrum Masters do at large corporations. They think that because they are building a SaaS product solo, or working on a small freelance project, they can just "keep everything in their head."

This is a critical mistake. If you do not externalize your tasks, your brain must constantly juggle *what* to do next while simultaneously trying to remember *how* to do it. This causes severe cognitive fatigue.

Professional developers, regardless of team size, use **Kanban**.

---

### 🧱 The 3-Column System

You do not need an enterprise Jira setup with 15 different columns, mandatory story points, and sprint retrospectives. You need a simple Trello, GitHub Project, or Notion board with precisely three columns.

#### 1. 🗄️ The Backlog (The Brain Dump)
*   **The Rule:** If a thought enters your mind about the project, it goes here immediately.
*   **The Why:** "Oh, I need to add an index to the `leads` table." If you keep that in your head, you will forget it, or you will interrupt your current task to do it immediately (destroying your focus). Write it in the Backlog and forget it.

#### 2. 🏗️ In Progress (The WIP Limit)
*   **The Rule:** You are only allowed to have **ONE** task in this column at any given time.
*   **The Why:** "WIP" (Work In Progress) limits are the secret to rapid delivery. If you have 5 tasks In Progress, you are context-switching constantly. You will end the week with 5 features that are 80% done, which equals 0 value delivered. 
*   *Force yourself to move a task to 'Done' before dragging the next one in.*

#### 3. ✅ Done (The Dopamine Hit)
*   **The Rule:** A task only enters this column if it is fully tested and merged into the main branch. 
*   **The Why:** Seeing a massive "Done" column provides psychological momentum. It is proof of velocity.

---

### 🗓️ The Weekly Sprint

As a solo developer, you do not need 2-week agile sprints. You need a **Weekly Planning Session**.

Every Monday morning before you write a single line of code, spend 15 minutes reviewing your Backlog.
1. Drag the 3 to 5 most important tasks to the top.
2. Formally commit: *"I will finish these 5 tasks by Friday."*
3. Ignore the rest of the backlog entirely for the duration of the week.

**Summary:** The brain is for processing logic, not storing to-do lists. Offload your project management to a strict Kanban board, enforce a WIP limit of 1, and watch your velocity skyrocket.
