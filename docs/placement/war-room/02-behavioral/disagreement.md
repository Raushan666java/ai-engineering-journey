# Tell Me About a Time You Disagreed With a Teammate or Manager

> **Status:** ○ Planned · Edit this line to ◐ Practicing · ● Drilled

The interviewer is checking three things at once: **do you have technical opinions, can you defend them with evidence, and can you do it without burning the relationship?**

The structure: respectful framing → evidence-based debate → outcome (one of three: convinced them, were convinced, compromised).

The tone is everything. **Collaborative, not combative.**

---

## Inhale.

Disagreement is not conflict. It's debate.

Read the template. Pick which outcome best matches your real story.

## The Script — 90 seconds (STAR template)

> **Situation (15s)** — "In my [Project Name / Internship], the team was deciding on [specific technical choice — which DB to use / how to structure the API / whether to use Tailwind or custom CSS / which Laravel auth package]. The lead/senior had proposed [their choice]. I disagreed — I thought [your alternative]."
>
> **Task (10s)** — "I had to decide whether to (a) go along with it, (b) raise the disagreement now. I decided to raise it — but I wanted to do it with evidence, not just opinion."
>
> **Action — how I raised it (45s)** — "Three things. First, I asked clarifying questions in standup: *'just to make sure I understand — the main reason for [their choice] is [reason]? Are there any other constraints?'* This forced me to confirm I wasn't disagreeing with a strawman. Second, I built a small spike — about 2 hours of prototyping — comparing their approach to mine, focused on [specific metric — query count / lines of code / build size / setup time]. Third, I shared the spike in the next standup as: *'I built a quick comparison so we can decide based on data, not opinion. Here's what I found...'*. I gave my recommendation but framed it as: *'Happy to go either way — wanted to bring the data so we choose with full info.'*"
>
> **Result (20s) — pick ONE outcome type** —
>
> *(A) Convinced them:* "After seeing the data, the team agreed my approach was better for our case — we went with [your choice]. We saved [quantified benefit — N hours of dev time / N queries per page]. The lead specifically said they appreciated that I brought data instead of arguing."
>
> *(B) Were convinced:* "After seeing their reasoning + the constraints I hadn't fully understood (they walked me through their experience with [their choice] in production), I agreed their approach was better. We shipped with their choice, and I learned [specific lesson — about the trade-off I'd missed]. The data spike still helped because it grounded the discussion."
>
> *(C) Compromised:* "We landed on a hybrid — we used [their choice] for the main flow because of [their valid constraint] and [your choice] for the [specific module] where my approach made more sense. Both of us got the parts we cared about."

Ninety seconds. Respectful framing → evidence-based action → mature outcome.

## The Anatomy — why this wins

The interviewer is **terrified of two candidate types**:

1. **The pushover** — agrees with everything, has no opinions, ships bad code because they couldn't say no.
2. **The bulldozer** — argues every decision, burns the team's morale, "wins" disagreements but loses the team.

You're going to position yourself as **neither**. You're the third type: **someone who disagrees with evidence and keeps the team's trust.**

The "asked clarifying questions first" line is the signal of seniority. You confirmed you weren't disagreeing with a misunderstanding before you escalated.

The "built a small spike" is the engineer move. Most candidates say "I told them I thought X was better." You're going to say "I built a comparison so we could decide based on data." That's a level above.

The "happy to go either way — wanted to bring the data" line is the **collaborative-not-combative** signal. You showed it was about the right answer, not about you being right.

---

## All three outcomes are valid — pick the most honest one

Don't pretend you convinced a senior just because it sounds better. **Being convinced (after evidence + their experience) is a senior signal too** — it shows you're not ego-attached. Compromise is the most realistic outcome and rarely sounds weak when framed well.

| Outcome | When to use it | Strength signal |
|---|---|---|
| Convinced them | When your story had clear data + a junior-to-senior context | Shows you can push back productively |
| Were convinced | When you went in with one view and learned something | Shows you're not ego-attached, you update on evidence |
| Compromised | When both sides had valid points | Shows mature judgment, real-world collaboration |

The interviewer is testing the *process* (respectful framing + evidence), not the outcome. **All three outcomes pass.**


If you've never disagreed with a teammate or manager — that's a problem. It usually means either (a) you've only done solo projects, or (b) you're a pushover. Fix (a) by getting into group projects. Fix (b) by practicing low-stakes pushback in your next project.

If your real disagreement was about something non-technical (process, scope, scheduling) — that works too. Same structure, just swap "technical choice" for the process question.

## The 5-day practice drill

- [ ] **Day 1 — List 3 real disagreements** from projects / internships. Pick the one with the clearest evidence-based resolution.
- [ ] **Day 2 — Write the STAR script.** Focus on the *process* (clarifying questions + spike + framing), not who won.
- [ ] **Day 3 — Practice the framing line.** *"Happy to go either way — wanted to bring the data so we choose with full info."* Memorize it word for word.
- [ ] **Day 4 — Record yourself.** Watch playback. Does the tone feel collaborative or combative? If combative, slow down + soften the verbs (proposed → suggested, argued → discussed).
- [ ] **Day 5 — Mock with a friend.** Ask: *"Would you want this person on your team?"* You want a yes. Tone calibrates more than content.

## Variants

=== "60s — Quick screen"

    > "In [project], we disagreed on [technical choice]. I built a small comparison spike and shared it in standup, framing it as 'let's decide with data, not opinion.' We ended up [outcome]. The lead appreciated the evidence-based approach."

    Use when: phone screen, time-pressed behavioral round.

=== "90s — Default"

    See main script above.

=== "2min — Hiring manager / panel"

    Use the 90s script. After the result, pause. If they want more, continue:

    > "What I learned from that disagreement: the right pushback isn't about winning, it's about giving the team better information to decide with. I've since used the 'small spike + share in standup' pattern multiple times — each time, the team makes a better decision, and even if my view doesn't win, the relationship stays strong because I came with data."

    Use when: hiring manager wants to assess collaboration style, panel asks about conflict.

## Common traps

| Trap | Why it kills | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Says "I just kept arguing until they agreed" | Bulldozer signal, instant downgrade | Use evidence + framing line, not persistence |
| Story shows you escalated to a manager over a small thing | Petty signal | Pick a stakes-appropriate story |
| You "won" with no data — just opinion | Sounds arrogant, can't be relied on | Always bring evidence (a spike, a benchmark, a doc) |
| Says "I was right and they were wrong" | Burns the relationship in the story | Mature framing: "we landed on the better answer" |
| No outcome named | Sounds incomplete | Pick A, B, or C clearly — all are valid |
| Disagreement was personal, not technical | Off-topic, this isn't the conflict question | Use the [Conflict](conflict.md) drill for interpersonal stories |
| Says you "couldn't say anything" because they were senior | Pushover signal, equally bad as bulldozer | Reframe with: "I raised it respectfully, with evidence" |

## Recovery — when you blank

If you forget the spike / evidence part:

> *"The way I raised it — I made sure to come with something tangible. I built a quick prototype / wrote up a comparison doc / brought a code example, so the discussion wasn't opinion vs opinion."*

Generic but works. The principle (evidence > opinion) is what matters.

If you forget how it resolved:

> *"Honestly, the outcome was less important than the way we got there. We landed on a decision we both believed in — I can't remember the exact split, but I know we shipped it and neither of us walked away frustrated. That's the win in a disagreement."*

Senior interviewers respect "process over outcome" as long as it's not a dodge for not having a story.
