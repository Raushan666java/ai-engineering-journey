# Why Should We Hire You?

> **Status:** ○ Planned · Edit this line to ◐ Practicing · ● Drilled

This is the closer. It usually comes in the last 10 minutes, after the technicals. The interviewer is checking one thing: **can you summarize your own value in 45 seconds without sounding arrogant or desperate?**

Most candidates fumble this because they treat it like a pitch. It's not a pitch. It's a **fit statement.**

---

## Inhale.

This is the last question before they decide. Don't rush.

Read the script. Then read it again.

---

## The Script — 45 seconds

> **Unique combination (10s)** — "Sir, what I bring is a combination most freshers don't — I'm strong in Laravel and PHP for backend, and I'm equally comfortable with AI engineering — OpenAI APIs, RAG pipelines, prompt engineering."
>
> **Proof point with a metric (10s)** — "I've shipped a Laravel + OpenAI tool that's currently used by 500+ people. That tells you I can take an idea, ship it to production, and get real users."
>
> **Their need + my strength (15s)** — "Your JD mentions [specific need — building AI features into existing PHP/Laravel systems / scaling APIs / shipping fast]. That's exactly the wedge I've been working on. I'm not coming in to learn — I'm coming in to contribute from week one in [their stack area]."
>
> **Close (10s)** — "I'm genuinely excited about this role. If you have any hesitation, I'd rather you tell me now so I can address it — but I think we'd be a good fit."

Forty-five seconds. Unique combination → proof → fit → confident close with an *ask*.

---

## The Anatomy — why this wins

Most candidates list traits ("hardworking, quick learner, team player"). The interviewer's brain filters out adjectives. You're going to lead with a **combination** — two specific skills that, together, are uncommon. Laravel + AI is uncommon. Backend + frontend depth is uncommon. SQL + system design is uncommon. Pick yours.

The proof point is **one metric**. Not three projects. One. *"500+ users"* sticks; *"various projects in PHP, JavaScript, Python, and AWS"* doesn't.

The fit section is the **mirror move** — you mirror back their JD in your words. They wrote "we need someone who can ship AI features on Laravel." You say back: *"That's exactly the wedge I've been working on."* It registers as alignment.

The close is the kill move. Most candidates end with *"thank you for considering me."* You're going to end with *"if you have any hesitation, tell me now."* That's confidence without arrogance. It also gives them a chance to surface their concern — and you to fix it before they leave the room.

## Fill in your blanks

```text
[Your unique combination — two skills that together are rare]:
[Proof point with one metric — users, projects shipped, scale, speed]:
[Exact phrase from their JD — copy 5–10 words from the listing]:
[Stack area where you'd contribute from week one]:
```

The two-skill combination is the most important field. Brainstorm yours:

- Laravel + OpenAI / RAG
- PHP backend + React frontend (full-stack)
- ML algorithms + production deployment (most data science grads can't deploy)
- SQL deep + Python — for data-heavy roles
- DSA + product taste — for product-engineer roles at startups
- Mobile (Flutter/React Native) + Backend — rare combo at most freshers

Pick the one that matches **their JD**. Not the one you're most proud of.

## The 5-day practice drill

- [ ] **Day 1 — List 3 unique combinations you could honestly claim.** Pick one. Memorize it as one sentence.
- [ ] **Day 2 — Identify your one strongest proof point.** A number — users, scale, time saved. Memorize it.
- [ ] **Day 3 — Open 3 JDs you're targeting. Highlight 5 words from each.** These are your mirror phrases.
- [ ] **Day 4 — Deliver the 45s script on camera 3 times.** Watch playback. The close ("address any hesitation") should sound confident, not pleading.
- [ ] **Day 5 — Mock with a friend.** Ask them to rate: did you sound *(a) arrogant, (b) desperate, (c) confident*? You want (c). Adjust delivery, not content.

## Variants

=== "Service-based (Infosys / TCS / Wipro / Cognizant)"

    Service-based companies want **adaptability and trainability** more than deep specialization. Adjust the script:

    > "Sir, my combination is full-stack range — I'm comfortable in PHP/Laravel for backend, React for frontend, and I've worked with Python for AI integrations. I've shipped 3 projects end-to-end, which means I can pick up the stack you put me on. I also have a fast onramp — I learned [stack X] in [N weeks] for [project]. For a service role where projects rotate, that's the trait that matters most."

    Don't oversell AI here. Service-based interviewers value range over depth.

=== "Product-based (Razorpay / Zerodha / Atlan / Postman)"

    Product companies want **depth in one stack and product taste.** Adjust:

    > "I'm strong in Laravel and PHP backend, and I've built one project end-to-end that solves a real user problem — [Project Name], used by [N] people. That tells you two things: I can go deep in a stack, and I care about whether users actually use what I ship. Your team is building [their product]. I want to contribute as someone who already thinks about users, not just features."

    Lead with depth + product instinct. Avoid generalist language.

=== "Startup (10–100 engineers)"

    Startups want **shipping speed and ownership.** Adjust:

    > "I ship. Three projects deployed, one with paying users, one open-source repo with stars. I don't wait for specs — I build, get feedback, iterate. At a startup, you need someone who'll own a feature from idea to deploy in a week, not wait for handoffs. That's how I've been operating in college. I'd rather do that on real product than on college projects."

    Mention specific shipping cadence. Drop GitHub link.

=== "FAANG / Big Tech"

    Big tech wants **fundamentals + scale thinking.** Adjust:

    > "I bring DSA fundamentals plus production engineering instinct. I've solved [N] LeetCode problems including [hard] ones, but more importantly I've shipped systems that handled [scale moment — 2000 concurrent users, 100k records, etc]. That combination — fundamentals plus shipping — is what your team needs in an entry engineer, especially for [team they're hiring for]."

    Mention scale numbers + system design instinct, not just project count.

## Common traps

| Trap | Why it kills | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Lists traits ("hardworking, quick learner") | Adjectives don't differentiate, every candidate says them | Lead with a skill combination, not personality traits |
| Says *"I'll work hard / give 100%"* | Effort isn't outcome — they want shipped work | Replace with a metric: "I shipped X to N users" |
| Names 5 skills | Sounds shallow, can't go deep on any | Two skills that together are uncommon |
| Doesn't mirror the JD | Generic answer = generic candidate | Lift 5 words from their JD, drop them into the script |
| Begs ("please give me a chance") | Desperate energy, instant downgrade | Close with confidence — "address any hesitation" |
| Says *"because I really want this job"* | Wanting it isn't a qualification | Replace with one concrete fit reason |
| Compares yourself to other candidates | You don't know them, sounds arrogant | Talk about your fit to the role, not your superiority |

## Recovery — when you freeze

This question hits late in the round. You're tired. Your brain blanks.

The line is:

> *"Sir, let me give you the honest version instead of the polished one — what you need is someone who can ship in [their stack] from week one, and someone who's curious enough to figure out the rest. That's me — here's why."*

Then pivot to your one strongest proof point. If you can name one shipped project with a number, you've answered the question.

If you blank on the proof point itself:

> *"The project I'd point you to is [Project Name] — I can walk you through how I built it in 90 seconds if that'd help."*

You converted a blank into a project pitch. Senior interviewers respect that.
