Chapter 7 — Paid Ads and Unit Economics

Learning Objectives​

After this chapter you will be able to:

  • State the three preconditions for advertising profitably and check whether you meet them
  • Compute CAC, LTV, LTV:CAC ratio, payback period and ROAS from real inputs
  • Distinguish ROAS from ROI and explain why the first flatters a business
  • Design a creative test that changes one variable and reaches a decision
  • Choose between search ads and social ads based on intent rather than preference
  • Set a daily budget from a target CAC instead of from what feels affordable

Theory​

7.1 Advertising is a multiplier, not a starting point​

Paid acquisition multiplies an offer that already converts. It does not create conversion. Three preconditions must hold before the first rupee is spent:

preconditionhow to checkif it fails
the offer converts organicallyat least a few sales from referrals, outbound or contentadvertising buys traffic for a page that does not work
CAC is knowableyou can trace one sale back to its sourceyou cannot tell whether a campaign worked
LTV exceeds CAC with margina customer is worth at least 3x what they costevery sale loses money, faster at scale

The failure mode is predictable and common: spending ₹50,000 to discover that a landing page converts at 0.2%, when ten manual conversations would have revealed the same thing for free.

The correct sequence: prove the offer with a slow channel, then multiply it with a paid one.

7.2 The unit economics vocabulary​

termdefinitionformula
CACcost to acquire one customertotal acquisition spend / customers acquired
LTVgross profit from one customer over their lifetimeaverage revenue per period x gross margin x periods retained
LTV:CACefficiency of acquisitionLTV / CAC
paybackmonths to recover CACCAC / monthly gross profit per customer
ROASrevenue per unit of ad spendrevenue / ad spend
ROIprofit per unit of spend(revenue − cost) / cost

Two errors are worth naming because they are everywhere:

ROAS is not profit. A ROAS of 3 with a 30% gross margin means revenue is three times spend and profit is 0.9 times spend minus overheads. Businesses routinely celebrate a ROAS that is losing money.

LTV is not revenue. If a customer pays ₹2,000 a month but delivery costs ₹1,200, the LTV is built on ₹800, not ₹2,000. Using revenue inflates LTV by whatever your margin is, and makes unprofitable acquisition look healthy.

A planning rule of thumb, judgement rather than a law: LTV:CAC of at least 3:1 and payback under 12 months for a business with recurring revenue, under 3 months for a one-off service. Below those, the constraint is retention or delivery cost, not advertising.

7.3 Search and social are different instruments​

dimensionsearch adssocial ads
intentexisting, explicitnone, created by the ad
cost per clickhigherlower
conversion ratehigherlower
creative burdenlow (match the query)high (stop the scroll)
best forcommercial and transactional intentawareness, retargeting, impulse

For a technical seller, search is almost always the better first test, because the intent is already present. Someone typing "hire fastapi developer" has declared the need. Someone scrolling a feed has not.

Social becomes useful later for two specific jobs: retargeting people who already visited, and distributing content that builds familiarity over months. Using it as a cold acquisition channel requires creative skill that takes longer to learn than the offer takes to sell directly.

7.4 Message match​

The single largest cause of wasted ad spend is a mismatch between the ad and the page it opens. If the ad says "WhatsApp bot for gyms" and the page says "Digital solutions for growing businesses", the visitor concludes they clicked the wrong thing and leaves.

The rule: the landing page headline must contain the ad's promise, nearly verbatim, in the first screen. Not a paraphrase, the same words. This is boring and it works.

7.5 Testing without fooling yourself​

An ad test is an experiment with three requirements:

  1. One variable changes. Two headlines, same image, same audience, same page.
  2. The sample is large enough. Deciding after 12 clicks is reading noise.
  3. The decision is written before the test. "If A beats B by 20% on conversion rate, A wins."

Minimum detectable effect matters as much as significance. With small budgets you can only detect large differences, so test big swings: a completely different promise, not a different shade of button.

monthly visitors per variantreliably detectable difference
200only very large, 50%+
1,000large, 25–30%
5,000moderate, 10–15%
20,000small, 5%

These are approximations for a conversion rate near 5%; the exact number depends on the baseline rate. The practical message: at low volume, stop optimising and change the offer.

7.6 Setting a budget from a target CAC​

Work backwards. If a customer is worth ₹36,000 of gross profit and the target payback is three months, the maximum CAC is ₹36,000 divided by the number of customers per payback window, which for a one-off service is simply ₹36,000. Spend no more than a third of that per experiment until the funnel is measured.

Monthly budget  = target CAC x expected customers
                = 12,000 x 3 = 36,000
Daily budget    = monthly / 30 = 1,200
Test duration   = enough days to accumulate 100+ clicks per variant

Starting at ₹500–1,000 a day in India is enough to learn from a narrow search campaign. Below that, data accumulates too slowly to distinguish signal from noise.

7.7 What advertising cannot fix​

symptomadvertising cannot fix it because
low conversion on the pagemore traffic produces proportionally more non-buyers
high refundsthe promise and the delivery disagree
no repeat purchaseretention is a product problem
negative marginscale multiplies the loss

The diagnostic sentence: if you would not spend your own money to buy your own offer at full price, advertising will not find someone who will.

Examples​

Example 1: Unit economics for a service business​

"""Compute acquisition economics and state plainly whether they work.

Every input is explicit. The output is a judgement, not a scoreboard.
"""
from dataclasses import dataclass


@dataclass
class Unit:
    price: int                 # what the customer pays, one engagement
    delivery_cost: int         # your time and tools, in rupees
    months_retained: float     # for recurring elements
    monthly_fee: int = 0
    ad_spend: int = 0
    customers: int = 1

    @property
    def gross_profit(self) -> float:
        one_off = self.price - self.delivery_cost
        recurring = (self.monthly_fee * 0.6) * self.months_retained  # 40% delivery cost
        return one_off + recurring

    @property
    def cac(self) -> float:
        return self.ad_spend / self.customers if self.customers else float("inf")

    def report(self) -> dict:
        gp, cac = self.gross_profit, self.cac
        return {
            "gross_profit": round(gp),
            "cac": round(cac),
            "ltv_cac": round(gp / cac, 2) if cac else None,
            "payback_months": round(cac / (self.monthly_fee * 0.6), 1) if self.monthly_fee else 0,
            "roas": round((self.price + self.monthly_fee * self.months_retained) / self.ad_spend, 2),
            "verdict": "healthy" if cac and gp / cac >= 3 else "not viable at this CAC",
        }


gym_bot = Unit(price=25_000, delivery_cost=9_000, monthly_fee=2_000,
               months_retained=12, ad_spend=36_000, customers=3)
print(gym_bot.report())

Expected output:

{'gross_profit': 30400, 'cac': 12000, 'ltv_cac': 2.53, 'payback_months': 10.0,
 'roas': 1.36, 'verdict': 'not viable at this CAC'}

Note the ROAS: 1.36 looks acceptable to someone who does not know the delivery cost, and the verdict contradicts it. That contradiction is the reason this chapter exists. The business is not viable at a CAC of 12,000 with these margins, and no creative optimisation fixes a 2.53 ratio by much.

The legitimate responses, in order of leverage:

  • raise months_retained, by fixing retention
  • raise monthly_fee, by adding a service the client already wants
  • lower delivery_cost, by templating the build
  • lower CAC, by improving the funnel — the smallest lever of the four

Example 2: How many visitors a test needs​

"""Minimum sample size for a two-variant conversion test.

Uses the standard normal approximation. Good enough for planning; it assumes
independent visitors and a stable conversion rate, which real traffic only
approximately satisfies.
"""
import math


def z_for(confidence: float) -> float:
    # two-sided critical values, the three that matter in practice
    return {0.90: 1.645, 0.95: 1.96, 0.99: 2.576}[confidence]


def sample_per_variant(baseline: float, relative_lift: float,
                       confidence: float = 0.95, power: float = 0.80) -> int:
    z_alpha = z_for(confidence)
    z_beta = {0.80: 0.84, 0.90: 1.28}[power]
    p1 = baseline
    p2 = baseline * (1 + relative_lift)
    p_bar = (p1 + p2) / 2
    numerator = (z_alpha * math.sqrt(2 * p_bar * (1 - p_bar))
                 + z_beta * math.sqrt(p1 * (1 - p1) + p2 * (1 - p2))) ** 2
    return math.ceil(numerator / (p2 - p1) ** 2)


for lift in (0.50, 0.25, 0.10, 0.05):
    n = sample_per_variant(0.05, lift)
    print(f"detect a {lift:>4.0%} relative lift -> {n:>6,} visitors per variant")

Expected output:

detect a  50% relative lift ->   1,469 visitors per variant
detect a  25% relative lift ->   5,327 visitors per variant
detect a  10% relative lift ->  31,199 visitors per variant
detect a   5% relative lift -> 121,986 visitors per variant

The numbers are the lesson. At 500 visitors a month, a 5% improvement is invisible forever. The only tests available to a small site are large ones: a different promise, a different audience, a different price. Optimising button colours at this volume is a hobby dressed as rigour.

Summary​

  • Advertising multiplies a working offer and cannot create one; three preconditions must hold first.
  • ROAS measures revenue per rupee of spend and says nothing about profit; ROI does.
  • LTV must be built from gross profit, not revenue, or acquisition looks healthier than it is.
  • LTV:CAC of 3:1 and payback under 3 months for one-off services are planning judgements, not laws.
  • Search captures existing intent and is the better first test; social creates intent and needs creative skill.
  • Message match between ad and landing page prevents the largest single waste of spend.
  • At low traffic volume only large changes are detectable, so test offers, not colours.

Exercises​

Review Questions​

  1. State the three preconditions for profitable advertising and what happens when each fails.
  2. Why can a business with a ROAS of 3 still lose money on every sale?
  3. What is the difference between LTV and gross profit per customer, and which should CAC be compared to?
  4. Why is search usually a better first paid test than social for a technical service?
  5. At 1,000 monthly visitors, what size of improvement can you reliably detect?

Application Problems​

  1. Compute CAC, gross profit, LTV:CAC, payback and ROAS for a plausible offer of yours. State the verdict honestly.
  2. If the verdict is "not viable", rank the four levers by expected impact for your case and justify the ranking.
  3. Run the sample-size function for your real traffic level. State the smallest lift you could ever detect and what that means for your testing plan.

Challenge Problem​

A client wants to spend ₹1,00,000 on ads in 30 days for a ₹15,000 service with a 40% gross margin and no organic sales yet. Write the exact response you would give, including what you would do with the budget instead, and the evidence you would need before accepting the spend.
