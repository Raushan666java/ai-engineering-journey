# Interview War Room

Not theory. Not lectures. Scripts you memorize, drills you repeat, recovery lines for when you blank.

This is the room you walk into **before** walking into the interview.

---

## How this works

Every page is one **drill** — one focused interview moment.

Each drill gives you five things:

1. **The Script** — exact words to memorize
2. **The Anatomy** — why those words win
3. **The Practice Plan** — 5 days of repetition
4. **The Recovery** — what to say when you blank
5. **The Variants** — 30-second / 90-second / 5-minute versions

No filler. No "complete course." Just what you actually say when the recruiter asks.

## Status legend

Track each drill's status as you work through it. Three states only — no half-marks.

- ○ **Planned** — not started
- ◐ **Practicing** — script read, working on delivery
- ● **Drilled** — can deliver under pressure, eyes closed

A drill is only **Drilled** when you can say it cold, with the timer running, into a phone recording, without filler words. Anything less is still **Practicing**.

## The one rule

> Read every script **aloud**. Out loud. Not in your head.
>
> Your interview happens out loud. Practice has to happen out loud too.

This is the rule that separates students who *think* they're prepared from students who actually crack the round. Silent reading is comfort. Aloud rehearsal is the work.

---

## What this room is *not*

This is **not** a "complete CS course." For theory and concepts, use the [12 placement modules](../index.md) — they are the textbook. This room is the rehearsal stage.

Theory tells you *what* an N+1 query is. The Laravel drill teaches you the exact 4-sentence answer that wins the Laravel round.

Use both. Theory first, drills second. Drills are what get you the offer.
