# Four Hours Before

> **Status:** ○ Planned · Edit this line to ◐ Practicing · ● Drilled

Interview is at 11 AM. It's 7 AM. The next four hours decide whether you walk in sharp or walk in foggy. Most candidates blow these four hours scrolling, skipping breakfast, and cramming new material.

This drill is the morning protocol. Wake → hydrate → move → eat → wash → dress → light review → leave.

---

## Inhale.

You did the prep. Today you execute. The work now is not study — it's *body and mood*.

Athletes warm up before a game. You warm up before the interview.

---

## The Script — what you tell yourself

> *"The material is in my head. Right now I take care of my body and my breathing. By the time I walk in, I am calm, alert, and rehearsed."*

That's the frame. Body first. Brain follows.

---

## The Anatomy — why the morning matters more than the prep

A candidate who slept well, hydrated, ate breakfast, and arrived 20 minutes early performs at 100%.

A candidate who slept 4 hours, skipped breakfast, drank 3 coffees, and rushed in 5 minutes late performs at 60%. Same prep, 40% worse outcome.

The interview measures clarity under pressure. Clarity is downstream of your body. Take care of the body, the brain will perform.

---

## The 4-Hour Morning Calendar

Assume interview at 11 AM. Adjust the absolute times if yours is different.

### 7:00 AM — Wake *(30 min)*

- [ ] **Alarm off.** Do not snooze. Snoozing wrecks the next 4 hours.
- [ ] **Drink 500ml water.** Bottle by the bed. Done before brushing teeth.
- [ ] **Open the curtains.** Daylight on your face. Signals your circadian system.
- [ ] **No phone for the first 20 minutes.** No news, no social, no email. Especially no checking the interview email "one more time."
- [ ] **Brush teeth, splash face with cold water.**

### 7:30 AM — Move *(15 min)*

- [ ] **Light exercise — pick one:**
    - 15-minute walk outside (best option).
    - 5 sun salutations + 5 min stretching.
    - 20 push-ups + 30 squats + 60s plank.
- [ ] Goal: heart rate up, blood moving, oxygen to brain.
- [ ] **Do not work out hard.** Soreness or fatigue will hurt you in 3 hours.

### 7:45 AM — Shower + Dress *(30 min)*

- [ ] **Hot shower** (or warm, not freezing). Wakes you up, relaxes shoulders.
- [ ] **Dress in the clothes you laid out last night.** Don't second-guess the outfit now.
- [ ] **Check yourself in the mirror once.** Hair, collar, fly. Done. Don't keep checking.

### 8:15 AM — Breakfast *(30 min)*

- [ ] **Eat something you've eaten before.** Today is not the day to try a new restaurant or new food.
- [ ] **Protein + slow carbs** — eggs + toast, paratha + curd, oats + nuts, idli + sambar. Whatever you usually eat that doesn't crash your blood sugar.
- [ ] **Avoid:** heavy fried food (sluggish), too much sugar (crash in 2 hours), too much caffeine (jitters), anything new (stomach risk).
- [ ] **Caffeine: one normal cup of your usual brew.** Not three. Not the strong stuff. Your normal cup.
- [ ] **Eat slowly.** Use the time to mentally rehearse TMAY in your head once.

### 8:45 AM — Light Review *(15 min only)*

This is the trap. The instinct is to study for an hour. Don't.

**15 minutes, three items:**

- [ ] **TMAY 90s script — read silently once, deliver aloud once.** If it comes out clean, move on.
- [ ] **Top 3 STAR stories — read the 4-bullet summary silently.** Don't deliver aloud, just refresh the structure.
- [ ] **JD — re-read it once.** Note the 3 tech keywords you'll work into answers.

**That's it. Close the laptop.**

Do NOT:

- Look up new tech topics.
- Try new LeetCode problems.
- Re-read war-room files end-to-end.
- Watch interview YouTube.
- Scroll Reddit r/cscareerquestions.

If you find yourself wanting to "study one more thing," that's anxiety, not preparation. Close the laptop. Trust the work.

### 9:00 AM — Mental Preparation *(15 min)*

- [ ] **Sit in a quiet spot.** Phone face-down.
- [ ] **Box breathing — 5 minutes.** Inhale 4, hold 4, exhale 4, hold 4. Ten cycles.
- [ ] **Visualization — 5 minutes.** Eyes closed. Picture: walking into the room, shaking the interviewer's hand, sitting down, hearing "Tell me about yourself," delivering the script clean, the interviewer nodding. Picture the whole 30 minutes going well.
- [ ] **Affirmation — 5 minutes.** Silent. "I've done the prep. I know my material. I'm here to have a conversation, not a test. Whatever they ask, I have something to say."
- [ ] **Posture check.** Stand up. Roll shoulders back. Stand straight. Carry that posture into the room.

### 9:15 AM — Final Logistics *(15 min)*

- [ ] **Bag check** — resume copies, JD, questions page, notepad, two pens, water bottle, ID, charger.
- [ ] **Phone fully charged.** Bring charger.
- [ ] **Wallet, keys, transport card.**
- [ ] **Check Maps for current traffic.** Adjust departure time if needed.
- [ ] **Leave 30+ minutes earlier than the absolute minimum.** Better to wait at a café near the office than to rush.

### 9:30 AM onwards — Travel

You're in transit. Continue to [One Hour Before](one-hour-before.md).

## The 5-day practice drill *(rehearse the morning routine before you need it)*

- [ ] **Day 1 — Wake at the interview-day time.** Even if no interview. Run the routine. Note what felt slow.
- [ ] **Day 2 — Try the breakfast.** Cook the exact breakfast you'll have on interview day. Confirm it agrees with you.
- [ ] **Day 3 — Run a tech check.** If virtual, open Zoom at the interview time, test the room, the lighting, the audio.
- [ ] **Day 4 — Mock-leave the house.** Get dressed, pack the bag, walk to the door. Time the routine end-to-end.
- [ ] **Day 5 — Box breathing + visualization for 10 minutes.** Build the habit so it's automatic on the day.

## Recovery — when the morning falls apart

You overslept. You spilled coffee on the shirt. Traffic is bad. Tech check failed.

> *"What can I still control? Posture, breathing, a clean smile when I walk in. The rest is noise."*

If you're running late:

- Call/email the recruiter immediately. *"Running 10 minutes late due to [traffic / tech issue], apologies, will be there at [time]."* Honesty + heads-up beats silent lateness.

If you spilled on the shirt:

- Backup shirt. (You should have one in the bag for any interview that matters. Add this to the Day 1 pack.)

If you overslept:

- Skip the workout, keep the shower, eat a banana on the way. Do the breathing in transit. Do not skip the breathing.

If tech check fails 30 min before virtual:

- Switch device (phone). Email recruiter with new join link if needed. Restart router. Do not panic-troubleshoot for 25 minutes — switch.

## Variants

=== "Interview at 9 AM (early morning)"

    Compress: wake at 6 AM. Skip the workout, keep the walk. Eat a small breakfast. Most of the routine works at compressed scale.

=== "Interview at 2 PM (afternoon)"

    Standard routine in morning. Light lunch at 12:30 PM — same rules as breakfast (no new food, no heavy, no excess caffeine).

=== "Interview at 7 PM (evening)"

    Lighter day. Avoid heavy lunch. 4-hour countdown starts at 3 PM with the same protocol. No coffee after 4 PM.

=== "Multi-round day (4+ interviews back-to-back)"

    Bigger breakfast (you'll need fuel). Pack 2 snacks + extra water + mints. Plan a 5-minute reset between rounds.

=== "Virtual interview"

    Subtract the travel time. Reclaim those 30 minutes as a quiet sit, not as bonus study time.

## Common traps

| Trap | Why it kills | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Snoozing the alarm | Drowsy for 2 hours | First alarm = up |
| Phone-first morning | Anxiety from inbox/social | No phone for 20 min |
| Skipping breakfast | Brain fog by 11 AM | Eat what you know |
| Three coffees | Jitters, shaky hands | One normal cup |
| 60-min study session at 9 AM | Anxiety spiral, no retention | 15 min light review, hard cap |
| New food this morning | Stomach distress | Eat your usual breakfast |
| Hard workout | Soreness, fatigue at 11 AM | Light movement only |
| Re-reading war-room files end-to-end | Surfaces topics you "forgot," panic | Skim TMAY + STAR + JD only |
| No buffer time | Lateness, cortisol spike | Leave 30 min earlier than minimum |
| No mental prep | Walks in tense | Box breathing + visualization, 15 min |
