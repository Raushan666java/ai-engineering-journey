# 06 — The Pro Execution System

## Learning Objectives

After this chapter you will be able to run a three-loop study engine (learn, retrieve, apply) inside a 3-hour weekday, build notes that compress to one page per block, use teach-back as a knowledge test rather than a performance, track leading indicators instead of mood, and recover from a missed week, a plateau or a burnout spiral without abandoning the plan.

## 1. The three-loop engine

Every study session contains three loops. Most candidates only run the first, which is why they read 900 pages and score 95.

| Loop | What happens | Time share | Failure mode if skipped |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Learn** | Read, diagram, explain aloud | 45% | Nothing to retrieve from |
| **Retrieve** | Closed-book questions, blank-page recall | 35% | Recognition mistaken for knowledge |
| **Apply** | Timed numericals, scenario questions | 20% | Knowledge that evaporates under time pressure |

### 1.1 The 90-minute technical block

| Minutes | Loop | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| 0–5 | Retrieve | Yesterday's 5 questions, closed book |
| 5–45 | Learn | Read one section; draw the diagram; say it aloud in your own words |
| 45–75 | Retrieve | 15 questions on today's section, closed book, timed |
| 75–90 | Apply | 3 numericals at 60 seconds each; write the steps, not just the answer |

**The 45-minute cap on "Learn" is deliberate.** Beyond that, retention per minute collapses. If a section needs more, split it across two days rather than reading for 90 minutes.

## 2. The note system

Notes are for retrieval, not for beauty. Three artefacts per block, no more:

| Artefact | Format | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Skeleton** | One page, headings only, written before reading | Gives the brain a structure to hang facts on |
| **Annotated chapter** | The chapter itself, with margin marks | The reference; never rewritten |
| **One-page map** | Diagram + 10 facts + 5 formulas, from memory | The revision artefact; rebuilt at weeks 10, 20, 26, 30 |

**Rule: never copy a chapter into a notebook.** Copying is transcription, and transcription produces the feeling of learning with none of the substance. If you cannot compress a block to one page, you do not understand its structure yet.

### 2.1 The blank-page test

Once a week, take a blank sheet, write the block name at the top, and reproduce its structure from memory in 10 minutes. Compare with the map. The gap is your study list for the week. This single exercise is worth more than re-reading four chapters.

## 3. Teach-back

The fastest way to find out whether you understand a mechanism is to explain it to someone who will interrupt.

| Level | Method | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Self | Explain aloud to a wall, no notes, then check | 5 min |
| Recording | Record a 3-minute voice note on your phone | 3 min |
| Adversarial | Explain to a friend who asks "why?" three times | 10 min |

**Use teach-back for L2 topics specifically** — IMS registration, MPLS label operations, 5G SA vs NSA, PERT vs CPM, OFDMA vs SC-FDMA. These are the questions where the paper asks "which element does what", and a candidate who can teach the flow answers them in 15 seconds.

**Test:** if you cannot explain it in 3 minutes without notes, you have a recognition-level familiarity, not knowledge. Schedule a second pass.

## 4. Deliberate practice on numericals

The L4 families in `01-master-syllabus.md` are 23 skills, not 23 topics. Skills are built by repetition with feedback, at the edge of your current speed.

### 4.1 The 60-second drill

| Phase | Target |
|---|---|
| Attempt | Solve one numerical in 60 seconds, written, units included |
| Mark | Correct / wrong, and *why* |
| Repeat | Same family, new numbers, immediately |
| Volume | 10 per family per session |
| Mastery | 9/10 correct at under 60 s |

### 4.2 The difficulty ladder

| Rung | Task | Move up when |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Solve with the formula sheet open | 8/10 correct |
| 2 | Solve with the sheet closed | 8/10 correct |
| 3 | Solve under 60 s | 8/10 correct |
| 4 | Solve inside a mixed set of 20 | 8/10 correct |
| 5 | Solve after a 7-day gap | 8/10 correct |

Rung 5 is the one that matters, and it is the one almost nobody does. A skill that only works the day you learned it is not a skill.

## 5. Interleaving

Blocked practice (all subnetting, then all modulation) feels productive and tests badly. The paper interleaves; your practice should too.

### 5.1 The weekly practice grid

| Day | Practice type |
|---|---|
| Mon | New topic only |
| Tue | New topic + 5 questions from Monday |
| Wed | New topic + 10 mixed questions from the current block |
| Thu | New topic + 10 mixed questions from two blocks |
| Fri | 20 mixed questions from all studied blocks |
| Sat | 40-question sectional set, timed |
| Sun | Error-log audit + blank-page test |

### 5.2 The three-week cycle

Every third week, replace Friday's mixed set with a set drawn only from material studied **three weeks earlier**. This is the point where forgetting is strongest and where the intervention is cheapest.

## 6. Metrics: leading vs lagging

Lagging indicators (mock scores) tell you where you were. Leading indicators tell you where you are going. Track both, act on the leading ones.

| Indicator | Type | Weekly target |
|---|---|---|
| Hours studied | Leading | 21–24 |
| Chapters completed with quiz ≥ 80% | Leading | 1–2 |
| Numerical drills completed | Leading | 60 |
| Error-log entries classified | Leading | 100% of errors |
| Flashcards reviewed | Leading | 150 |
| Blank-page tests passed | Leading | 1 |
| Aptitude accuracy | Leading | ≥ 80% |
| Sectional set score | Lagging | Rising trend |
| Mock net score | Lagging | Per gate |

**Rule: if the leading indicators are green and the lagging ones are flat for three weeks, the method is wrong, not the effort.** Change the method (more retrieval, more L4, more mixed sets), not the hours.

## 7. The weekly self-audit (Sunday, 20 minutes)

Answer in writing. Vague answers are the failure mode.

1. How many hours did I actually study, and how many did I plan?
2. Which chapter did I finish, and what did I score on its quiz?
3. Which numerical family did I drill, and at what accuracy and speed?
4. How many errors did I log, and what was the dominant cause?
5. Which block is weakest right now, by score?
6. Did I hit this week's gate? If not, what is the repeat plan?
7. What did I skip that I told myself I would do?
8. Is my sleep at 7+ hours on at least five nights?
9. What is the single highest-value thing for next week?
10. Am I on schedule against `02-study-plan-32-weeks.md` — and if not, do I switch variants?

## 8. Monthly recalibration (last Sunday of the month, 45 minutes)

1. Recompute the mock trend with `net = a(1.25p − 0.25)`.
2. Compare block scores against the SC target table in `05-sc-category-playbook.md` §9.
3. Rebuild two formula sheets from memory; score them honestly.
4. Reorder the next month's weeks if a block is failing a gate twice.
5. Check administrative items: certificate status, notification watch, portfolio applications.
6. Decide one thing to **stop** doing. Every month should retire something.

## 9. Energy management

Cognitive performance is a physiological variable, not a virtue.

| Factor | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Sleep | 7–8 h, same wake time daily | Memory consolidation happens in sleep; a short night erases the day's retrieval gains |
| Exercise | 20–30 min, 4x/week | Best-evidenced cognitive enhancer available for free |
| Screens | No scrolling before the first study block | Attention residue costs the first 30 minutes |
| Caffeine | Not after 2 pm | Sleep quality, not sleep quantity |
| Study timing | Hardest block at your peak (usually morning) | Numerical drills need the peak, reading does not |
| Breaks | 5 min per 45, 20 min per 3 h | Sustained attention decays predictably |
| Entrainment | From week 24, study at exam start time | Reduces exam-morning variability |

**Entrainment matters more than people think.** If your mocks are always at 9 pm and the exam is at 10 am, you have trained a different brain.

## 10. Failure modes and recovery

| Failure | Early signal | Recovery |
|---|---|---|
| **Missed week** | Two days without a session | Do not "catch up" by doubling. Resume at the current week, absorb the gap by switching to the 20-week variant |
| **Plateau at ~110** | Net flat for 3 mocks with green leading indicators | The ceiling is L4. 100 numericals in a week, nothing else |
| **Burnout** | Dreading sessions, poor sleep, cynicism | 3-day full stop; then 5 days at half volume; keep the aptitude block only |
| **Anxiety spiral** | Checking cutoff rumours daily | Ban aggregator sites; one official portal check per week; run one mock to restore agency |
| **Comparison trap** | Telegram groups making you feel behind | Leave the groups. Your error log is the only benchmark that matters |
| **Over-reading** | 6 chapters read, 0 quizzes passed | Hard rule: no new chapter until the previous quiz is ≥ 80% |
| **Certificate complacency** | "I'll do it after the notification" | Do it this week; authorities are slow and the DV date is immovable |

## 11. The 6-hour weekend deep-dive

Use this for a block that failed a gate, or for a weekend with no mock.

| Block | Minutes | Activity |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 30 | Blank-page test on the block; list the gaps |
| 2 | 90 | Re-teach the two weakest sub-topics, aloud, from the chapter |
| 3 | 60 | 30 questions on the block, timed, closed book |
| 4 | 60 | 20 numericals from the block's L4 families |
| 5 | 45 | Build the one-page map from memory; compare |
| 6 | 30 | Log every error with a cause; set next week's target |
| 7 | 45 | Aptitude set (do not skip because it is a technical day) |

## 12. Anti-patterns that look like work

1. **Highlighting.** Produces familiarity, not retrieval.
2. **Watching videos at 1.5x.** Passive; retention near zero without notes and questions.
3. **Rewriting notes.** Transcription with extra steps.
4. **Doing only the easy block.** Feels good, moves the score by zero.
5. **Mock after mock without auditing.** Ten mocks, no diagnosis, no change.
6. **Studying the syllabus you like.** The paper does not care what you enjoy.
7. **Waiting for the notification to start.** It lands ~8 weeks from now; that is 8 wasted weeks.

## Summary

Execution is a system of small, enforced loops: 45 minutes of learning, 30 of retrieval, 15 of timed application, every day, with a weekly audit that acts on leading indicators. Notes compress to one page per block or they have failed. Numerical skills are drilled on a five-rung ladder that ends with a seven-day gap, because that is what the exam actually tests. Energy, not willpower, decides whether the plan survives month four.

## Practical Takeaways

- Run the 90-minute block: 5 retrieve, 40 learn, 30 retrieve, 15 apply.
- One page per block, from memory, weekly.
- Teach-back for every L2 mechanism; 3 minutes, no notes.
- Drill L4 families to rung 5 (correct after a 7-day gap).
- Track leading indicators weekly; change the method when they are green and the score is not.
- Sleep 7–8 hours. It is part of the syllabus.
- Nothing new in the last 14 days.

## Chapter Quiz

**Q1.** The correct time split inside a study session is approximately:
(a) 100% learning (b) 45% learn, 35% retrieve, 20% apply (c) 20% learn, 80% apply (d) 50% notes, 50% reading
**Answer: (b).**

**Q2.** A numerical family is "mastered" at rung 5 when you can:
(a) solve it with the formula sheet (b) solve it in under 30 s (c) solve it correctly after a 7-day gap (d) explain it aloud
**Answer: (c).**

**Q3.** Leading indicators are green but the mock score has been flat for three weeks. The correct response is:
(a) study more hours (b) change the method (c) take more mocks (d) lower the target
**Answer: (b).**

**Q4.** The most reliable early signal of an L4 ceiling at ~110 is:
(a) low aptitude scores (b) flat net score with high attempts and high accuracy on L1 (c) missed weeks (d) anxiety
**Answer: (b).**

**Q5.** After a missed week, the correct recovery is:
(a) double the hours to catch up (b) skip the gate and continue (c) resume at the current week and switch to a compressed variant (d) restart from chapter one
**Answer: (c).**

## Exercises

1. Run the 90-minute block for three consecutive days and record the actual minute split. Compare with the target.
2. Take one block you have finished and produce its one-page map from memory in 10 minutes. Score it out of 10 against the chapter.
3. Pick three L4 families and take each to rung 5 over ten days. Log accuracy and time at each rung.
4. Write your ten weekly audit answers for the week just finished, even if the week was poor.
5. Identify the anti-pattern you are most prone to, and write the specific rule that prevents it.
