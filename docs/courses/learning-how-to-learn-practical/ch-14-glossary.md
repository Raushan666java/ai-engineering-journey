# Master Glossary

> **400+ terms** defined across all 13 chapters. Use this as a quick reference while studying.

## A

**Active Recall** — A learning technique where you actively retrieve information from memory rather than passively re-reading it. The single most effective study method known to cognitive science. (Ch 4)

**Act** — The fourth phase of the OODA loop. Execute your chosen action. In learning: do the practice problems, write the code, teach the concept. (Ch 1)

**Attention Residue** — The cognitive cost of switching between tasks. When you leave one task unfinished, part of your attention stays on it, reducing performance on the next task. (Ch 5)

**Auditory Learning** — A VARK learning style. Prefers learning through listening — lectures, discussions, audio recordings, podcasts. (Ch 3)

## B

**Backlog** — A prioritized list of topics or tasks you plan to study. Used in sprint planning to decide what to work on next. (Ch 2)

**Blank Page Method** — An active recall technique: close your book, open a blank page, and write everything you remember about a topic without looking at notes. (Ch 4)

**Block Schedule** — A daily schedule divided into fixed time blocks for different types of study work (deep work, practice, review). Matched to your energy levels. (Ch 3)

**Building in Public** — Sharing your learning journey publicly (via blog, social media, or presentations) to create accountability, get feedback, and help others. (Ch 12)

## C

**Cadence** — The rhythm or frequency of a recurring activity. Examples: daily check-in, weekly review, monthly retrospective. (Ch 1)

**Capstone** — A final project that integrates all skills learned across a course. In this course, Ch 13 is the capstone where you design your complete learning system. (Ch 13)

**Capture Pad** — A physical notepad kept beside you during deep work sessions. When an unrelated thought or urge arises, write it down instead of acting on it. Returns focus to the task. (Ch 5)

**Cheat Sheet** — A one-page summary of key formulas, concepts, and common mistakes for a topic. Creating one forces clarity; reviewing one saves time. (Ch 12)

**Common Mistakes** — A section in each chapter listing typical errors learners make and how to avoid them. (All chapters)

**Compound Effect** — Small daily actions that accumulate into significant results over time. The daily minimum rule leverages this. (Ch 2)

**Concept Gap** — A type of error where you lack understanding of the fundamental idea needed to solve a problem. Fix: review theory, do easy problems. (Ch 7)

**Context Switching** — Moving between unrelated tasks frequently. Each switch costs 15+ minutes of lost focus due to attention residue. (Ch 5)

**Course Correction** — Adjusting your study plan based on performance data. If a metric stays flat for 2 weeks, change your approach. (Ch 11)

**Critical 20%** — The small subset of topics or skills that produce 80% of results. Derived from the Pareto Principle. Find and master this first. (Ch 2)

## D

**Daily Log** — A simple record of what you studied each day: subject, time spent, takeaways. The foundation of all tracking. (Ch 11)

**Daily Minimum** — The smallest amount of study you commit to every single day. Set it so low you can't fail. Never break the streak. (Ch 2)

**Decide** — The third phase of the OODA loop. Choose the best action based on your analysis. In learning: pick which topic to study next, which sprint to start. (Ch 1)

**Deep Work** — Professional activity performed in a state of distraction-free concentration that pushes your cognitive capabilities to their limit. (Ch 5)

**Deliberate Practice** — Structured practice with specific goals, immediate feedback, and tasks just beyond your current ability. Not the same as mindless repetition. (Ch 7)

**Diagnostic Test** — A baseline test taken before starting a study plan. Used to identify current level and weak areas. (Ch 8)

**Difficulty Rating** — A label (Beginner, Intermediate, Advanced) indicating the prerequisite knowledge needed for a chapter. (All chapters)

**Distraction Audit** — A 3-day tally of every interruption during study sessions. Used to identify and eliminate top distractions. (Ch 5)

## E

**Energy-Aware Scheduling** — Planning study activities based on your natural energy peaks and troughs throughout the day. Match deep work to peaks, shallow work to troughs. (Ch 3)

**Energy Pattern** — Your personal daily cycle of high and low energy. Typically: morning peak, afternoon dip, evening rise. Track for 3 days to find yours. (Ch 3)

**Environment Layer** — The foundation of the learning stack. Physical space where you study: phone away, desk clear, Do Not Disturb on. Fix this first before any technique. (Ch 1)

**Error Analysis** — The systematic categorization and study of mistakes. After a practice set or mock test, classify each error by type and fix accordingly. (Ch 7)

**Error Log** — A running record of mistakes categorized by type (concept gap, calculation, speed, reading). Used to identify patterns and focus practice. (Ch 7)

**Evening Review** — A 5-minute end-of-day reflection: what did I study, what stuck, what didn't, tomorrow's priority. (Ch 3)

## F

**Feedback Loop** — A system that uses output data to inform future input. In learning: track metrics → review weekly → adjust approach → track again. (Ch 1)

**Feynman Technique** — Explain a concept in plain language as if teaching a 12-year-old. Every jargon word you use is a gap in your understanding. (Ch 9)

**Flashcards** — Small cards with a question on one side and answer on the other. Used for spaced repetition. Can be physical (Leitner box) or digital (Anki). (Ch 4)

**Focus Score** — A self-rated 1-5 measure of how focused you felt during a study session. A leading indicator of learning effectiveness. (Ch 11)

**Foundation Phase** — The first phase of a 30-day sprint. Focus on core concepts, vocabulary, and fundamentals. No speed work, no advanced problems. (Ch 2)

## G

**Gap Analysis** — Comparing your current knowledge against the full syllabus of an exam or topic. Identifies what you need to learn. (Ch 8)

**Goal Decomposition** — Breaking a large goal (e.g., "crack SSC CGL") into smaller pieces: milestones → sprints → daily actions. (Ch 1)

## H

**Hard Subjects** — Topics with high prerequisite chains and dense jargon. Mastered through spiral learning, prerequisite mapping, and the Feynman technique. (Ch 9)

**Hero's Journey** — A narrative framework for learning: start as a beginner, face challenges, get help (mentors/resources), transform, return to share knowledge. (Ch 12)

## I

**Implementation Pass** — The third pass of spiral learning. After understanding the big picture (Pass 1) and mechanics (Pass 2), implement by building/solving/doing. (Ch 9)

**Input Metrics** — See "Leading Indicators".

**Integration** — Combining all 12 learning techniques into one unified system using the OODA loop as the backbone. The goal of Ch 13. (Ch 13)

**Interview Prep** — Structured preparation for any interview (tech, government, campus) using a 3-phase system: foundations → company-specific → mocks. (Ch 10)

## J

**Just-in-Time Learning** — Learning a concept exactly when you need it, not in advance. Useful for large prerequisite chains where learning everything upfront is impractical. (Ch 9)

## K

**Kinesthetic Learning** — A VARK learning style. Prefers learning through doing — hands-on practice, experiments, building, moving. (Ch 3)

**Knowledge Map** — A visual diagram of a topic showing its subtopics, their relationships, and prerequisite dependencies. (Ch 6)

## L

**Lagging Indicators** — Metrics that tell you about past performance. Examples: mock test score, accuracy percentage. Useful for weekly/monthly review. (Ch 11)

**Leading Indicators** — Metrics that predict future performance. Examples: focus score, deep work hours, problems solved per day. Track these daily. (Ch 11)

**Learning Log** — A running journal of what you learn each week. Not just what you studied, but key insights and remaining questions. (Ch 12)

**Learning OS** — Your personal learning system. The combination of environment, tools, methods, and the OODA loop that governs how you learn. (Ch 1)

**Learning Stack** — The four layers of a learning system: Environment → Tools → Methods → System. Fix from bottom to top. (Ch 1)

**Learning Style** — Your preferred mode of taking in information. VARK model: Visual, Auditory, Read/Write, Kinesthetic. Not fixed — develop all styles. (Ch 3)

**Leitner Box** — A physical spaced repetition system using 5 boxes with increasing review intervals. Cards move forward on correct answers, backward on wrong answers. (Ch 4)

## M

**Mastery Phase** — The third phase of a 30-day sprint or study plan. Full mock tests, speed drills, error analysis. Target: 90%+ accuracy. (Ch 2, Ch 8)

**Mentoring** — Teaching someone who is at an earlier stage than you. The highest form of learning — you must truly understand to guide another. (Ch 12)

**Mermaid Diagram** — A text-based diagram format used throughout this course for flowcharts, Gantt charts, and process maps. (All chapters)

**Milestone** — A significant checkpoint in a learning journey. Between a goal and daily actions. Example: "Complete all geometry topics" is a milestone. (Ch 1)

**Mock Interview** — A practice interview with a partner, mentor, or recording. The primary improvement driver in interview preparation. (Ch 10)

**Mock Test** — A full-length practice exam under timed conditions. Used to measure progress and identify weak areas before the real exam. (Ch 8)

## N

**No-Code Track** — The plain-language walkthrough in every chapter. No programming knowledge required. Follow this track if you're not an engineer. (Index)

## O

**Observe** — The first phase of the OODA loop. Gather information. In learning: read, watch, listen, take diagnostic tests. (Ch 1)

**OODA Loop** — Observe, Orient, Decide, Act. A decision-making framework developed by John Boyd. The backbone of this course's learning system. (Ch 1)

**Orient** — The second phase of the OODA loop. Analyze what you observed. In learning: identify gaps, categorize errors, map prerequisites. (Ch 1)

**Output Metrics** — See "Leading Indicators". Metrics that measure what you produced, not just time spent. (Ch 11)

## P

**Pareto Principle** — 80% of results come from 20% of efforts. Applied to learning: find the 20% of topics that give 80% of exam scores. Master those first. (Ch 2)

**Phased Plan** — A study plan divided into distinct phases (Foundation → Practice → Mastery) with specific goals and milestones for each. (Ch 8)

**Plain-Language Walkthrough** — The no-code version of examples in each chapter. A step-by-step scenario using everyday language. (All chapters)

**Plateau** — A period where your performance metric stays flat despite consistent effort. Detection rule: if flat for 2 weeks, change your approach. (Ch 11)

**Pomodoro Technique** — A time management method using focused work intervals (typically 25 min) followed by short breaks (5 min). Adapted for different task types in this course. (Ch 3)

**Practice System** — A structured approach to deliberate practice: 3-pass method (Understand → Attempt → Analyze), error categorization, spaced review. (Ch 7)

**Prerequisite** — A concept you need to understand BEFORE learning a target topic. Mapping prerequisites prevents hitting walls. (Ch 9)

**Prerequisite Chain** — The sequence of concepts leading up to a target topic. Each link must be understood before the next. (Ch 9)

**Progress Tracker** — A checklist of all chapters with columns for each section. Helps you track completion and stay motivated. (Index)

**Protege Effect** — The phenomenon where teaching someone else deepens your own understanding. You learn more by teaching than by studying alone. (Ch 12)

## Q

**Quick Reference** — A printable cheat sheet at the end of each chapter containing key formulas, templates, and steps. (All chapters)

**Quick Start (10 min)** — A section at the beginning of each chapter showing exactly what to do in the first 10 minutes. For readers who are short on time. (All chapters)

**Quiz** — Five multiple-choice questions per chapter with explanations for both correct and common wrong answers. Self-test after each chapter. (All chapters)

## R

**Read/Write Learning** — A VARK learning style. Prefers learning through reading texts and writing notes, summaries, and lists. (Ch 3)

**Recognition** — Identifying the correct answer from a set of options (multiple choice). Creates an illusion of competence. Weaker than recall. (Ch 4)

**Recall** — Generating information from memory without cues. The gold standard of learning. Built through active recall techniques. (Ch 4)

**Resource Learning** — The process of extracting knowledge from any source — textbooks, video courses, lectures, codebases — using the SQ3R method. (Ch 6)

**Retrospective** — A periodic review (monthly recommended) where you look back at what worked, what didn't, and what to change. Longer and deeper than a weekly review. (Ch 1)

## S

**Shallow Work** — Non-cognitively demanding, logistical-style work. Examples: checking email, organizing files, browsing. Does not build skill. (Ch 5)

**SM-2 Algorithm** — A spaced repetition algorithm that calculates optimal review intervals based on your self-rated recall quality (0-5). Used by Anki. (Ch 4)

**Spaced Repetition** — A learning technique where you review material at increasing intervals over time. Prevents forgetting and builds long-term memory. (Ch 4)

**Spiral Learning** — Learning a topic in three passes: Big Picture (what/why) → Mechanics (how) → Implementation (do/build/solve). Each pass goes deeper. (Ch 9)

**Sprint** — A fixed time period (typically 30 days) dedicated to mastering a specific topic or skill. Has three phases: Foundation, Core, Mastery. (Ch 2)

**SQ3R** — A resource learning method: Survey, Question, Read, Recite, Review. Works for textbooks, articles, and videos. (Ch 6)

**STAR Method** — A structured way to answer behavioral interview questions: Situation, Task, Action, Result. (Ch 10)

**Startup Ritual** — The same 3 actions you perform before every study session. Signals to your brain: "It's time to focus." (Ch 3)

**Streak** — A consecutive sequence of days where you met your daily minimum. Never break the streak — it's more important than any single day's output. (Ch 2)

**Syllabus** — The complete list of topics covered by an exam. The starting point for any exam study plan. (Ch 8)

**System Audit** — A self-assessment of your current learning system across 8 dimensions: goal clarity, study plan, energy scheduling, active recall, deep work, error analysis, tracking, teaching. (Ch 13)

## T

**Teaching** — The act of explaining a concept to someone else. The most effective way to solidify your own understanding. (Ch 12)

**30-Day Sprint** — See "Sprint".

**3-Pass Method** — A practice technique: Pass 1 (Understand), Pass 2 (Attempt), Pass 3 (Analyze). Used for solving problems in any subject. (Ch 7)

**Time Estimate** — The approximate total time needed to complete a chapter, shown in the badge at the top. (All chapters)

**Tracking** — The practice of recording learning metrics daily to measure progress and detect plateaus. (Ch 11)

**Tutorial Hell** — A state where you consume tutorials without implementing what you learn. Feels productive but builds no real skill. (Ch 1)

## U

**Universal Exercises** — The first 3 exercises in each chapter that require no programming knowledge. Designed for all learners. (All chapters)

## V

**VARK** — A learning styles model: Visual, Auditory, Read/Write, Kinesthetic. Knowing your dominant style helps choose effective study methods. (Ch 3)

**Visual Learning** — A VARK learning style. Prefers learning through diagrams, charts, mind maps, videos, and color coding. (Ch 3)

## W

**Weakest Link** — The component of your learning system with the lowest performance. Fix this first before optimizing anything else. (Ch 13)

**Weekly Review** — A 15-minute Sunday session where you review the past week's metrics, identify plateaus, and plan the next week. (Ch 11)

## Y

**Your Learning OS** — See "Learning OS". The overall system you build in Ch 1 and integrate in Ch 13.
