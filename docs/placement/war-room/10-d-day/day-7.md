# Day 7 — The Week Plan

> **Status:** ○ Planned · Edit this line to ◐ Practicing · ● Drilled

You got the interview invite. The date is one week out. You have seven days. The wrong move is to panic-grind LeetCode for 12 hours a day. The right move is a calendar.

This drill gives you the calendar. Each day is 2–4 focused hours. Not more. Sleep is part of the protocol.

---

## Inhale.

Open your calendar. Block 2–4 hours per day for the next seven days at the time you're sharpest (usually morning, before noise).

The rest of the day, **live normally**. Eat, walk, scroll, see friends. Burnout the day before the interview is the most common failure mode.

---

## The Script — what you tell yourself

> *"Seven days is enough. I'm not learning new material. I'm sharpening what I already know, in the specific shape this company asks it in."*

That's the frame. **Sharpening, not learning.** If you don't know it by Day 7, you don't know it by Day 0 either — pick a different angle.

---

## The Anatomy — why a calendar wins

Without a calendar, the week becomes a guilt loop. You "study" for 11 hours on Day 5, sleep four hours, and walk in foggy on Day 0.

With a calendar, each day has one job. You finish the job, close the laptop, recover. By Day 0, you're rested and rehearsed instead of fried and unfocused.

The interviewer doesn't measure how many hours you studied. They measure how clearly you think under pressure.

---

## The 7-Day Calendar

### Day 7 — Company Research + JD Breakdown *(3 hours)*

The most under-done step. Most candidates skip it.

- [ ] **(45 min)** Read the company's About page, careers page, engineering blog (if any), and most recent product launch.
- [ ] **(30 min)** Open Glassdoor, Levels.fyi, LeetCode discuss for this company. Read 10 recent interview reviews. Note the *patterns* — what topics keep coming up?
- [ ] **(45 min)** Read the JD line by line. Highlight every tech keyword (Laravel, MySQL, Docker, AWS, etc.). Map each to your projects — *"For Docker, I'll talk about how I containerized X."*
- [ ] **(30 min)** Write 5 questions you'll ask the interviewer. Make them specific — *"I noticed your blog post on migrating to PostgreSQL — what was the catch you didn't expect?"* beats *"What's the team culture?"*.
- [ ] **(30 min)** Update your resume bullets to match the JD vocabulary. Same achievement, but if their JD says "RESTful APIs", your bullet should say "RESTful APIs" not "web services".

**Output:** A one-page note with company facts, JD-matched projects, 5 questions, and updated resume bullets.

### Day 6 — STAR Stories Review *(3 hours)*

Six stories. That's the goal.

- [ ] **(60 min)** List 6 STAR stories — 2 technical wins, 2 conflict/team stories, 2 failure/learning stories. Write each in 4 bullets: Situation, Task, Action, Result.
- [ ] **(60 min)** Read each one aloud. Time it. **Each must land at 60–90 seconds.** Trim the ones that go over.
- [ ] **(30 min)** For each story, identify the *metric* in the Result. No metric = weak story. Find a number (users, percent, time saved, bug count).
- [ ] **(30 min)** Test each story against 3 common prompts: *"Tell me about a time you failed."* / *"Describe a conflict with a teammate."* / *"What's your proudest project?"* — at least one story should fit each.

**Output:** 6 STAR stories on one page, each 60–90s, each with a metric.

### Day 5 — Top 10 Tech Questions for the Role *(4 hours)*

This is the longest day. Earn your rest.

- [ ] **(45 min)** Based on the JD and Glassdoor patterns, list the top 10 tech topics they'll probably ask. (For Laravel role: N+1, service container, queues, middleware, migrations, eloquent relationships, validation, auth, caching, testing.)
- [ ] **(2 hours)** For each topic, drill the matching war-room file. Read aloud. Re-write the code block from memory. Time the script.
- [ ] **(45 min)** Pick the 3 weakest topics. Drill them again. Re-do the code from memory.
- [ ] **(30 min)** Skim 5 LeetCode Easy + 3 Medium in the language the company uses (Java for product cos, Python for ML, PHP+SQL for Laravel shops).

**Output:** 10 drilled topics, 3 re-drilled weak topics, hands warm on coding syntax.

### Day 4 — Mock Interview *(3 hours)*

Book a friend, a senior, or use Pramp/Interviewing.io.

- [ ] **(90 min)** Run a 45-min mock — 5 min TMAY, 20 min behavioral, 20 min tech. Have them grade you brutally.
- [ ] **(30 min)** Listen back if recorded. Note 3 fixes: a filler word, a rambling answer, a missed STAR structure.
- [ ] **(60 min)** Re-drill the 3 fixes. Re-record the same question. Confirm the fix landed.

**Output:** 3 specific improvements drilled, fresh under-pressure reps.

### Day 3 — Weak Areas Drill *(3 hours)*

By now you know your weaknesses. Attack them.

- [ ] **(60 min)** Top 3 weakest tech topics — re-drill, re-code, time the answer.
- [ ] **(60 min)** Top 2 shaky STAR stories — re-deliver, sharpen the metric, kill the rambling.
- [ ] **(60 min)** Run through TMAY (90s version) 5 times cold. Then 30s version 5 times. Then 5-min version once.

**Output:** Weak spots reinforced. TMAY locked in three lengths.

### Day 2 — Light Review + Sleep Discipline *(2 hours)*

You are not learning today. You are *resting smart*.

- [ ] **(45 min)** Skim — don't drill — the top 5 tech topics from Day 5. Just re-read the scripts.
- [ ] **(30 min)** Re-read your 6 STAR stories silently. Don't deliver them aloud — let your brain rest.
- [ ] **(30 min)** Pack tomorrow's bag: resume copies (3), notepad, pen, water bottle, ID, JD printout. Lay out clothes.
- [ ] **(15 min)** Set 3 alarms. Pick the route. If virtual, test laptop, camera, mic, internet.
- [ ] **Sleep by 10 PM.** Phone in another room.

**Output:** Bag packed, clothes out, alarms set, brain calm.

### Day 1 — Walk Through *(2 hours)*

- [ ] **(30 min)** TMAY out loud 3 times — 30s, 90s, 5min. That's it.
- [ ] **(30 min)** Read your 5 prepared questions for the interviewer aloud. Pick the 3 strongest.
- [ ] **(30 min)** Walk or light exercise. Get blood moving.
- [ ] **(30 min)** Re-read the JD and company About page once. Just to refresh.
- [ ] **Light dinner. Sleep by 10 PM.** No new material. No LeetCode. No YouTube interview-anxiety videos.

**Output:** Calm, rested, ready.

### Day 0 — Interview Day

You've done the work. Today you execute. Open [Four Hours Before](four-hours-before.md).

## The 5-day practice drill *(use this if you have more time)*

- [ ] **Day 1 — Build your calendar.** Block the seven sessions in your phone calendar with alarms.
- [ ] **Day 2 — Pre-research the company.** Even before Day 7, gather the materials.
- [ ] **Day 3 — Inventory STAR stories.** Have 6 ready before Day 6 forces you.
- [ ] **Day 4 — Mock interview baseline.** One mock with a friend before the formal Day 4.
- [ ] **Day 5 — Sleep audit.** Track your sleep for 5 days. If you're under 7 hours regularly, fix that before Day 7 arrives.

---

## Recovery — when the week falls apart

You got sick on Day 5. You had a family emergency on Day 4. The calendar broke.

> *"Skip to the priority days. Day 7 (research) and Day 1 (rest) are non-negotiable. The middle days compress into whatever time you have."*

If you only have 3 days left, jump to **[Day 3 — The Crunch Playbook](day-3.md)**.

If you only have 24 hours left, jump to **[Day 1 — No New Learning](day-1.md)**.

Don't try to do the whole 7-day plan in 2 days. That's how you arrive broken.

---

## Variants

=== "Tech-heavy role (Big Tech)"

    Shift 1 extra hour each day from STAR review to LeetCode. Day 5 becomes 3 hours of LeetCode, 1 hour of system design.

=== "Laravel/PHP product company"

    Day 5 = drill the Laravel war-room phase end to end. Day 4 mock = have a friend pretend to be a Laravel tech lead. Day 3 = re-drill your strongest project.

=== "AI/ML role"

    Day 5 = drill the AI/ML war-room phase. Day 4 mock = whiteboard a RAG system. Day 3 = re-read papers/blogs on the company's published ML work.

---

## Common traps

| Trap | Why it kills | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| 12-hour cram on Day 5 | Burnout by Day 2, foggy on Day 0 | 2–4 hours max per day. Hard cap. |
| Skipping company research | Sounds like spray-and-pray applicant | Day 7 is non-negotiable |
| No mocks | First time talking under pressure = the actual interview | Day 4 mock or it didn't happen |
| Learning new tech | New material under stress doesn't stick, takes mental space | Sharpen what you have, don't add |
| All-nighter on Day 1 | Cortisol wrecks recall | Sleep by 10 PM, every day this week |
| No calendar | Guilt loop, "studying" all day, retaining nothing | Block sessions in your phone calendar |
