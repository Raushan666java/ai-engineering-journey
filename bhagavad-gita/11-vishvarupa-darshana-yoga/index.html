Chapter 11: Vishvarupa Darshana Yoga — The Whole Universe as One Body

"When the one becomes visible as the all, the small self trembles — and in that trembling, the great self is born." — The Osho Way

Arjuna has been listening for ten chapters. He has heard about the unborn, the beginningless, the source of all. His mind is convinced, his heart is in love — but there is still a distance between him and the truth. He has understood with words; he has not yet seen. And so, at the peak of his devotion, he asks for the impossible: show me your divine form. Krishna grants the request as only a teacher in love could — he gives Arjuna a divine eye, and the whole universe opens like a flower inside a single body. This is the most photographed moment in the Gita: the vishvarupa, the cosmic form, with a thousand mouths and a thousand suns.

Osho reads this chapter as the moment the Gita stops being philosophy and becomes experience. All the earlier chapters were maps; this chapter is the terrain. Arjuna does not understand the cosmic form — no one can understand it — he sees it, and the seeing destroys his small self. Watch what happens in his speech: the warrior who argued with Krishna in chapter one now cannot even speak without trembling. His arguments are burned; his plans are burned; his fears are burned. And in that burning, something new is born: the instrument. "You have already slain them all," Krishna says. "Be a mere instrument." The death of the doer is the birth of the devotee.

The chapter moves through four great waves. First the granting of the vision and its dazzling description — a thousand suns, endless mouths, the three worlds trembling. Then Arjuna's terror and his stammering questions: who are you, fierce of form? Then Krishna's answer — the most famous lines in the Gita: I am Time, the destroyer of worlds; the warriors are already dead; be an instrument. And finally the return: the form withdraws, the gentle Krishna returns, and the last teaching is spoken — this vision cannot be earned by Vedas, gifts or austerities; it comes only through single-minded devotion.

Osho's favourite paradox lives in this chapter. The vision that terrified Arjuna is the same vision that liberated him. The cosmic form is not a threat; it is the truth of who you are. When the small self sees the whole, it trembles — but the trembling is only the ego's farewell. Behind the trembling, the witness stands. This is the deepest message of Vishvarupa Darshana Yoga: you are not the drop that fears the ocean; you are the ocean dreaming it is a drop. The chapter will break the dream — and the breaking will be the most beautiful thing that ever happens to you.

Vishvarupa Darshana Yoga at a Glance​

The scene and the speakers​

The scene is still the battlefield of Kurukshetra, the chariot still stands between the armies, but the setting has grown cosmic. The speakers are three: Krishna, who grants the vision; Arjuna, who receives it with wonder and terror; and Sanjaya, who for a moment takes over the narration to describe what even the divine eye sees — the universe resting in one body. The chapter is unique in the Gita because its centre is not a teaching but a sight. Sanjaya, reporting to the blind Dhritarashtra, becomes the witness of a vision that no blind man and no seer could ever exhaust. The dialogue pauses; the whole of creation takes the stage.

The Osho lens​

Osho reads this chapter as the Gita's great experiment with the limits of mind. The mind knows by dividing — it is a knife. The cosmic form is undivided — it is the whole. Therefore the mind cannot grasp it; only the divine eye can see it, and the divine eye is a gift, not an achievement. Osho says: the whole is not a bigger object; it is the absence of the small self. That is why Arjuna trembles — not because the form is dangerous but because the container of his identity is cracking. And that is why Krishna's first word after the vision is compassion: be not afraid. The vision destroys only the illusion; what remains is more real than anything the world ever called real.

The structure of the chapter​

This chapter of 55 shlokas moves in four movements:

  1. Movement 1: 11.1–11.14 — The request and the granting. Arjuna asks to see the divine form; Krishna grants the divine eye; Sanjaya describes the cosmic form — countless mouths, divine weapons, a thousand suns, the universe resting in one body.
  2. Movement 2: 11.15–11.31 — The seeing and the terror. Arjuna sees the hosts of gods and heroes, the terrible mouths devouring the warriors like moths into a flame, and cries out: who are you, fierce of form? Have mercy.
  3. Movement 3: 11.32–11.35 — The revelation of Time. Krishna declares: I am Time, the world-destroyer; the warriors are already slain; stand up, win fame, be a mere instrument.
  4. Movement 4: 11.36–11.55 — The praise, the return, and the way. Arjuna's great hymn of surrender, his plea for the gentle form, Krishna's withdrawal of the vision, and the final key: by single-minded devotion alone is the divine known, seen and entered.

The three-framework note​

All three paths of the Gita converge on this chapter's final verse. Karma Yoga appears as "matkarmakrit" — he who acts for the divine; Bhakti Yoga appears as "madbhaktah" and "ananya bhakti" — single-minded devotion, the only door to the vision (11.54); Jnana Yoga appears in the seeing itself — the knowledge that transforms, not information. But the chapter's centre of gravity is bhakti: the vision is granted to the beloved, not to the scholar. Notice also the gunas: the cosmic form is described in sattvic terms of splendour and in rajasic terms of terror — the whole contains all three qualities. The mature devotee bows to all of them.

The Complete Text — All 55 Shlokas​

Shloka 11.1 — My Delusion Is Gone​

अर्जुन उवाच | मदनुग्रहाय परमं गुह्यमध्यात्मसंज्ञितम् | यत्त्वयोक्तं वचस्तेन मोहोऽयं विगतो मम

Transliteration: arjuna uvāca . madanugrahāya paramaṃ guhyamadhyātmasaṃjñitam . yattvayoktaṃ vacastena moho.ayaṃ vigato mama

Translation: Arjuna said: By that word of the highest secret concerning the Self, which you have spoken for my blessing, this delusion of mine is gone.

Osho Darshan: Arjuna reports the first fruit of ten chapters: the delusion is gone. Osho says this is the moment every seeker recognises — the fog lifts, and for the first time the road is visible. But notice what Arjuna does next. A man whose delusion is truly gone would rest; Arjuna's disappearance of doubt only makes him ask for more. The Gita's psychology is precise: clarity is not the end of the journey; it is the platform from which the deepest hunger begins. First the head clears, then the heart demands to see.

Shloka 11.2 — Origin and Destruction Heard in Detail​

भवाप्ययौ हि भूतानां श्रुतौ विस्तरशो मया | त्वत्तः कमलपत्राक्ष माहात्म्यमपि चाव्ययम्

Transliteration: bhavāpyayau hi bhūtānāṃ śrutau vistaraśo mayā . tvattaḥ kamalapatrākṣa māhātmyamapi cāvyayam

Translation: The origin and the dissolution of beings, O lotus-eyed one, and your inexhaustible greatness, I have heard from you in detail.

Osho Darshan: The disciple's gratitude is honest: he has heard, and the hearing was real. Osho says hearing is the first door of the temple — shravana, the listening that is not mere noise but reception. Arjuna has listened so deeply that the words have begun to change his chemistry. And yet the ear is still a window, not a wall removed. The next sentence will ask for the door itself. The Gita never belittles what the disciple has; it always invites him to the more that stands before him.

Shloka 11.3 — I Wish to See Your Divine Form​

एवमेतद्यथात्थ त्वमात्मानं परमेश्वर | द्रष्टुमिच्छामि ते रूपमैश्वरं पुरुषोत्तम

Transliteration: evametadyathāttha tvamātmānaṃ parameśvara . draṣṭumicchāmi te rūpamaiśvaraṃ puruṣottama

Translation: As you have described yourself, O Supreme Lord, so it is; but I wish to see your sovereign divine form, O Supreme Person.

Osho Darshan: "It is so — but let me see." Osho says this is the most revolutionary sentence in the whole Gita: the disciple dares to ask for more than words. All religions are built on "it is so"; the Gita is built on "let me see." Arjuna is not satisfied with a beautiful description of the mountain; he wants his feet on the mountain. This restlessness is the very life of the spiritual search. The man who never asks to see remains a believer forever; the man who asks becomes a seer.

Shloka 11.4 — Show Me Your Imperishable Self​

मन्यसे यदि तच्छक्यं मया द्रष्टुमिति प्रभो | योगेश्वर ततो मे त्वं दर्शयात्मानमव्ययम्

Transliteration: manyase yadi tacchakyaṃ mayā draṣṭumiti prabho . yogeśvara tato me tvaṃ darśayātmānamavyayam

Translation: If you think it possible for me to see it, O Lord, O Lord of Yoga, then show me your imperishable Self.

Osho Darshan: The request is wrapped in humility — "if you think it possible." Osho says this is the exact posture of the seeker: total trust and total asking at the same time. Arjuna does not demand the vision; he offers himself to it. He has learned something from ten chapters: the highest cannot be taken by force; it is given. "Yogeshvara" — Lord of Yoga — is the right address: only the master of all joining can join the seer to the whole. The question of worthiness dissolves; the seeker simply asks, with love.

Shloka 11.5 — Behold My Forms by Hundreds and Thousands​

श्रीभगवानुवाच | पश्य मे पार्थ रूपाणि शतशोऽथ सहस्रशः | नानाविधानि दिव्यानि नानावर्णाकृतीनि च

Transliteration: śrībhagavānuvāca . paśya me pārtha rūpāṇi śataśo.atha sahasraśaḥ . nānāvidhāni divyāni nānāvarṇākṛtīni ca

Translation: The Blessed Lord said: Behold, O Partha, my forms by the hundreds and thousands, of different kinds, divine, of various colours and shapes.

Osho Darshan: Krishna does not argue, does not negotiate; he simply opens the door: pasya — see. Osho says this is the whole method of the master: the truth is not explained, it is shown. And the showing is generous beyond arithmetic — hundreds, thousands, colours and shapes beyond the inventory of any eye. The divine does not ration its beauty. The first word of the vision is abundance. Whatever you think the divine is, it is more — and the "more" is not quantity but depth.

Shloka 11.6 — The Gods and the Wonders Never Seen​

पश्यादित्यान्वसून्रुद्रानश्विनौ मरुतस्तथा | बहून्यदृष्टपूर्वाणि पश्याश्चर्याणि भारत

Transliteration: paśyādityānvasūnrudrānaśvinau marutastathā . bahūnyadṛṣṭapūrvāṇi paśyāścaryāṇi bhārata

Translation: Behold the Adityas, the Vasus, the Rudras, the two Ashvins and the Maruts; behold many wonders never seen before, O Bharata.

Osho Darshan: The catalogue of heaven pours out — the divine orders of beings, all the powers the mind has ever worshipped, gathered in one glance. And then the phrase that matters: wonders never seen before. Osho says the real wonder of the vision is not the gods but the seeing — the eye that suddenly sees without its old habits. You have looked at your own hand ten thousand times; the day you truly see it, it will be the first time. The vision is not a new object; it is a new eye.

Shloka 11.7 — The Whole Universe in One Body​

इहैकस्थं जगत्कृत्स्नं पश्याद्य सचराचरम् | मम देहे गुडाकेश यच्चान्यद् द्रष्टुमिच्छसि

Transliteration: ihaikasthaṃ jagatkṛtsnaṃ paśyādya sacarācaram . mama dehe guḍākeśa yaccānyad draṣṭumicchasi

Translation: Now behold, O Gudakesha, in this my body, the whole universe centred in one — the moving and the unmoving — and whatever else you wish to see.

Osho Darshan: "Whatever else you wish to see" — the divine offers not only its own inventory but the seer's entire longing. Osho says this is the miracle of the vision: the whole universe — moving and unmoving, mountains and minds, past and future — is seen in one place, in one body. And that one place is the body of the teacher, which is to say: the body of existence. The universe is not many things; it is one thing wearing many names. The seeing of this single fact is the vision.

Shloka 11.8 — I Give You the Divine Eye​

न तु मां शक्यसे द्रष्टुमनेनैव स्वचक्षुषा | दिव्यं ददामि ते चक्षुः पश्य मे योगमैश्वरम्

Transliteration: na tu māṃ śakyase draṣṭumanenaiva svacakṣuṣā . divyaṃ dadāmi te cakṣuḥ paśya me yogamaiśvaram

Translation: But you are not able to behold me with these your own eyes; I give you the divine eye — behold my lordly yoga.

Osho Darshan: The fleshly eye is a camera for surfaces; the whole has no surface to photograph. Osho says the divine eye is not an extra organ — it is the old eye emptied of its projections, a mirror that has stopped claiming to be the picture. And note the sequence: first the divine gives itself as the vision, then as the eye. In the world, the instrument comes before the object; in grace, the object comes first and fashions the instrument. The whole has always been seeing itself; the divine eye is the whole waking up inside you.

Shloka 11.9 — The Great Lord of Yoga Shows the Form​

सञ्जय उवाच | एवमुक्त्वा ततो राजन्महायोगेश्वरो हरिः | दर्शयामास पार्थाय परमं रूपमैश्वरम्

Transliteration: sañjaya uvāca . evamuktvā tato rājanmahāyogeśvaro hariḥ . darśayāmāsa pārthāya paramaṃ rūpamaiśvaram

Translation: Sanjaya said: Having spoken thus, O king, the great Lord of Yoga, Hari, showed to Arjuna his supreme sovereign form.

Osho Darshan: The narration hands over to Sanjaya — and Osho says this is the moment the Gita turns into a play: the teacher speaks, and the whole of creation walks onto the stage. "The great Lord of Yoga" — mahayogeshvara — the master of all joining: he joins the seer to the seen, the part to the whole. What he shows is not a second Krishna; it is the first and only reality behind all the Krishnas. Every form that will ever appear in this description is that one reality, wearing itself.

Shloka 11.10 — Countless Mouths and Eyes​

अनेकवक्त्रनयनमनेकाद्भुतदर्शनम् | अनेकदिव्याभरणं दिव्यानेकोद्यतायुधम्

Transliteration: anekavaktranayanamanekādbhutadarśanam . anekadivyābharaṇaṃ divyānekodyatāyudham

Translation: With numerous mouths and eyes, with numerous wonderful sights, with numerous divine ornaments, with numerous divine weapons uplifted — such a form he showed.

Osho Darshan: Mouths and eyes beyond counting, ornaments and weapons raised — the vision opens in its full paradox. Osho says the cosmic form is the universe in its entirety: it sees with every eye, speaks with every mouth, and its weapons are already lifted in the dance of existence. Nothing in the universe is outside this body. The devotee who can see this much has begun to lose his fear of the world — for how can you fear the world when you have seen that it is one body, one beloved?

Shloka 11.11 — Divine Garlands, Infinite Faces​

दिव्यमाल्याम्बरधरं दिव्यगन्धानुलेपनम् | सर्वाश्चर्यमयं देवमनन्तं विश्वतोमुखम्

Transliteration: divyamālyāmbaradharaṃ divyagandhānulepanam . sarvāścaryamayaṃ devamanantaṃ viśvatomukham

Translation: Wearing divine garlands and apparel, anointed with divine unguents, the all-wonderful, resplendent Being, endless, with faces on every side.

Osho Darshan: "Faces on every side" — the most beautiful phrase of the vision. Osho says the whole looks at you from every direction; there is no back of the divine, no blind spot, no exit from the beloved's gaze. The universe is not a wall facing you; it is a presence surrounding you. When you have seen even one face of the divine in one creature, you have touched a face of the endless. Every encounter in life is a meeting with one side of the all-faced.

Shloka 11.12 — A Thousand Suns Rising Together​

दिवि सूर्यसहस्रस्य भवेद्युगपदुत्थिता | यदि भाः सदृशी सा स्याद्भासस्तस्य महात्मनः

Transliteration: divi sūryasahasrasya bhavedyugapadutthitā . yadi bhāḥ sadṛśī sā syādbhāsastasya mahātmanaḥ

Translation: If the splendour of a thousand suns were to blaze out at once in the sky, that would be like the splendour of that mighty Being.

Osho Darshan: The most famous image of the Gita: a thousand suns rising simultaneously. Osho says the image is not exaggeration but humility — the Sanskrit imagination reaching for the brightest thing it knows and confessing it is still a candle before that light. The divine is not the biggest of things; it is the light in which every thing — including every sun — appears. To see it is to be flooded so completely that the seer, for a moment, forgets to be a separate someone.

Shloka 11.13 — The Whole Universe Resting in One​

तत्रैकस्थं जगत्कृत्स्नं प्रविभक्तमनेकधा | अपश्यद्देवदेवस्य शरीरे पाण्डवस्तदा

Transliteration: tatraikasthaṃ jagatkṛtsnaṃ pravibhaktamanekadhā . apaśyaddevadevasya śarīre pāṇḍavastadā

Translation: There, in the body of the God of gods, Arjuna then saw the whole universe resting in one, divided into its many groups.

Osho Darshan: The vision's content in one line: the whole universe, divided into all its groups, resting in one body. Osho says "resting in one" is the key — the many does not disappear in the vision; it appears as the one. The world is not an illusion to be destroyed; it is the truth to be seen from the other side. Arjuna sees the gods, the sages, the serpents, the armies — all of them, for the first time, as the limbs of one body. That is the vision, and that is the only cure for all his fears: you cannot fear your own body's limbs.

Shloka 11.14 — Hair Standing on End​

ततः स विस्मयाविष्टो हृष्टरोमा धनञ्जयः | प्रणम्य शिरसा देवं कृताञ्जलिरभाषत

Transliteration: tataḥ sa vismayāviṣṭo hṛṣṭaromā dhanañjayaḥ . praṇamya śirasā devaṃ kṛtāñjalirabhāṣata

Translation: Then Arjuna, filled with wonder, his hair standing on end, bowed his head to the God and spoke with joined palms.

Osho Darshan: The body answers before the tongue: hair standing on end, hands joining themselves. Osho says wonder — vismaya — is the gateway between the mind and the whole: in wonder, the thinking stops and the being opens. Arjuna has been a thinker for ten chapters; now he is a worshipper. The joined palms are the geometry of surrender — two separate hands becoming one. When the many hands of the universe meet in the gesture of the one, the vision has done its work.

Shloka 11.15 — All Gods, Brahma, Sages and Serpents​

अर्जुन उवाच | पश्यामि देवांस्तव देव देहे सर्वांस्तथा भूतविशेषसङ्घान् | ब्रह्माणमीशं कमलासनस्थ- मृषींश्च सर्वानुरगांश्च दिव्यान्

Transliteration: arjuna uvāca . paśyāmi devāṃstava deva dehe sarvāṃstathā bhūtaviśeṣasaṅghān . brahmāṇamīśaṃ kamalāsanasthaṃ ṛṣīṃśca sarvānuragāṃśca divyān

Translation: Arjuna said: I see all the gods, O God, in your body, and hosts of various classes of beings; Brahma the Lord seated on the lotus, all the sages and the celestial serpents.

Osho Darshan: Arjuna begins to report what he sees — and the report reads like a confession of the new geography: everything is inside. Brahma himself, the creator of the worlds, sits within the body. Osho says this is the vision's deepest gift: the creator of the universe is a limb of the whole, and so is every creature, and so are you. There is no outside in the divine. When a man sees this, his ambition and his fear both find their true proportion — nothing is gained by grasping, nothing is lost by letting go.

Shloka 11.16 — Neither End, Middle, nor Beginning​

अनेकबाहूदरवक्त्रनेत्रं पश्यामि त्वां सर्वतोऽनन्तरूपम् | नान्तं न मध्यं न पुनस्तवादिं पश्यामि विश्वेश्वर विश्वरूप

Transliteration: anekabāhūdaravaktranetraṃ paśyāmi tvāṃ sarvato.anantarūpam . nāntaṃ na madhyaṃ na punastavādiṃ paśyāmi viśveśvara viśvarūpa

Translation: I see you, of boundless form on every side, with many arms, stomachs, mouths and eyes; I see neither your end, nor your middle, nor your beginning, O Lord of the universe, O cosmic form.

Osho Darshan: The eye searches for the edges and finds none — the first honest report of the infinite. Osho says everything in the world is born, lives and dies; the whole is not a bigger thing but the disappearance of those three verbs. The vision is not of an object without borders but of the seer's own limitlessness. Arjuna's words falter because language itself is made of edges. The formless is not far away from the form; it is the form's truth — and the seer who looks long enough finds that his own edges were the mirage.

Shloka 11.17 — Diadem, Mace, Discus, Mass of Radiance​

किरीटिनं गदिनं चक्रिणं च तेजोराशिं सर्वतो दीप्तिमन्तम् | पश्यामि त्वां दुर्निरीक्ष्यं समन्ताद् दीप्तानलार्कद्युतिमप्रमेयम्

Transliteration: kirīṭinaṃ gadinaṃ cakriṇaṃ ca tejorāśiṃ sarvato dīptimantam . paśyāmi tvāṃ durnirīkṣyaṃ samantād dīptānalārkadyutimaprameyam

Translation: I see you with the diadem, the mace and the discus, a mass of radiance shining everywhere, very hard to look at, blazing all round like burning fire and the sun, and immeasurable.

Osho Darshan: "Hard to look at" — the honest admission that the truth can be too bright for the eye that is still a little ego. Osho says the divine's light does not intend to blind; the eye itself must grow. First comes the unbearable seeing, then the gradual widening. The devotee does not demand comfort from the divine; he asks only for the strength to keep looking. Every glimpse of truth that is too bright today is preparing the eye for a fuller vision tomorrow.

Shloka 11.18 — The Imperishable, the Treasure-House of the Universe​

त्वमक्षरं परमं वेदितव्यं त्वमस्य विश्वस्य परं निधानम् | त्वमव्ययः शाश्वतधर्मगोप्ता सनातनस्त्वं पुरुषो मतो मे

Transliteration: tvamakṣaraṃ paramaṃ veditavyaṃ tvamasya viśvasya paraṃ nidhānam . tvamavyayaḥ śāśvatadharmagoptā sanātanastvaṃ puruṣo mato me

Translation: You are the imperishable, the supreme, worthy to be known; you are the great treasure-house of this universe; you are the imperishable protector of the eternal dharma; you are the ancient Person, so I deem.

Osho Darshan: The seer's theology pours out — but Osho says the real content is not the names but the movement: the seeing is now speaking. Arjuna has crossed from report to recognition. "The treasure-house of this universe" — everything the universe will ever need is already stored in the whole. And "the protector of the eternal dharma" — the order of existence is not a human invention; it is guarded by the ancient one. The devotee who sees this no longer fights existence; he serves it, like a limb serving the body.

Shloka 11.19 — Without Beginning, Middle or End​

अनादिमध्यान्तमनन्तवीर्य- मनन्तबाहुं शशिसूर्यनेत्रम् | पश्यामि त्वां दीप्तहुताशवक्त्रं स्वतेजसा विश्वमिदं तपन्तम्

Transliteration: anādimadhyāntamanantavīryam anantabāhuṃ śaśisūryanetram . paśyāmi tvāṃ dīptahutāśavaktraṃ svatejasā viśvamidaṃ tapantam

Translation: I see you without beginning, middle or end, infinite in power, of endless arms, the sun and the moon your eyes, burning fire your mouth, heating the whole universe with your radiance.

Osho Darshan: Sun and moon as the two eyes — the vision of the divine seeing through the whole of time and light. Osho says this is the perfect icon of non-duality: the pair of opposites — sun and moon, day and night, birth and death — become the two eyes of one face. When you can see the opposites as the eyes of the same being, your anxiety about choosing between them dissolves. The fire of the mouth is the same fire that consumes and cooks; the devotee does not choose between warmth and destruction — he sees one flame.

Shloka 11.20 — The Three Worlds Tremble​

द्यावापृथिव्योरिदमन्तरं हि व्याप्तं त्वयैकेन दिशश्च सर्वाः | दृष्ट्वाद्भुतं रूपमुग्रं तवेदं लोकत्रयं प्रव्यथितं महात्मन्

Transliteration: dyāvāpṛthivyoridamantaraṃ hi vyāptaṃ tvayaikena diśaśca sarvāḥ . dṛṣṭvādbhutaṃ rūpamugraṃ tavedaṃ lokatrayaṃ pravyathitaṃ mahātman

Translation: This space between the earth and heaven, and all the directions, are filled by you alone; having seen this wonderful and terrible form of yours, the three worlds tremble with fear, O great-souled Being.

Osho Darshan: The world trembles — and Osho says the trembling is the world's first honesty. Every creature senses the vastness beneath its small arrangements, and the sensing shakes it. But note the address: "O great-souled Being" — even in terror, Arjuna's heart recognises the beloved. The fear of the whole is the door to the love of the whole; the child afraid of the dark ocean is one step from the swimmer who has surrendered to it. Trembling is not the end — it is the sign that the small has met the great.

Shloka 11.21 — The Hosts of Gods Enter You​

अमी हि त्वां सुरसङ्घा विशन्ति केचिद्भीताः प्राञ्जलयो गृणन्ति | स्वस्तीत्युक्त्वा महर्षिसिद्धसङ्घाः स्तुवन्ति त्वां स्तुतिभिः पुष्कलाभिः

Transliteration: amī hi tvāṃ surasaṅghā viśanti kecidbhītāḥ prāñjalayo gṛṇanti . svastītyuktvā maharṣisiddhasaṅghāḥ stuvanti tvāṃ stutibhiḥ puṣkalābhiḥ

Translation: Into you enter these hosts of gods; some, in fear, with joined palms, extol you; crying "may it be well," the bands of great sages and perfected ones praise you with abundant hymns.

Osho Darshan: Even the gods enter the body — the whole is not one more being among beings, it is the home of all beings. Osho says the sight reverses the cosmology of the fearful mind: nothing is falling out of the universe; everything is entering it. And the sages cry "swasti" — may it be well — which is the deepest prayer: not "give me" but "let all be well." The devotee who prays for the welfare of the whole has understood the vision better than the one who prays for his own safety.

Shloka 11.22 — All Orders of Beings Gaze in Wonder​

रुद्रादित्या वसवो ये च साध्या विश्वेऽश्विनौ मरुतश्चोष्मपाश्च | गन्धर्वयक्षासुरसिद्धसङ्घा वीक्षन्ते त्वां विस्मिताश्चैव सर्वे

Transliteration: rudrādityā vasavo ye ca sādhyā viśve.aśvinau marutaścoṣmapāśca . gandharvayakṣāsurasiddhasaṅghā vīkṣante tvāṃ vismitāścaiva sarve

Translation: The Rudras, Adityas, Vasus, Sadhyas, Vishvedevas, the two Ashvins, Maruts and the manes, and the hosts of Gandharvas, Yakshas, Asuras and Siddhas — all gaze at you in great wonder.

Osho Darshan: The census of heaven: every order of beings, every name the mythology has ever collected, standing as one audience. Osho says the list is the mind's last attempt to count — and the counting breaks. Wonder — vismaya — is the one response common to gods and demons and sages. In the presence of the whole, every hierarchy collapses into one equality: all are amazed. The vision does not ask who you are; it asks only whether you can wonder.

Shloka 11.23 — The Worlds Are Terrified, and So Am I​

रूपं महत्ते बहुवक्त्रनेत्रं महाबाहो बहुबाहूरुपादम् | बहूदरं बहुदंष्ट्राकरालं दृष्ट्वा लोकाः प्रव्यथितास्तथाहम्

Transliteration: rūpaṃ mahatte bahuvaktranetraṃ mahābāho bahubāhūrupādam . bahūdaraṃ bahudaṃṣṭrākarālaṃ dṛṣṭvā lokāḥ pravyathitāstathāham

Translation: Having seen your immeasurable form with many mouths and eyes, O mighty-armed, with many arms, thighs and feet, many stomachs and fearful with many teeth — the worlds are terrified, and so am I.

Osho Darshan: The honesty deepens: "so am I." Osho says the seeker's confession of fear is the beginning of his freedom — a frightened man who pretends to be brave is doubly enslaved. Arjuna does not hide his terror from the teacher; he offers it. And the terror itself is instructive: the whole is too much for the separate self, and the self knows it. The fear is not the truth; it is the smoke of the ego burning. Wait — the vision will burn the smoke too.

Shloka 11.24 — No Courage, No Peace​

नभःस्पृशं दीप्तमनेकवर्णं व्यात्ताननं दीप्तविशालनेत्रम् | दृष्ट्वा हि त्वां प्रव्यथितान्तरात्मा धृतिं न विन्दामि शमं च विष्णो

Transliteration: nabhaḥspṛśaṃ dīptamanekavarṇaṃ vyāttānanaṃ dīptaviśālanetram . dṛṣṭvā hi tvāṃ pravyathitāntarātmā dhṛtiṃ na vindāmi śamaṃ ca viṣṇo

Translation: Seeing you touching the sky, shining in many colours, mouths wide open, with large fiery eyes, my inner self is terrified; I find neither courage nor peace, O Vishnu.

Osho Darshan: "I find neither courage nor peace" — the old supports are gone. Osho says this is the precise geography of transformation: first the supports of the ego disappear, and the ego calls it a crisis; later they are seen to have been unnecessary. Arjuna's courage was built on knowing who he was; the vision has dissolved the knower. But the dissolution is the gift. When courage is gone, trust can begin; when peace cannot be found, the one who seeks peace must be found.

Shloka 11.25 — Have Mercy, O Lord of Gods​

दंष्ट्राकरालानि च ते मुखानि दृष्ट्वैव कालानलसन्निभानि | दिशो न जाने न लभे च शर्म प्रसीद देवेश जगन्निवास

Transliteration: daṃṣṭrākarālāni ca te mukhāni dṛṣṭvaiva kālānalasannibhāni . diśo na jāne na labhe ca śarma prasīda deveśa jagannivāsa

Translation: Having seen your mouths, fearful with teeth blazing like the fires of cosmic dissolution, I know not the directions, nor do I find peace; have mercy, O Lord of gods, O abode of the universe.

Osho Darshan: The word that saves the chapter: prasida — have mercy. Osho says the cry for grace is the highest prayer there is, because it confesses that effort has reached its end. Arjuna has lost his directions — he knows not east from west — and in that losing he has found the one direction that matters: toward the beloved. The mouths of dissolution are terrible only to the separate; to the surrendered they are the gates of the only home. Mercy is not earned; it is asked.

Shloka 11.26 — The Sons of Dhritarashtra and the Heroes​

अमी च त्वां धृतराष्ट्रस्य पुत्राः सर्वे सहैवावनिपालसङ्घैः | भीष्मो द्रोणः सूतपुत्रस्तथासौ सहास्मदीयैरपि योधमुख्यैः

Transliteration: amī ca tvāṃ dhṛtarāṣṭrasya putrāḥ sarve sahaivāvanipālasaṅghaiḥ . bhīṣmo droṇaḥ sūtaputrastathāsau sahāsmadīyairapi yodhamukhyaiḥ

Translation: And these sons of Dhritarashtra, with the hosts of kings, Bhishma, Drona and Karna, with the chief warriors of our side too, rush into you.

Osho Darshan: The vision turns personal — the enemies Arjuna feared to kill are now seen rushing into the divine body. Osho says this is the moment the teaching becomes experiential: the people of the battlefield, the very names that caused chapter one's collapse, appear as swallows entering the sky. What Arjuna was afraid to do, the whole is doing before his eyes. The insight of the vision: the killing was never in his hands. This is not fatalism; it is the end of the doer's burden.

Shloka 11.27 — Rushing into the Terrible Mouths​

वक्त्राणि ते त्वरमाणा विशन्ति दंष्ट्राकरालानि भयानकानि | केचिद्विलग्ना दशनान्तरेषु सन्दृश्यन्ते चूर्णितैरुत्तमाङ्गैः

Transliteration: vaktrāṇi te tvaramāṇā viśanti daṃṣṭrākarālāni bhayānakāni . kecidvilagnā daśanāntareṣu sandṛśyante cūrṇitairuttamāṅgaiḥ

Translation: Some, hurrying, enter your terrible, fearful-toothed mouths; some are seen sticking between the teeth, their heads crushed to powder.

Osho Darshan: The vision turns grisly — heads crushed between teeth. Osho says the Gita does not flinch from the terror of existence, and the devotee must not either. Birth, death, dissolution are not accidents; they are the teeth of the whole. But the deeper teaching is in Arjuna's eyes: he watches his dreaded enemies die in the vision and finds, to his surprise, that his own hatred has already died with them. What you have seen dissolve in the whole, you can no longer fight as an enemy.

Shloka 11.28 — Rivers Flowing to the Ocean​

यथा नदीनां बहवोऽम्बुवेगाः समुद्रमेवाभिमुखा द्रवन्ति | तथा तवामी नरलोकवीरा विशन्ति वक्त्राण्यभिविज्वलन्ति

Transliteration: yathā nadīnāṃ bahavo.ambuvegāḥ samudramevābhimukhā dravanti . tathā tavāmī naralokavīrā viśanti vaktrāṇyabhivijvalanti

Translation: Just as many torrents of rivers flow toward the ocean, even so these heroes of the world of men enter your flaming mouths.

Osho Darshan: The most consoling image of the chapter: the warriors are rivers, the divine is the ocean, and their rushing is not destruction but return. Osho says this image is the whole of the Gita in one breath — the drop that fears losing itself is, in fact, coming home. The mouth of the divine is the sea into which every river falls. What looked like death in 11.27 is seen as return in 11.28. The difference is the eye of the seer: the same event, seen from fear or seen from love.

Shloka 11.29 — Moths into the Flame​

यथा प्रदीप्तं ज्वलनं पतङ्गा विशन्ति नाशाय समृद्धवेगाः | तथैव नाशाय विशन्ति लोकास्- तवापि वक्त्राणि समृद्धवेगाः

Transliteration: yathā pradīptaṃ jvalanaṃ pataṅgā viśanti nāśāya samṛddhavegāḥ . tathaiva nāśāya viśanti lokāsa- tavāpi vaktrāṇi samṛddhavegāḥ

Translation: As moths, with quickened speed, enter a blazing fire for their own destruction, so do these creatures, with quickened speed, enter your mouths for their own destruction.

Osho Darshan: Moths into the flame — the image turns darker, and Osho says the dark image must stand beside the bright one. The river image showed grace; the moth image shows the price of ignorance. The same return that is rest for the river is destruction for the moth that fights the flame. The difference is surrender: the river does not resist the ocean; the moth does not understand the fire. The devotee's work is to become a river — to flow, without resistance, into the burning love that awaits everything.

Shloka 11.30 — Licking and Devouring the Worlds​

लेलिह्यसे ग्रसमानः समन्ताल्- लोकान्समग्रान्वदनैर्ज्वलद्भिः | तेजोभिरापूर्य जगत्समग्रं भासस्तवोग्राः प्रतपन्ति विष्णो

Transliteration: lelihyase grasamānaḥ samantāl- lokānsamagrānvadanairjvaladbhiḥ . tejobhirāpūrya jagatsamagraṃ bhāsastavogrāḥ pratapanti viṣṇo

Translation: You lick up, devouring all the worlds on every side with your flaming mouths; your fierce rays, filling the whole world with radiance, are burning, O Vishnu.

Osho Darshan: The vision's terror reaches its peak — the divine as devourer, licking up the worlds. Osho says the Gita refuses the polite God. The same mouth that speaks the Upanishads swallows the universes; the same flame warms the child and consumes the forest. Maturity is the capacity to hold both faces without splitting the divine in two. Vishnu — the pervader — is the name Arjuna cries, and the pervader pervades the devouring too. When you can say "thou" to the fire that burns you, you have become a devotee indeed.

Shloka 11.31 — Who Are You, Fierce of Form?​

आख्याहि मे को भवानुग्ररूपो नमोऽस्तु ते देववर प्रसीद | विज्ञातुमिच्छामि भवन्तमाद्यं न हि प्रजानामि तव प्रवृत्तिम्

Transliteration: ākhyāhi me ko bhavānugrarūpo namo.astu te devavara prasīda . vijñātumicchāmi bhavantamādyaṃ na hi prajānāmi tava pravṛttim

Translation: Tell me who you are, so fierce of form; salutations to you, O best of gods; have mercy. I desire to know you, the original Being; I do not know your working.

Osho Darshan: The question returns to its simplest form: who are you? Osho says the whole spiritual journey can be told as this one question — asked at the beginning with arrogance, asked here with bowed head. The difference is the posture. Arjuna's "I do not know your working" is the confession that makes knowledge possible: the teacher can only teach the empty vessel. The student who still knows everything is unreachable; the student who knows he does not know has already begun to know.

Shloka 11.32 — I Am Time, the World-Destroyer​

श्रीभगवानुवाच | कालोऽस्मि लोकक्षयकृत्प्रवृद्धो लोकान्समाहर्तुमिह प्रवृत्तः | ऋतेऽपि त्वां न भविष्यन्ति सर्वे येऽवस्थिताः प्रत्यनीकेषु योधाः

Transliteration: śrībhagavānuvāca . kālo.asmi lokakṣayakṛtpravṛddho lokānsamāhartumiha pravṛttaḥ . ṛte.api tvāṃ na bhaviṣyanti sarve ye.avasthitāḥ pratyanīkeṣu yodhāḥ

Translation: The Blessed Lord said: I am the full-grown Time, the destroyer of worlds, now engaged in destroying them; even without you, none of the warriors arrayed in the hostile armies shall live.

Osho Darshan: The answer that shakes the world: I am Time. Osho says this is the Gita's most radical utterance — the divine does not fight against time; the divine is time, is the devouring current of existence itself. And the second sentence changes Arjuna's whole war: the warriors are already dead. The doer has been dismissed. Every anxiety about killing, every fear of sin, every hesitation collapses before this single fact: your hand is not the cause. The universe moves by its own current; you are invited to swim in it, not to push it.

Shloka 11.33 — Stand Up; Be a Mere Instrument​

तस्मात्त्वमुत्तिष्ठ यशो लभस्व जित्वा शत्रून् भुङ्क्ष्व राज्यं समृद्धम् | मयैवैते निहताः पूर्वमेव निमित्तमात्रं भव सव्यसाचिन्

Transliteration: tasmāttvamuttiṣṭha yaśo labhasva jitvā śatrūn bhuṅkṣva rājyaṃ samṛddham . mayaivaite nihatāḥ pūrvameva nimittamātraṃ bhava savyasācin

Translation: Therefore, stand up and obtain fame; conquer your enemies and enjoy the prosperous kingdom. By me alone have they already been slain; be a mere instrument, O Savyasachin.

Osho Darshan: "Nimittamatram" — a mere instrument. Osho says these are the two most liberating words in the Gita. The flute does not worry about the song; the hammer does not worry about the building. The instrument is not passive — the flute must be perfectly hollow, the hammer must strike true. Instrumentality is not laziness; it is the highest activity without the actor. Stand up, conquer, enjoy — all the verbs remain, only the doer is gone. That is karma yoga in a single breath: action in full, ownership in none.

Shloka 11.34 — They Are Already Slain; Do Not Fear​

द्रोणं च भीष्मं च जयद्रथं च कर्णं तथान्यानपि योधवीरान् | मया हतांस्त्वं जहि मा व्यथिष्ठा युध्यस्व जेतासि रणे सपत्नान्

Transliteration: droṇaṃ ca bhīṣmaṃ ca jayadrathaṃ ca karṇaṃ tathānyānapi yodhavīrān . mayā hatāṃstvaṃ jahi mavyathiṣṭhā yudhyasva jetāsi raṇe sapatnān

Translation: Drona, Bhishma, Jayadratha, Karna and the other brave warriors — they are already slain by me; kill them, be not distressed; fight, and you shall conquer your enemies in battle.

Osho Darshan: The teacher names the very men whose names broke Arjuna in chapter one — and pronounces them already slain. Osho says this is the completion of the circle: what began as the deepest wound ends as the deepest freedom. The beloved teacher, the noble grandfather, the peerless rival — the vision has dissolved them into the current of time. Arjuna is told to fight without the guilt of the killer and without the pride of the victor. The instrument wins; the doer is nowhere. That is the only conquest that leaves no scars.

Shloka 11.35 — Trembling, He Bows Again​

सञ्जय उवाच | एतच्छ्रुत्वा वचनं केशवस्य कृताञ्जलिर्वेपमानः किरीटी | नमस्कृत्वा भूय एवाह कृष्णं सगद्गदं भीतभीतः प्रणम्य

Transliteration: sañjaya uvāca . etacchrutvā vacanaṃ keśavasya kṛtāñjalirvepamānaḥ kirīṭī . namaskṛtvā bhūya evāha kṛṣṇaṃ sagadgadaṃ bhītabhītaḥ praṇamya

Translation: Sanjaya said: Having heard that speech of Kesava, Arjuna, with joined palms and trembling, prostrating himself, overwhelmed with fear, again spoke to Krishna with a choked voice.

Osho Darshan: The great archer stammers. Osho says there is nothing shameful in this stammer — it is the first authentic speech of the soul. The old eloquence of Arjuna, the smooth arguments of chapter one, are gone; what remains is a voice broken by the vision, and a broken voice is the truest instrument for the divine. "Bhita-bhita" — overwhelmed with fear — is the ego's last report before its surrender. After this, the words that come are not arguments but worship.

Shloka 11.36 — The World Delights in Your Praise​

अर्जुन उवाच | स्थाने हृषीकेश तव प्रकीर्त्या जगत्प्रहृष्यत्यनुरज्यते च | रक्षांसि भीतानि दिशो द्रवन्ति सर्वे नमस्यन्ति च सिद्धसङ्घाः

Transliteration: arjuna uvāca . sthāne hṛṣīkeśa tava prakīrtyā jagatprahṛṣyatyanurajyate ca . rakṣāṃsi bhītāni diśo dravanti sarve namasyanti ca siddhasaṅghāḥ

Translation: Arjuna said: It is fitting, O Hrishikesha, that the world delights and rejoices in your praise; the demons fly in fear to all directions, and the hosts of the perfected ones bow to you.

Osho Darshan: The hymn begins with the two faces of the divine's fame: the good rejoice, the demonic flee. Osho says this is not a morality play but a law of consciousness — light attracts and scatters in the same movement. The demon in you flees from the truth; the devotee in you bows to it. The whole of your inner world is in this shloka: part of you is a frightened rakshasa running to the corners, part is a siddha bowing. The vision's work is to strengthen the bower and calm the runner.

Shloka 11.37 — Why Should They Not Bow?​

कस्माच्च ते न नमेरन्महात्मन् गरीयसे ब्रह्मणोऽप्यादिकर्त्रे | अनन्त देवेश जगन्निवास त्वमक्षरं सदसत्तत्परं यत्

Transliteration: kasmācca te na nameranmahātman garīyase brahmaṇo.apyādikartre . ananta deveśa jagannivāsa tvamakṣaraṃ sadasattatparaṃ yat

Translation: Why should they not bow to you, O great Soul, who are greater than all, the primal cause even of Brahma, O infinite Being, O Lord of gods, O abode of the universe? You are the imperishable, the Being, the non-being, and that which is beyond both.

Osho Darshan: Arjuna reaches the final theological summit: the divine is sat and asat — being and non-being — and beyond both. Osho says the mind can name the being and the non-being, but "that which is beyond both" is the silence that naming cannot reach. Here the hymn touches its own limit and goes quiet. The bowing is no longer an act; it has become the natural gravity of the soul. When every category has been crossed, only the bow remains — and the bow is the last and first posture of the devotee.

Shloka 11.38 — The Primal God, the Ancient Person​

त्वमादिदेवः पुरुषः पुराणस्- त्वमस्य विश्वस्य परं निधानम् | वेत्तासि वेद्यं च परं च धाम त्वया ततं विश्वमनन्तरूप

Transliteration: tvamādidevaḥ puruṣaḥ purāṇasa- tvamasya viśvasya paraṃ nidhānam . vettāsi vedyaṃ ca paraṃ ca dhāma tvayā tataṃ viśvamanantarūpa

Translation: You are the primal God, the ancient Person, the supreme refuge of this universe; you are the knower, the knowable, and the supreme abode. By you is this whole universe pervaded, O being of infinite forms.

Osho Darshan: The hymn rises to its peak of recognition: knower, knowable and abode in one word. Osho says the whole triangle of the mind — the one who knows, what is known, and the place of knowing — collapses here into a single "you," and when that happens the mind has done all it can do; what remains is not a concept but a presence. "Pervaded by you" is the last word of the vision's geography: there is no outside. Every cell of the universe is inside the body of the beloved.

Shloka 11.39 — Vayu, Yama, Agni, Varuna; Salutations a Thousand Times​

वायुर्यमोऽग्निर्वरुणः शशाङ्कः प्रजापतिस्त्वं प्रपितामहश्च | नमो नमस्तेऽस्तु सहस्रकृत्वः पुनश्च भूयोऽपि नमो नमस्ते

Transliteration: vāyuryamo.agnirvaruṇaḥ śaśāṅkaḥ prajāpatistvaṃ prapitāmahaśca . namo namaste.astu sahasrakṛtvaḥ punaśca bhūyo.api namo namaste

Translation: You are Vayu, Yama, Agni, Varuna, the moon, the Creator, and the great-grandfather of all. Salutations, salutations to you a thousand times; and again, and yet again, salutations to you.

Osho Darshan: The census of the gods dissolves into worship: every power the mind has ever bowed to is a face of the one. Osho says polytheism is not the mind's mistake — it is the mind trying to count the uncountable, and the counting collapses into the only response left: namo namah. "A thousand times" — devotion is not stingy; it multiplies its bowing because it has finally found what cannot be thanked enough. When you have seen the whole, every name you ever used for the divine becomes true.

Shloka 11.40 — Salutations from Every Direction​

नमः पुरस्तादथ पृष्ठतस्ते नमोऽस्तु ते सर्वत एव सर्व | अनन्तवीर्यामितविक्रमस्त्वं सर्वं समाप्नोषि ततोऽसि सर्वः

Transliteration: namaḥ purastādatha pṛṣṭhataste namo.astu te sarvata eva sarva . anantavīryāmitavikramastvaṃ sarvaṃ samāpnoṣi tato.asi sarvaḥ

Translation: Salutations to you from the front and from behind; salutations to you on every side, O All. You, infinite in power and prowess, pervade everything; therefore you are everything.

Osho Darshan: Front and behind — and then the geometry breaks, for the all-pervading has no front or back; only the small self has a position. Osho says this is the moment the devotee's geography dissolves: wherever he bows, he is already at the centre. "You are all" is not a statement of theology; it is the report of a seeing. The one who pervades everything cannot be worshipped from a corner — he is worshipped by the whole universe bowing in every direction at once.

Shloka 11.41 — Forgive the Familiarity of Friendship​

सखेति मत्वा प्रसभं यदुक्तं हे कृष्ण हे यादव हे सखेति | अजानता महिमानं तवेदं मया प्रमादात्प्रणयेन वापि

Transliteration: sakheti matvā prasabhaṃ yaduktaṃ he kṛṣṇa he yādava he sakheti . ajānatā mahimānaṃ tavedaṃ mayā pramādātpraṇayena vāpi

Translation: Whatever I have presumptuously said to you, addressing you as "O Krishna, O Yadava, O friend," not knowing this greatness of yours — whether from carelessness or from love — forgive me.

Osho Darshan: The confession of the friend: Arjuna called the infinite "friend," and now the friendship feels like an insult. Osho says this is the most human moment of the vision — the beloved remembers the jokes, the shared meals, the easy intimacy, and suddenly sees that the playmate was the whole. Notice that the divine never once complained; the complaints are the devotee's. And do not be too quick to call your familiarity a sin. The friendship was the first door; the vision is the second. Both were needed.

Shloka 11.42 — Whatever I Did in Play, Forgive​

यच्चावहासार्थमसत्कृतोऽसि विहारशय्यासनभोजनेषु | एकोऽथवाप्यच्युत तत्समक्षं तत्क्षामये त्वामहमप्रमेयम्

Transliteration: yaccāvahāsārthamasatkṛto.asi vihāraśayyāsanabhojaneṣu . eko.athavāpyacyuta tatsamakṣaṃ tatkṣāmaye tvāmahamaprameyam

Translation: And whatever disrespect I may have shown you in jest, while at play, resting, sitting or at meals, alone with you or in company, O Achyuta — for all of that I, the immeasurable one, implore you to forgive me.

Osho Darshan: The list is precise: play, bed, seat, meals — the whole texture of a friendship. Osho says this is the confession every devotee must eventually make: "I lived beside you and did not know who you were." The divine in the kitchen, in the joke, in the quarrel — only later does the devotee see that every moment of the ordinary was a visit from the whole. The forgiveness is granted before it is asked; the vision itself was the forgiveness.

Shloka 11.43 — The Father, the Guru, the Unequalled​

पितासि लोकस्य चराचरस्य त्वमस्य पूज्यश्च गुरुर्गरीयान् | न त्वत्समोऽस्त्यभ्यधिकः कुतोऽन्यो लोकत्रयेऽप्यप्रतिमप्रभाव

Transliteration: pitāsi lokasya carācarasya tvamasya pūjyaśca gururgarīyān . na tvatsamo.astyabhyadhikaḥ kuto.anyo lokatraye.apyapratimaprabhāva

Translation: You are the father of this world, moving and unmoving; you are its object of worship and its greatest teacher. There is none equal to you — how could there be anyone greater, in the three worlds, O being of unequalled power?

Osho Darshan: The heart's whole inventory of love is searched: father, teacher, object of worship. Osho says the devotee tries every human relationship to find the one that fits the divine — and finds all of them fitting at once. "There is none equal" is not a competition; it is the grammar of a heart that has seen the whole. Equality is a concept of the divided world; in the vision there is only one. When you have seen the one, "another" no longer exists.

Shloka 11.44 — Forgive as a Father, a Friend, a Lover​

तस्मात्प्रणम्य प्रणिधाय कायं प्रसादये त्वामहमीशमीड्यम् | पितेव पुत्रस्य सखेव सख्युः प्रियः प्रियायार्हसि देव सोढुम्

Transliteration: tasmātpraṇamya praṇidhāya kāyaṃ prasādaye tvāmahamīśamīḍyam . piteva putrasya sakheva sakhyuḥ priyaḥ priyāyārhasi deva soḍhum

Translation: Therefore, bowing down and prostrating my body, I ask your grace, O adorable Lord. As a father forgives his son, a friend his dear friend, a lover his beloved — so should you forgive me, O God.

Osho Darshan: Three images of forgiveness: father and son, friend and friend, lover and beloved. Osho says these are the three stages of the spiritual path — duty, friendship, love — and each forgives in its own way. The father forgives because the son belongs; the friend forgives because the bond is chosen; the lover forgives because the separation is unbearable. Arjuna asks for all three at once, and the answer is hidden in the asking: the divine loves with all three loves together. You are never outside that forgiveness.

Shloka 11.45 — Delighted and Distressed; Show Me Your Gentle Form​

अदृष्टपूर्वं हृषितोऽस्मि दृष्ट्वा भयेन च प्रव्यथितं मनो मे | तदेव मे दर्शय देव रूपं प्रसीद देवेश जगन्निवास

Transliteration: adṛṣṭapūrvaṃ hṛṣito.asmi dṛṣṭvā bhayena ca pravyathitaṃ mano me . tadeva me darśaya deva rūpaṃ prasīda deveśa jagannivāsa

Translation: I am delighted, having seen what was never seen before; and yet my mind is distressed with fear. Show me that other form of yours, O God; have mercy, O Lord of gods, O abode of the universe.

Osho Darshan: The two faces of the vision in one breath: delight and terror. Osho says this is the honest report of every meeting with the whole — the heart is filled and the small self is shaken in the same instant. Arjuna does not pretend that delight has cancelled the fear; he offers both to the teacher. And he asks for the gentle form. This is the exact psychology of grace: the vision breaks you open, and the gentleness heals you. Both are the divine.

Shloka 11.46 — I Wish to See the Four-Armed Form​

किरीटिनं गदिनं चक्रहस्तं इच्छामि त्वां द्रष्टुमहं तथैव | तेनैव रूपेण चतुर्भुजेन सहस्रबाहो भव विश्वमूर्ते

Transliteration: kirīṭinaṃ gadinaṃ cakrahastaṃ icchāmi tvāṃ draṣṭumahaṃ tathaiva . tenaiva rūpeṇa caturbhujena sahasrabāho bhava viśvamūrte

Translation: I wish to see you as before, crowned, with the mace and the discus in hand, in that very form with four arms — become that, O thousand-armed, O cosmic form.

Osho Darshan: The seeker asks for the smaller form — and Osho says this request is not a retreat; it is the maturity of the vision. The infinite was glimpsed; now the heart wants to rest in the familiar face. The thousand-armed and the four-armed are not two gods; they are the same one at two distances. You cannot live always at the summit of the vision; the vision returns as the ordinary. The art is to see the cosmic in the four-armed — and later, in any face at all.

Shloka 11.47 — Shown by My Grace, Seen by No One Before​

श्रीभगवानुवाच | मया प्रसन्नेन तवार्जुनेदं रूपं परं दर्शितमात्मयोगात् | तेजोमयं विश्वमनन्तमाद्यं यन्मे त्वदन्येन न दृष्टपूर्वम्

Transliteration: śrībhagavānuvāca . mayā prasannena tavārjunedaṃ rūpaṃ paraṃ darśitamātmayogāt . tejomayaṃ viśvamanantamādyaṃ yanme tvadanyena na dṛṣṭapūrvam

Translation: The Blessed Lord said: O Arjuna, by my grace, through my own yoga power, I have shown you this supreme, effulgent, universal, infinite, primal form of mine, which no one other than you has ever seen before.

Osho Darshan: Krishna's first words after the terror set the gift in context: you have been given what even the gods long for. Osho says the grace is not a reward — Arjuna earned nothing on this battlefield — it is the gift of the beloved to the one who asked. "By my own yoga power" — the vision is not manufactured by effort; it is a joining (yoga) performed by the whole itself. The seer is not the achiever of the vision; he is its guest.

Shloka 11.48 — Not by Study, Gifts or Austerities​

न वेदयज्ञाध्ययनैर्न दानैर्- न च क्रियाभिर्न तपोभिरुग्रैः | एवंरूपः शक्य अहं नृलोके द्रष्टुं त्वदन्येन कुरुप्रवीर

Transliteration: na vedayajñādhyayanairna dānaira- na ca kriyābhirna tapobhirugraiḥ . evaṃrūpaḥ śakya ahaṃ nṛloke draṣṭuṃ tvadanyena kurupravīra

Translation: Neither by the study of the Vedas nor by sacrifices, nor by gifts, nor by rituals, nor by severe austerities can I be seen in this form in the world of men by anyone other than you, O great hero of the Kurus.

Osho Darshan: The list of every religious currency — scripture, ritual, charity, penance — and all of it falls short. Osho says this is the Gita's great iconoclasm: the divine is not a purchase. All the mechanisms of religion are mechanisms; the vision is a meeting. That is why the statement ends with the personal: "by anyone other than you." The vision comes through relationship, not through transaction. Arjuna is not holier than the sages; he is closer.

Shloka 11.49 — Be Not Afraid; Behold the Gentle Form​

मा ते व्यथा मा च विमूढभावो दृष्ट्वा रूपं घोरमीदृङ्ममेदम् | व्यपेतभीः प्रीतमनाः पुनस्त्वं तदेव मे रूपमिदं प्रपश्य

Transliteration: mā te vyathā mā ca vimūḍhabhāvo dṛṣṭvā rūpaṃ ghoramīdṛṅmamedam . vyapetabhīḥ prītamanāḥ punastvaṃ tadeva me rūpamidaṃ prapaśya

Translation: Be not afraid, nor bewildered, having seen this terrible form of mine; with your fear dispelled and your heart gladdened, now behold again this former form of mine.

Osho Darshan: The teacher's medicine: "Be not afraid." Osho says fear is the only real obstacle on the path — not ignorance, not sin, but the contracted heart that cannot receive. Krishna does not argue the fear away; he withdraws the cause of it. The gentleness itself is the teaching: the one who was terrible is now tender. When you have seen both faces of the divine in one beloved, the fear of the world ends — for the terrible and the tender are one.

Shloka 11.50 — The Gentle Form Returns; the Terrified Consoled​

सञ्जय उवाच | इत्यर्जुनं वासुदेवस्तथोक्त्वा स्वकं रूपं दर्शयामास भूयः | आश्वासयामास च भीतमेनं भूत्वा पुनः सौम्यवपुर्महात्मा

Transliteration: sañjaya uvāca . ityarjunaṃ vāsudevastathoktvā svakaṃ rūpaṃ darśayāmāsa bhūyaḥ . āśvāsayāmāsa ca bhītamenaṃ bhūtvā punaḥ saumyavapurmahātmā

Translation: Sanjaya said: Having thus spoken to Arjuna, Vasudeva again showed his own form, and the great-souled one, assuming his gentle form, consoled him who was terrified.

Osho Darshan: The narration closes the vision: the cosmic form withdraws and the charioteer returns. Osho says the return is not a loss — it is the completion of the teaching. The vision gave the whole; the gentle form gives the everyday door to it. "Asvasa" — the word for consolation — also means breath. The return of the gentle one is a breath of air after the summit. This is how the divine always works: it shows the infinite, and then it sits beside you as a friend.

Shloka 11.51 — Composed Again, Restored to My Nature​

अर्जुन उवाच | दृष्ट्वेदं मानुषं रूपं तव सौम्यं जनार्दन | इदानीमस्मि संवृत्तः सचेताः प्रकृतिं गतः

Transliteration: arjuna uvāca . dṛṣṭvedaṃ mānuṣaṃ rūpaṃ tava saumyaṃ janārdana . idānīmasmi saṃvṛttaḥ sacetāḥ prakṛtiṃ gataḥ

Translation: Arjuna said: Having seen this gentle human form of yours, O Janardana, I am now composed, my mind clear, restored to my own nature.

Osho Darshan: "Restored to my own nature" — Osho says this is the deepest phrase of the whole chapter. Not restored to his old self — the old self is gone — but to his nature, prakriti, the being that was there all along beneath the identity. After the vision Arjuna is not the same warrior with more information; he is the same being with the same eyes, seeing the same charioteer, but now the seeing itself is different. The gentle form is the ordinary made transparent.

Shloka 11.52 — Hard to See; the Gods Long for It​

श्रीभगवानुवाच | सुदुर्दर्शमिदं रूपं दृष्टवानसि यन्मम | देवा अप्यस्य रूपस्य नित्यं दर्शनकाङ्क्षिणः

Transliteration: śrībhagavānuvāca . sudurdarśamidaṃ rūpaṃ dṛṣṭavānasi yanmama . devā apyasya rūpasya nityaṃ darśanakāṅkṣiṇaḥ

Translation: The Blessed Lord said: Very hard indeed is it to see this form of mine which you have seen; even the gods are ever longing to behold it.

Osho Darshan: The value of the vision is set from the outside: the gods long for it. Osho says this is the paradox of grace — the most precious seeing is given to the one who deserved it by no calculation, only by love. Arjuna is a warrior, not a sage; he asked with longing, and the longing was the whole qualification. The longing itself is the gift. The vision is never the achievement; it is the answer to a heart that would not settle.

Shloka 11.53 — Not by Vedas, Austerity, Gift or Sacrifice​

नाहं वेदैर्न तपसा न दानेन न चेज्यया | शक्य एवंविधो द्रष्टुं दृष्टवानसि मां यथा

Transliteration: nāhaṃ vedairna tapasā na dānena na cejyayā . śakya evaṃvidho draṣṭuṃ dṛṣṭavānasi māṃ yathā

Translation: Not by the Vedas, nor by austerity, nor by gift, nor by sacrifice can I be seen in this form, in the way you have seen me.

Osho Darshan: The same truth, declared again in simpler words. Osho says the repetition is deliberate — the mind must hear twice what it wants to deny: that the divine is not a product of effort. Every religion is an effort to climb; the vision is a descent. You cannot reach the whole by climbing; the whole reaches you. Arjuna's seeing was not the fruit of his practice; it was the fruit of his asking.

Shloka 11.54 — Only by Single-Minded Devotion​

भक्त्या त्वनन्यया शक्य अहमेवंविधोऽर्जुन | ज्ञातुं द्रष्टुं च तत्त्वेन प्रवेष्टुं च परन्तप

Transliteration: bhaktyā tvananyayā śakya ahamevaṃvidho.arjuna . jñātuṃ draṣṭuṃ ca tattvena praveṣṭuṃ ca parantapa

Translation: But by single-minded devotion, O Arjuna, can I be known in truth, be seen, and be entered into, O scorcher of foes.

Osho Darshan: The key of the whole chapter: ananya bhakti — devotion without a second. Osho says the three verbs are the complete journey: to know, to see, to enter. Knowledge is the head; seeing is the heart; entering is the whole being. And the door is one: a love that keeps no other object, no other ambition, no other god. The vision cannot be bought with practices, but it cannot be refused to a love that has nowhere else to go.

Shloka 11.55 — Act for Me; Be Without Enmity; Come to Me​

मत्कर्मकृन्मत्परमो मद्भक्तः सङ्गवर्जितः | निर्वैरः सर्वभूतेषु यः स मामेति पाण्डव

Transliteration: matkarmakṛnmatparamo madbhaktaḥ saṅgavarjitaḥ . nirvairaḥ sarvabhūteṣu yaḥ sa māmeti pāṇḍava

Translation: He who does all actions for me, who holds me as the supreme, who is devoted to me, who is free from attachment, who bears enmity toward no creature — he comes to me, O Arjuna.

Osho Darshan: The chapter closes with the five fingers of the teaching: action for the divine, the divine as the supreme goal, devotion, freedom from attachment, and enmity toward none. Osho says the list is not a law; it is the description of a man the vision has burned clean. "He comes to me" — the coming is not a journey through space; it is the dissolving of a distance that was never there. The devotee does not reach the divine; he discovers he has always been inside it.

Science Note — Ego Dissolution and Mystical Experiences

Chapter 11 is the Gita's most dramatic chapter — the cosmic vision that terrifies Arjuna. Modern psychology of mystical experiences (Hood, 1975) and ego dissolution (Millière et al., 2017) describe exactly what Arjuna undergoes: the temporary collapse of the self-boundary, producing both terror and ecstasy. The "thousand suns" (11.12) is not just poetry — it maps to the phenomenology of ego dissolution: a flash of boundless awareness that overwhelms the ordinary self.

The terror of the vision (11.20-11.31)Ego dissolution (Millière et al., 2017)The ego's fear response to boundary loss is universal — the self defends its own annihilation. The terror is the ego's farewell, not danger
"I am Time, the world-destroyer" (11.32)Impermanence awareness (Epstein, 2008)Confronting impermanence directly produces both terror and liberation — the same insight that underlies Buddhist anicca and Stoic memento mori
The gentle form returns (11.47-11.51)Integration after mystical experience (Welwood, 2000)The return to the familiar is essential — mystical experiences must be integrated into daily life, not clung to. Grace is the integration
Single-minded devotion (11.54)Absorption states (Csikszentmihalyi, 1990)Ananya bhakti maps to deep absorption — the total engagement that produces the same boundary-dissolution as the cosmic vision, but sustained

Try This: Reflect on a moment when you felt both terrified and exhilarated — a risk taken, a boundary crossed, a truth confronted. That moment is a miniature vishvarupa. The ego shrinks; the awareness expands. The terror and the ecstasy are two sides of the same coin.

Summary​

  • The chapter opens with the request: Arjuna, his delusion cleared, asks to see the divine form, not just hear about it (11.1–11.4).
  • Krishna grants the divine eye and reveals the cosmic form — a thousand suns rising together, the whole universe resting in one body (11.5–11.14).
  • Arjuna sees the hosts of gods and the warriors rushing into the terrible mouths; the three worlds tremble (11.15–11.31).
  • Krishna declares "I am Time, the destroyer of worlds": the warriors are already slain, and Arjuna is only an instrument (11.32–11.35).
  • Arjuna's hymn rises into surrender — praise, confession, forgiveness — and the plea to return to the gentle form (11.36–11.46).
  • Krishna withdraws the vision and gives the final key: by single-minded devotion alone the divine is known, seen and entered (11.47–11.55).
  • Osho's reading: the vision is not a spectacle but the experience of the whole; the terror is the ego's farewell, and the devotee who acts for the divine is the one who has seen.

Practical Takeaways​

  • Ask to see, not just to know — words clarify, but only direct experience transforms. Let "let me see" become your daily question.
  • Prepare the eye — the fleshly eye sees surfaces; the divine eye is the old eye emptied of projections. Meditate to empty the eye.
  • Let the trembling pass — when your identity trembles before something greater, do not flee; that trembling is growth, not breakdown.
  • Act as an instrument — do your work fully, but release ownership of results. You are the flute, not the song.
  • Let praise become bowing — when analysis ends, surrender begins; the deepest response to the whole is namah.
  • Return to the ordinary with new eyes — the gentle Krishna is the everyday door to the vision; see the cosmic in the small and the familiar.
  • Devote single-mindedly — the vision is not earned by practices but given to a love that has nowhere else to go.

  2. Analysis exercise — the instrument test: Pick one current responsibility and perform it for a day as "nimittamatram" — a mere instrument. Observe how the action changes when ownership of results is released, and note what remains of your old fear.

For the Engineer​

  • The cosmic vision in system design: When you first see a complex system's full architecture — all the services, databases, queues, and dependencies — the experience can be overwhelming (terror) and exhilarating (wonder). This is the vishvarupa moment. The return to the gentle form is the integration: you focus on one module at a time while carrying the whole architecture in mind.
  • "I am Time" in production incidents: When a critical incident occurs, panic is the dark path. "Remember Me and fight" (8.7) combined with "I am Time" (11.32) means: accept the impermanence of the situation, act as an instrument, and let the incident unfold through you rather than by you.
  • Ego dissolution in code review: When your code is thoroughly critiqued, the ego's defense is the "terror" response. The integration is recognizing that the code is not you — the critique targets the artifact, not the person. This is the gentle form returning after the cosmic vision.

TypeScript Tool: Vishvarupa Vision Log​

/**
 * Vishvarupa Vision Log — Ego Dissolution Edition
 * Based on Vishvarupa Darshana Yoga (Gita 11.1-11.55) and
 * ego dissolution research (Millière et al., 2017): the
 * temporary collapse of the self-boundary produces both
 * terror and ecstasy. The vision's arc maps the phenomenology
 * of ego dissolution and its integration.
 *
 * Run: npx ts-node vishvarupa-vision-log.ts
 */

interface ShlokaEntry {
  number: number;
  movement: string;
  emotionalState: string;
  egoIntensity: number;   // 0–10: how strongly is the self defending?
  awarenessBreadth: number; // 0–10: how expansive is awareness?
}

interface MovementSummary {
  name: string;
  range: [number, number];
  shlokas: number;
  dominantEmotion: string;
  peakEgoIntensity: number;
  peakAwarenessBreadth: number;
  dissolutionScore: number;  // awareness - ego: positive = breakthrough
}

const movements: [string, number, number][] = [
  ['The request and the granting', 1, 14],
  ['The seeing and the terror', 15, 31],
  ['The revelation of Time', 32, 35],
  ['The praise, the return and the way', 36, 55]
];

const emotionData: Record<number, { emotion: string; ego: number; awareness: number }> = {
  1: { emotion: 'gratitude', ego: 3, awareness: 5 },
  3: { emotion: 'longing', ego: 4, awareness: 6 },
  8: { emotion: 'trust', ego: 2, awareness: 7 },
  12: { emotion: 'wonder', ego: 5, awareness: 8 },
  14: { emotion: 'awe', ego: 6, awareness: 9 },
  20: { emotion: 'fear', ego: 9, awareness: 7 },
  23: { emotion: 'terror', ego: 10, awareness: 6 },
  25: { emotion: 'desperation', ego: 8, awareness: 5 },
  28: { emotion: 'consolation', ego: 5, awareness: 7 },
  31: { emotion: 'humility', ego: 3, awareness: 8 },
  32: { emotion: 'shock', ego: 7, awareness: 9 },
  33: { emotion: 'clarity', ego: 2, awareness: 10 },
  37: { emotion: 'praise', ego: 3, awareness: 8 },
  44: { emotion: 'surrender', ego: 1, awareness: 9 },
  45: { emotion: 'longing for the gentle', ego: 4, awareness: 7 },
  51: { emotion: 'peace', ego: 2, awareness: 8 },
  55: { emotion: 'devotion', ego: 1, awareness: 9 }
};

function movementOf(verse: number): string {
  const match = movements.find(([, start, end]) => verse >= start && verse <= end);
  return match ? match[0] : 'Unknown';
}

function buildLog(): ShlokaEntry[] {
  const entries: ShlokaEntry[] = [];
  for (let v = 1; v <= 55; v++) {
    const data = emotionData[v] ?? { emotion: 'awakening', ego: 4, awareness: 6 };
    entries.push({
      number: v,
      movement: movementOf(v),
      emotionalState: data.emotion,
      egoIntensity: data.ego,
      awarenessBreadth: data.awareness
    });
  }
  return entries;
}

function summarize(log: ShlokaEntry[]): MovementSummary[] {
  return movements.map(([name, start, end]) => {
    const slice = log.filter((e) => e.number >= start && e.number <= end);
    const counts: Record<string, number> = {};
    slice.forEach((e) => { counts[e.emotionalState] = (counts[e.emotionalState] ?? 0) + 1; });
    const dominant = Object.entries(counts).sort((a, b) => b[1] - a[1])[0][0];
    const peakEgo = Math.max(...slice.map((e) => e.egoIntensity));
    const peakAwareness = Math.max(...slice.map((e) => e.awarenessBreadth));
    return {
      name,
      range: [start, end] as [number, number],
      shlokas: slice.length,
      dominantEmotion: dominant,
      peakEgoIntensity: peakEgo,
      peakAwarenessBreadth: peakAwareness,
      dissolutionScore: peakAwareness - peakEgo
    };
  });
}

const log = buildLog();
const summaries = summarize(log);

console.log('=== Vishvarupa Vision Log ===');
console.log('The arc of the seer through the vision:\n');
for (const s of log) {
  const bar = '█'.repeat(s.awarenessBreadth) + '░'.repeat(10 - s.awarenessBreadth);
  console.log(`${String(s.number).padStart(2)} | ${s.movement.padEnd(36)} | ${s.emotionalState.padEnd(18)} | ${bar}`);
}
console.log('\n=== Movement Summaries ===');
for (const m of summaries) {
  const dissolution = m.dissolutionScore > 0 ? `+${m.dissolutionScore}` : `${m.dissolutionScore}`;
  console.log(`${m.name}: ego ${m.peakEgoIntensity}/10, awareness ${m.peakAwarenessBreadth}/10, dissolution ${dissolution}`);
}
console.log('\nTerror is the ego farewell; devotion is the return home. (11.47-11.55)');
