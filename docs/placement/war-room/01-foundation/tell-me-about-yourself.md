# Tell Me About Yourself

> **Status:** ○ Planned · Edit this line to ◐ Practicing · ● Drilled

This question opens 95% of interviews. Nail it, the interviewer relaxes and the round flows your way. Fumble it, and the next 30 minutes are recovery mode.

You will memorize **three versions** — 30 seconds, 90 seconds, 5 minutes — and rehearse each one out loud until the words come without thinking.

## The Script — 90 seconds

> **Hook (10s)** — "Sir, my name is Raushan Kumar. I'm a final-year Computer Science student at [College Name]. The thing I'm most proud of is shipping a Laravel + OpenAI tool that's currently used by 500+ people to cut content-writing time by 80%."
>
> **Body (60s)** — "My core stack is PHP, Laravel 12, MySQL, and React. I also work with Python for AI integrations — specifically the OpenAI API, LangChain, and RAG pipelines. I have three deployed projects on GitHub: [Project 1, one line], [Project 2, one line], [Project 3, one line]. The one I usually walk people through first is [Lead Project], because it solved a real problem for [users / company / college]. I was lead developer on my college fest portal — handled 2,000 concurrent users on registration day without crashing."
>
> **Close (20s)** — "I'm targeting roles where I can pair my Laravel backend skills with AI engineering — building APIs and LLM-powered features. I'm excited about [Company Name] specifically because [one concrete reason from your research]. I'm open to relocation, and I can start [date]."

Three sentences in. Stack and projects in the middle. Their two unasked questions answered at the end. **Ninety seconds.**

---

## The Anatomy — why this wins

The interviewer hears fifty of these per day. Most are biographies that start in school. Yours opens with **proof of impact** — a number (500 users, 80%), a real product, a real outcome. That hooks them in 10 seconds.

The body is a **stack inventory plus a project pointer**, not a story. Stories take five minutes. The body sets up *which* project they should ask about next — which means **you control the next question**. That's a power move and they won't realize you made it.

The close removes their objections before they ask. *"Open to relocation"* and *"can start [date]"* are the two things they were planning to ask. You answered both. They move on, impressed.

If you don't have a 500-user project or a 2,000-user portal, **swap the hook**. Real options:

- "I'm a final-year student who solved [specific problem] for [specific group]."
- "I've shipped [N] projects on GitHub — one was [feature in newsletter / used by class / hackathon prize]."
- "I'm a self-taught full-stack developer — moved from [previous field] to Laravel and AI engineering over the last [N] months, and shipped [project] as my first production app."

The hook needs **one specific number or one specific outcome**. No number = no hook = next question is *"tell me more about yourself"* and you're in recovery mode.

## The 5-day practice drill

- [ ] **Day 1 — Read aloud, 5×.** Don't memorize. Just say the words. Notice where your tongue stumbles. Mark those spots.
- [ ] **Day 2 — Record on phone, listen back.** You will hate it. Find one filler word (*uh, like, basically, actually, ya, so*) you say too often. Decide to kill it.
- [ ] **Day 3 — Practice with a timer.** Hit 90 seconds ± 5. Over 100s = trim. Under 80s = add a detail to the body, not the close.
- [ ] **Day 4 — Mock with a friend or a mirror.** Maintain eye contact. Hand gestures only when natural. No fidgeting — train your hands to rest on the table.
- [ ] **Day 5 — Cold delivery.** Wake up, drink water, deliver the script before reading it. If you can do it cold, you own it.

Five days. Twenty minutes a day. That's the price for owning the opening of every interview you'll take for the next decade.

## Recovery — when you blank

You will blank at some point. Here is the line:

> *"Sorry, give me one second — let me actually start with my projects, that's the part I'm most excited about."*

Then pivot directly to your strongest project's 30-second pitch.

The interviewer will **not** penalize you for collecting yourself. They will penalize you for filler (*"um, so, basically, ya so, what I do is..."*). Buying time with a clean sentence is professional. Drowning in filler is fatal.

If you blank on a specific number ("how many users?"), say:

> *"The last metric I checked was around [round number] — let me verify after the call."*

Never invent a number. They sometimes check.

## Variants

=== "30s — Elevator"

    > "Hi, I'm Raushan. Final-year CS student. Strongest skill is Laravel — shipped a Laravel + OpenAI tool used by 500 people. Also do React frontend and Python AI work. Looking for full-stack or AI-engineering roles. Three projects on GitHub if you'd like the links."

    Use when: walking to a coffee chat, networking at a meetup, recruiter calls cold to confirm interest.

=== "90s — Default"

    See main script above.

    Use when: standard 1:1 interview opening, screening round, HR round.

=== "5min — Senior screen / panel"

    Use the 90s script as the spine. After the close, **pause**. If they don't jump in, continue:

    > "Would it help if I walked through how I built [Lead Project] end-to-end? I usually structure it as the problem, the stack decision, one hard bug I had to debug, and the metric it moved."

    Then deliver the 5-minute Project Pitch (covered in [Project Pitch — Deep Dive](../03-project-pitch/deep-dive.md), once that drill is written).

    Use when: senior engineer screen, panel interview, "walk me through your background" with a 30-minute slot.

## Common traps

| Trap | Why it kills | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Starts at *"I was born in…"* | Wastes the opening on biography no one asked for | Lead with the hook (one number, one impact) |
| No specific number | Sounds generic, instantly forgettable | One concrete metric in the hook — users, percent, scale |
| Lists every tech you've ever touched | Sounds shallow, can't go deep on any | Name 5 core techs with versions (Laravel 12, not "Laravel") |
| *"I'm a quick learner / hard worker"* | Recruiter cliché bingo, they tune out | Show learning by naming a project you shipped this month |
| Doesn't mention the company name | Sounds like spray-and-pray applications | Last sentence: one specific reason from their site/blog |
| Talks about hobbies | This is the wrong question for that | Save hobbies for "tell me about you outside work" |
| Speaks too fast (anxiety) | They can't follow, you sound nervous | Pause after every sentence. The pauses make you sound senior. |
